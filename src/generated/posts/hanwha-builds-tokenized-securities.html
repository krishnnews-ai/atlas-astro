Hanwha Builds Tokenized Securities Platform on Avalanche in South Korea

September 7, 2026

South Korean financial giant Hanwha is moving deeper into blockchain-based finance, with Hanwha Investment & Securities completing a tokenized-securities platform that supports the Avalanche blockchain.

The development comes as South Korea prepares to formally integrate tokenized securities into its regulated capital markets. The platform was developed with blockchain technology company FairSquare Lab and is designed to support multiple blockchain environments, including Avalanche and Hyperledger Besu, according to Seoul Economic Daily.

The move gives Avalanche another institutional use case in South Korea, while also highlighting how traditional financial firms are preparing for the country's upcoming tokenized-securities market.

What Hanwha Is Building

Hanwha Investment & Securities' project is focused on tokenized securities, rather than launching a new cryptocurrency.

Tokenized securities use blockchain or distributed-ledger technology to represent ownership or rights connected to traditional financial assets. Depending on the regulatory framework, those assets can include funds, bonds, stocks and fractional investment products.

Hanwha began developing the platform in 2025 with FairSquare Lab. The completed infrastructure can operate across different blockchain networks, with Avalanche and Hyperledger Besu among the supported environments.

Why Avalanche Is Part of the Platform

The use of Avalanche is notable because the project is being developed for a regulated financial application rather than a conventional crypto trading product.

Avalanche can support customizable blockchain environments, including networks designed for specific institutional requirements. That makes the technology potentially useful for financial applications where participants, access rules and transaction processes need to be controlled.

Hanwha's decision to support both Avalanche and Hyperledger Besu also shows that institutional blockchain strategies do not necessarily revolve around one network.

The development should therefore not be interpreted as South Korea choosing Avalanche as its national blockchain for securities. It is one platform being developed by a financial institution within a broader multi-network market.

South Korea Is Preparing for Tokenized Securities

The timing of Hanwha's platform is particularly important.

South Korea is preparing a regulatory framework that will bring tokenized securities into its existing capital-market system. The new rules are scheduled to take effect in February 2027.

The Financial Services Commission has outlined a phased approach for tokenization, with the scope expected to expand beyond fractional investment products to conventional securities such as stocks, bonds and funds.

Under the first stage, certain tokenized products will be introduced through regulated structures, while later phases are expected to expand the range of securities that can be tokenized and potentially introduce on-chain settlement using payment instruments such as stablecoins.

This regulatory shift gives Korean financial institutions a reason to build the necessary infrastructure before the market officially expands.

Hanwha's Platform Arrives Ahead of the 2027 Market

For Hanwha Investment & Securities, completing the platform ahead of the regulatory transition could allow the company to test and develop its blockchain infrastructure before tokenized securities become a larger part of South Korea's financial market.

The Korea Securities Depository is also developing tokenized-securities infrastructure capable of connecting with multiple blockchain technologies, including Avalanche. Seoul Economic Daily described this as evidence that demand from private financial firms is beginning to influence regulated market infrastructure.

That connection could eventually become important for issuance, trading, ownership records and settlement of tokenized financial products.

Hanwha Has Been Expanding Its Blockchain Exposure

The Avalanche platform is part of a wider blockchain strategy at Hanwha Investment & Securities.

Earlier in 2026, the securities firm invested approximately $13 million in Kresus Labs, a blockchain company focused on digital-asset infrastructure. The investment was linked to areas including enterprise wallet infrastructure and real-world asset tokenization.

Hanwha has also expanded its exposure to the broader tokenization sector through investments and partnerships involving companies such as Securitize.

Taken together, these developments suggest that Hanwha is exploring blockchain as financial infrastructure rather than simply treating digital assets as a speculative investment theme.

What Tokenization Could Change

Tokenization could change how financial assets are issued, transferred and recorded.

Traditional securities rely on multiple systems and intermediaries to maintain ownership records, process transactions and complete settlement. Blockchain-based infrastructure can potentially bring some of those functions onto a shared digital ledger.

Potential advantages include:

  • Faster settlement
  • Programmable financial products
  • More transparent transaction records
  • Potentially more efficient asset administration
  • Fractional ownership for certain assets

However, tokenization does not eliminate financial regulation. Tokenized securities still need to comply with rules covering investor protection, ownership rights, issuance, trading and settlement.

Hanwha Is Not Moving South Korea's Entire Market to Avalanche

One distinction is important when interpreting the announcement.

Hanwha Investment & Securities has built a platform supporting Avalanche, but there is no indication that South Korea has selected Avalanche as the country's exclusive blockchain for tokenized securities.

South Korea's market infrastructure is being developed around multiple technologies. The Korea Securities Depository's work and the government's phased tokenization roadmap show that the country's approach is broader than any single blockchain network.

That makes Hanwha's development significant as an institutional adoption story, but not as a national blockchain-selection announcement.

Why This Matters for Avalanche

For Avalanche, the project adds another example of its infrastructure being considered for institutional financial applications.

The larger opportunity is the growing market for real-world asset tokenization. Banks, brokerages, asset managers and other financial institutions are increasingly exploring whether blockchain can be used for funds, bonds, private assets and other securities.

But infrastructure development alone does not guarantee large-scale adoption. The success of tokenized securities will depend on regulation, liquidity, investor demand, custody, interoperability and the ability of blockchain systems to integrate with existing financial infrastructure.

What Happens Next?

The next major milestone will be South Korea's implementation of its tokenized-securities framework in February 2027.

That transition should provide a clearer picture of how financial institutions such as Hanwha can use blockchain infrastructure in regulated securities markets.

For Avalanche, the important question will be whether institutional projects move beyond technical platforms and into actual issuance, trading and settlement of tokenized assets.

For now, the development is clear: Hanwha Investment & Securities has completed a tokenized-securities platform supporting Avalanche and Hyperledger Besu as South Korea prepares for a regulated tokenization market.
