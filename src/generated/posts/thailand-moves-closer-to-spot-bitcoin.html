Thailand Moves Closer to Spot Bitcoin and Ether ETFs With Draft Rules

Top Crypto News 2026-08-25T11:55:54.457Z
ETFsBitcoin NewsEthereum News



By CoinAINews Staff | 

Thailand is moving closer to bringing spot Bitcoin and Ether exchange-traded funds (ETFs) to its domestic market after the country's Securities and Exchange Commission opened consultations on draft regulations for locally established crypto ETFs.

The Thai SEC opened the consultations on August 24, 2026, covering proposed rules for crypto ETFs as well as revised qualification requirements for foreign digital-asset custodians serving mutual and private funds. The consultation period is scheduled to remain open until September 20.

The move does not mean Thailand has approved the ETFs for trading yet. Instead, it marks another step in the regulator's process of building a formal framework for crypto investment products.

What Thailand Is Proposing

Under the draft framework, asset managers could establish passive ETFs that track individual cryptocurrencies.

For the initial phase, Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH) would be the eligible assets.

Each proposed ETF would track a single cryptocurrency and would be listed and traded on the Stock Exchange of Thailand (SET).

The proposed framework would also require each fund to maintain average net exposure of at least 80% of its net asset value to the underlying crypto asset over an accounting year. The requirement is intended to keep the fund's performance closely aligned with the asset it tracks.

Bitcoin and Ether Would Come First

The SEC's decision to start with Bitcoin and Ether gives the framework a relatively narrow initial scope.

Both assets have large global markets and established institutional investment products in other jurisdictions.

Thailand's proposed ETFs would give investors another way to obtain exposure to the two cryptocurrencies through a regulated investment structure rather than buying and storing the assets directly.

That could be particularly relevant for investors who already use traditional securities accounts and are more comfortable with exchange-listed products than crypto wallets or digital-asset exchanges.

However, the proposal remains limited to the initial framework and does not mean other cryptocurrencies will automatically qualify for future ETFs.

Custody Is a Major Part of the Framework

The SEC is also putting significant attention on digital-asset custody.

Under the revised proposal, locally established crypto ETFs would primarily be required to use digital-asset custodians in Thailand during the initial phase.

The regulator could, however, allow qualified foreign custodians when it considers such an arrangement necessary and appropriate under prevailing circumstances.

The SEC has separately proposed qualification standards for foreign digital-asset custodians serving mutual and private funds.

Those requirements are intended to ensure that foreign providers operate under appropriate regulatory supervision and investor-asset protection standards.

The custody rules matter because an ETF structure still needs secure arrangements for holding the underlying crypto assets.

Mutual and Private Funds Could Also Invest

The proposed framework would also affect how Thai mutual and private funds gain exposure to crypto investment products.

Under the proposal, these funds would be allowed to invest in Thai-domiciled crypto ETFs, alongside foreign crypto ETFs where existing investment rules permit them.

At the same time, the regulator is proposing restrictions on alternative products linked to foreign crypto ETFs during the initial phase, including certain depositary receipt structures.

This suggests that Thailand wants to establish direct oversight of its domestic crypto ETF market before allowing a wider range of indirect products.

Why the 80% Rule Matters

The 80% exposure requirement is more than a technical detail.

An ETF is generally expected to provide investors with exposure to the asset or market described in its investment objective.

By requiring average net exposure of at least 80% to the underlying crypto asset, the proposed framework seeks to keep a Bitcoin or Ether ETF closely connected to the asset it tracks.

It also gives investors a clearer understanding of what the proposed products are designed to provide.

Thailand's Bigger Digital-Asset Strategy

The proposed ETF framework fits into Thailand's broader effort to develop its digital-asset market under regulatory supervision.

Thailand already has a regulatory framework covering licensed digital-asset businesses and other crypto-related activities.

Adding locally listed ETFs would create another connection between the digital-asset market and the country's traditional capital-market infrastructure.

The SEC has said the proposals are intended to broaden investment opportunities, strengthen market participants and increase product diversity within Thailand's capital market.

Thailand Is Following a Global ETF Trend

Thailand's proposal comes as major financial markets continue developing regulated cryptocurrency investment products.

The United States approved spot Bitcoin ETFs in January 2024 and spot Ether ETFs later that year, creating regulated routes for traditional investors to gain exposure to the two assets.

Thailand's proposed framework would bring a similar type of investment structure into its domestic market, although the country's rules are still at the consultation stage.

For Thailand, establishing a domestic framework could give local investors access to cryptocurrency exposure through familiar capital-market structures while keeping the products under domestic regulatory oversight.

What Happens Next?

The consultation period is scheduled to run until September 20.

Fund managers, custodians, financial institutions and other market participants can submit feedback on the proposed regulations before the SEC moves toward final rules.

There is currently no confirmed launch date for Thailand's first spot Bitcoin or Ether ETF.

The final framework could also differ from the current draft depending on the feedback received during the consultation.

For investors, that distinction is important: Thailand is moving toward crypto ETFs, but the products are not yet approved for trading simply because the draft regulations have been published.

The Bigger Picture

If the framework is finalized, Thailand could give traditional investors a regulated route to Bitcoin and Ether exposure through the country's existing securities infrastructure.

That could make crypto more accessible to investors who prefer exchange-listed products over direct ownership.

At the same time, the success of the framework will depend on more than simply getting the rules approved.

Liquidity, custody, investor demand, pricing and secondary-market activity will all matter once the products actually reach the market.

The SEC's consultation is therefore an important stage, but it is still only one part of the process.

The Bottom Line

Thailand has moved another step toward spot Bitcoin and Ether ETFs after the SEC opened consultations on draft rules for locally established crypto ETFs.

The proposal would initially focus on Bitcoin and Ether, require at least 80% average net exposure to the underlying asset and make Thai digital-asset custodians the primary custody option during the initial phase.

The consultation remains open until September 20, and final approval has not yet been announced.

For now, Thailand is not launching crypto ETFs just yet. It is building the regulatory framework that could eventually make them possible.

If the SEC's draft rules survive the consultation process, Thailand could be taking a significant step toward bringing Bitcoin and Ether closer to its traditional capital markets — while keeping the products inside a regulated investment framework.
