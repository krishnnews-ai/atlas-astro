Brian Armstrong Predicts CLARITY Act Could Win Far More Than 60 Senate Votes

Top Crypto News 2026-08-23T11:52:16.740Z
Market NewsCoin Ai News

 


By CoinAINews Staff

The U.S. crypto industry may be heading toward another major test in Washington, and Coinbase CEO Brian Armstrong believes the numbers could be much stronger than many expected.

In a fresh update circulating on social media, Armstrong said he expects the CLARITY Act to receive “far more than 60 votes” in the U.S. Senate, describing the legislation as a strong bipartisan compromise.

The comment comes as lawmakers prepare for a crucial September test of the bill. The Senate is scheduled to consider a procedural vote on September 15, meaning Armstrong's prediction arrives at an important moment for the future of U.S. crypto regulation.

For the crypto market, the distinction matters. The CLARITY Act has not passed the Senate and is not yet law. The September vote is a test of whether senators can move the legislation forward.

But Armstrong's confidence suggests that Coinbase and other major crypto companies see a realistic path toward bipartisan support.

Armstrong's 60-Vote Prediction

Armstrong's latest comment is essentially a political forecast, not a vote count.

He believes the CLARITY Act's bipartisan positioning could allow it to clear the Senate's 60-vote threshold with room to spare.

That would be significant.

The Senate's procedural rules generally make 60 votes an important threshold for advancing major legislation. In the case of the CLARITYAct, the upcoming September 15 cloture vote is expected to determine whether the Senate can formally move forward with consideration of the bill.

Armstrong's argument is that the current version represents enough of a compromise to attract support from senators on both sides of the political aisle.

That does not guarantee passage.

Still, coming from the CEO of one of the world's largest publicly traded crypto companies, the prediction is likely to attract attention from investors and digital-asset businesses watching the legislation closely.

What Is the CLARITY Act?

The Digital Asset Market CLARITY Act is designed to establish clearer rules for the U.S. cryptocurrency market.

At its core, the legislation attempts to answer one of the biggest questions facing the American crypto industry:

Which digital assets should fall under the SEC, and which should be regulated by the CFTC?

The bill would establish a broader federal framework for digital assets, including rules around market structure, regulatory jurisdiction and the treatment of different types of crypto assets.

That could potentially reduce some of the uncertainty that has surrounded the U.S. crypto industry for years.

The legislation is particularly important because crypto companies have often operated in an environment where the regulatory boundaries between securities and commodities were heavily disputed.

The CLARITY Act seeks to create clearer lines.

The Bill Has Already Cleared Important Hurdles

The CLARITY Act is not starting from scratch.

The legislation has already passed the House of Representatives, while Senate committees have also advanced the measure.

The Senate Banking Committee approved the bill in May in a 15-9 vote, marking an important step for the legislation.

But committee approval is only part of the process.

The bill still needs to navigate the Senate floor, survive negotiations and ultimately move through the remaining stages of the legislative process before it could become law.

That is why the September vote is attracting so much attention.

Why September 15 Matters

One point is particularly important for anyone following the story.

September 15 is not necessarily a final vote to pass the CLARITY Act.

Instead, the Senate is expected to vote on cloture related to moving forward with consideration of the legislation.

In simple terms, senators will be deciding whether the bill has enough support to advance through the chamber.

That makes the 60-vote threshold extremely important.

If Armstrong's prediction proves accurate and the bill receives substantially more than 60 votes, it would send a strong political signal that bipartisan support exists.

If the vote falls short, however, the legislation could face another round of negotiations or delays.

Recent reporting has described the Senate's timeline as tight, with lawmakers returning from recess and facing a limited window to advance the bill.

Why Crypto Companies Want the Bill

For the crypto industry, regulatory clarity is more than a political talking point.

Companies need to know which rules apply to their businesses before making long-term decisions about products, exchanges, custody, token issuance and investment.

The absence of clear federal legislation has been a major source of uncertainty for U.S. crypto companies.

Coinbase, for example, has spent years pushing for clearer rules around digital assets.

Armstrong has previously been critical of versions of crypto legislation that Coinbase believed did not adequately protect the industry's interests. Earlier in 2026, he argued that the company would rather see no bill than support legislation it considered unfavorable.

That makes his latest confidence notable.

The current version appears to have moved closer to the compromise that Armstrong and other industry executives have been seeking.

What Could the CLARITY Act Mean for Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is not simply a political asset, but regulation can influence how investors and institutions approach it.

One of the biggest potential benefits of clearer U.S. crypto rules would be reduced uncertainty for financial institutions and market participants.

Bitcoin already has a relatively established position in the U.S. market compared with many other digital assets.

A clearer market-structure framework could nevertheless make it easier for traditional financial firms to build products and services around digital assets.

That could include exchanges, custody services, institutional trading infrastructure and other financial products.

The impact would not necessarily appear overnight.

Regulation does not automatically create a Bitcoin rally.

But clearer rules could remove one of the barriers that has historically made some institutions cautious about entering the market.

The Political Backdrop Is Also Changing

Armstrong's comments come during a period of increasing political attention on cryptocurrency in Washington.

President Donald Trump has publicly pushed Congress to advance crypto legislation, while the administration has also pursued changes through federal financial regulators.

Reuters recently reported that the CLARITY Act's congressional progress has stalled while regulators such as the SEC and CFTC continue moving forward with their own crypto-related policy initiatives.

That creates an unusual situation.

The industry is getting more regulatory activity from Washington, but a comprehensive law from Congress would provide a more durable framework than agency-level policy changes alone.

That is one reason the Senate process remains important.

Why 60 Votes Are So Important

The Senate is different from the House when it comes to major legislation.

A simple majority is not always enough to move a bill through the chamber because procedural rules can require a larger coalition to end debate and advance legislation.

For the CLARITY Act, that makes the upcoming 60-vote threshold a major checkpoint.

Armstrong is effectively saying he believes the legislation has enough bipartisan support to clear that hurdle comfortably.

Whether that confidence translates into actual votes remains to be seen.

That is the key distinction investors should keep in mind.

A prediction is not a vote.

What a Strong Vote Could Signal

If the CLARITY Act receives substantially more than 60 votes, the market could interpret the result as evidence that crypto legislation has broader bipartisan support than previously assumed.

That could be positive for companies operating in the digital-asset sector.

It could also strengthen the argument that the United States is moving toward a more defined regulatory framework for cryptocurrency.

But there are still several steps between a successful procedural vote and the bill becoming law.

The Senate would need to continue working through the legislation, differences between House and Senate versions would need to be addressed, and the final legislation would still have to reach the president.

In other words, a September victory would be important, but it would not be the finish line.

What Crypto Investors Should Watch

The biggest event on the calendar is the Senate's September 15 procedural vote.

Investors should watch three things in particular.

First, the vote count.

A result significantly above 60 votes would support Armstrong's argument that the legislation has strong bipartisan backing.

Second, the final legislative language.

Even if the bill advances, negotiations over specific provisions could continue.

Third, the reaction from the crypto industry.

Major exchanges, asset managers, blockchain companies and other digital-asset businesses are likely to pay close attention to the final framework.

For Bitcoin holders, the broader question is whether regulatory clarity eventually encourages more institutional participation.

A Potential Turning Point for U.S. Crypto Regulation

The CLARITY Act has already survived several difficult stages.

It has moved through the House, cleared Senate committee hurdles and now faces the next major test.

Armstrong's latest prediction adds another layer of interest because he is not merely saying the bill could pass.

He is suggesting that it could receive far more than the 60 votes needed for the Senate's procedural hurdle.

That would represent a strong showing of bipartisan support.

Still, investors should avoid treating the prediction as a guarantee.

The legislation remains subject to negotiations, political disagreements and the realities of the Senate calendar.

The Bottom Line

Brian Armstrong is betting that the CLARITY Act has enough bipartisan support to win far more than 60 votes in the U.S. Senate.

His prediction comes as the legislation approaches a crucial September 15 procedural test.

The bill is designed to create a clearer regulatory framework for digital assets and define the roles of the SEC and CFTC in overseeing different parts of the crypto market.

For the industry, that clarity could be significant.

For Bitcoin, the biggest potential benefit may come indirectly through greater institutional confidence and a more predictable regulatory environment.

But there is an important caveat: the CLARITY Act is not law yet, and Armstrong's vote prediction is not a confirmed Senate outcome.

The real answer will come when senators cast their votes.

Until then, Armstrong's bullish assessment gives the crypto market another reason to watch Washington closely.
