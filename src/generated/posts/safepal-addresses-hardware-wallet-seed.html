SafePal Addresses Hardware Wallet Seed Phrase Randomness Incident

Top Crypto News 2026-08-02T12:58:09.599Z
Learn crypto

 


💡 Key Takeaways:

• Seed phrases generated by specific models (S1/S1 Pro/X1) are unaffected.
• Recovery phrases from impacted COLDCARD firmwares require attention.
• Best security practices remain essential for all crypto wallet users.

SafePal, a provider of cryptocurrency hardware wallets, has issued an update regarding a potential issue related to seed phrase randomness. In their communication via Twitter, SafePal confirmed that the seed phrases generated by their models—S1, S1 Pro, and X1—are not impacted by this incident. Users have been reassured that they do not need to regenerate their seed phrases or move funds from these devices.

The company’s announcement comes in light of concerns raised about certain firmware from competing hardware wallet manufacturer COLDCARD. According to SafePal's statement, recovery phrases originally generated using affected versions of COLDCARD firmware remain compromised even if imported into SafePal or other wallets. Users who find themselves in this situation are advised to follow COLDCARD’s official migration guidance for mitigating any risks associated with the compromised seeds.

Addressing the underlying technology employed in their devices, SafePal highlighted its dual-chip architecture and cryptographic mixing capabilities. This design approach enables the generation of fresh true-random entropy each time a wallet is created, thereby reducing reliance on any single chip or entropy source and enhancing overall security.

In addition to clarifying the status of its own products amidst ongoing discussions about crypto security vulnerabilities across various platforms and manufacturers, SafePal also provided some best practices for users regardless of which hardware wallet they utilize:

Never photograph or screenshot your seed phrase; avoid storing it digitally.
Only download and update firmware through official channels provided by manufacturers.
Treat requests for inputting your seed phrase—especially those asking you to “check randomness,” “update firmware,” or “claim compensation”—as phishing attempts.
