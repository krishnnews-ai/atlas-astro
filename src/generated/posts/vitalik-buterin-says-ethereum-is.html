Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin says Ethereum is no longer "just a blockchain." In a new essay published on September 27, he described the network as a "cryptographic world computer" — a hybrid system that combines blockchain consensus with modern cryptography, zero-knowledge proofs, and decentralized computation .

The essay, titled "The Cryptographic World Computer," outlines how Ethereum will change after the Hegota upgrade, which is expected in late 2026 or 2027.

Buterin wrote on X that Ethereum is "really not just a blockchain anymore" — it's "a hybrid architecture that combines blockchains and modern cryptography, to enable much more powerful properties" .

What Hegota Changes

Buterin described Hegota as Ethereum's last "normal" fork — meaning it will still include features that would look familiar to someone working on the network in 2015. After Hegota, everything changes.

The headline feature of Hegota is FOCIL (Fork-Choice Enforced Inclusion Lists). This is a censorship-resistance mechanism that forces validators to include valid transactions in blocks. Under FOCIL, a committee of 17 randomly selected validators creates a list of transactions that must be included. If a block builder ignores that list, the network rejects the block .

Why does this matter? In the past, large validators have excluded transactions tied to sanctioned protocols like Tornado Cash. FOCIL makes that kind of censorship much harder, because an attacker would need to control both the builder and the committee — an extremely difficult and costly task .

Buterin said FOCIL, combined with EIP-8141 (account abstraction), gives "guaranteed rapid inclusion" for almost any valid transaction, including smart wallet transactions and privacy protocol transactions, within one to two slots .

What Comes After Hegota

After Hegota, Ethereum's roadmap shifts toward what Buterin calls "cryptographic" infrastructure:

  • Recursive STARKs — Instead of every node downloading and re-executing every block, a single computer would generate a concise cryptographic proof that it followed the rules. Other nodes would verify that proof, which is orders of magnitude faster than re-executing the computation .
  • PeerDAS data sampling — Nodes would verify data availability by sampling parts of the dataset instead of downloading everything .
  • Quantum-resistant cryptography — The Ethereum Foundation has set a December 2029 target for making Ethereum's execution, consensus, and data layers quantum-resistant .
  • Lean consensus — A redesigned consensus mechanism aiming for faster finality and simpler architecture .

Buterin also named concrete performance targets for 2030: block time falling from about 12 seconds today to 4 to 8 seconds, and finality from several minutes to 8 to 32 seconds .

Why This Matters

Ethereum's shift from "download and re-execute" to "verify proofs" is a fundamental change in how blockchains work. It could reduce hardware requirements for validators while increasing throughput. Buterin said nodes should still obtain consensus and validity guarantees while storing less history and performing less redundant computation .

The change also has implications for Layer 2 networks. FOCIL is designed to strengthen Layer 2 settlement guarantees, making it harder for a single actor to censor transactions that need to settle on Ethereum .

Buterin acknowledged that making proof systems efficient enough for widespread use remains difficult. But he said AI tools are helping progress, and the 2030 vision is a technical direction rather than a locked schedule .
