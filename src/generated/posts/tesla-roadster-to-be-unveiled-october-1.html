Tesla is finally putting a date on one of the automotive industry's longest-running promises.

The next-generation Tesla Roadster will be unveiled on October 1, 2026, nearly nine years after Elon Musk first introduced the second-generation electric sports car as a prototype in 2017.

The upcoming event could be very different from a conventional Tesla product launch. Reports surrounding the Roadster program indicate that Tesla may demonstrate a vehicle equipped with SpaceX-related cold-gas thrusters, potentially allowing a remotely operated Roadster to briefly leave the ground.

Those details have not all been publicly confirmed by Tesla as final production specifications. The October 1 event should therefore be watched closely for confirmation of what technology will actually be demonstrated and which features will make it into customer vehicles.

The event is expected to take place in Waco, Texas. Reported reservation-holder invitations list an 8:30 p.m. Eastern Time start, a September 16, 2026 RSVP deadline and a 21-and-over age requirement.

Tesla Roadster October 1 Event Details

Tesla teased the event with the phrase "Go for launch" and an image showing what appear to be propulsion plumes behind the Roadster.

Reuters has reported that Tesla plans to unveil the long-delayed Roadster on October 1, making the event an important milestone for a vehicle first shown publicly in 2017.

Why the Tesla Roadster Reveal Is So Unusual

The biggest story surrounding the new Roadster may not simply be its acceleration or top speed. It is the possibility that Tesla could demonstrate a vehicle capable of briefly leaving the ground.

According to reporting on the Roadster development program, Tesla and SpaceX have been working on a cold-gas propulsion system intended to provide additional performance.

Reports have referred to the Roadster development project and its reported SpaceX-related propulsion work by the internal code name A-71. Reporting has also said that Tesla and SpaceX personnel demonstrated an early version of the system for Elon Musk in late April 2026.

The reported testing is significant because it suggests the propulsion concept had progressed beyond a purely theoretical idea.

However, Tesla has not publicly confirmed every detail of the reported A-71 system or stated that a flying or hovering capability will be included in every production Roadster.

Could the Tesla Roadster Actually Leave the Ground?

This is the detail that has transformed the Roadster story from a conventional electric-supercar launch into something much more unusual.

Reports indicate that a planned thruster demonstration could involve a remotely operated Roadster with no driver inside.

The reported cold-gas propulsion system is also said to be extremely loud. Spectators could therefore be positioned hundreds of yards away from the demonstration because of potential hearing risks.

If Tesla proceeds with the reported demonstration, the company could show how the Roadster's electric drivetrain works alongside the additional propulsion system.

The most dramatic possibility would be enough vertical thrust to temporarily reduce or overcome the vehicle's weight, allowing the car to leave the ground.

That does not mean Tesla has confirmed a production flying car.

The safest interpretation is that Tesla may demonstrate an extreme propulsion technology on October 1, while the final production Roadster could have a substantially different configuration.

What Is A-71 and How Could the SpaceX Technology Work?

The reported A-71 project is one of the most unusual aspects of Tesla's Roadster development.

The system is described in reporting as a cold-gas thruster technology associated with SpaceX. Unlike a conventional combustion rocket engine, a cold-gas system uses stored pressurized gas to generate thrust.

For a vehicle such as the Roadster, additional thrust could theoretically provide extremely rapid acceleration and assist with other high-performance maneuvers.

A sufficiently powerful arrangement could also generate vertical force, which explains the reports about a possible lift-off demonstration.

Nevertheless, the exact engineering configuration, thrust output and production availability of the system remain matters for Tesla to clarify publicly.

The Thruster Roadster May Not Be Street Legal

Another important distinction concerns road legality.

Reports indicate that the extreme thruster-equipped demonstration vehicle may not be street legal.

That would create an important difference between a potential SpaceX-enhanced demonstration Roadster and the conventional production vehicle intended for customers.

There has also been reporting about Tesla considering a more specialized, track-focused approach for the most extreme version of the Roadster.

That possibility would be comparable in concept to the limited, track-oriented programs offered by some exotic-car manufacturers, although Tesla has not publicly confirmed the final structure of such a program.

Until Tesla releases official specifications, the exact relationship between the road-going Roadster and any thruster-equipped version remains uncertain.

Tesla Reportedly Changed the Roadster's Entire Design

The Roadster Tesla is preparing to show in 2026 is reportedly much more than an updated version of the 2017 prototype.

During development, Tesla reportedly considered using an architecture based heavily on existing Model S Plaid components. That approach would have allowed the company to reuse more established Tesla technology.

Reports indicate that Tesla ultimately moved toward a much more ambitious design based around an all-new carbon-fibre structure.

This reported redesign helps explain why the Roadster program has taken so long. Instead of simply converting an existing Tesla platform into a two-door sports car, the company appears to have pursued a far more specialized hypercar architecture.

The result is expected to be substantially different from the original 2017 concept.
