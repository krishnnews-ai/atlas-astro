Ripple Adds XRP and RLUSD Support to Stripe and Tempo’s AI Agent Payment Protocol

ETFsBitcoin NewsMarket News



Ripple is taking XRP and RLUSD deeper into the emerging AI-agent economy after updating its XRPL AI Starter Kit to support the Machine Payments Protocol, an open payment standard co-authored by Stripe and Tempo.

The latest update gives developers a way to build AI agents that can use XRP and RLUSD when paying for digital services, including APIs, computing resources and other machine-readable services.

The development is significant because AI agents are increasingly moving beyond generating text and answering questions. The next phase involves software that can independently perform tasks, access services and make payments on behalf of users or businesses.

Ripple's latest developer tools are designed to give those agents access to blockchain-based payment infrastructure on the XRP Ledger.

Ripple Adds MPP Support to the XRPL AI Starter Kit

Ripple's updated XRPL AI Starter Kit now supports the Machine Payments Protocol (MPP) as well as the Open Wallet Standard.

MPP was introduced by Stripe and Tempo as an open, internet-native protocol designed specifically for payments initiated by software agents. The protocol allows an agent to request a digital resource, receive payment requirements, authorize the transaction and obtain the resource programmatically.

Ripple's integration connects that payment architecture with the XRP Ledger.

According to current reporting on Ripple's developer update, the toolkit supports payments in XRP and RLUSD, giving developers both a native XRP Ledger asset and a dollar-denominated stablecoin option for agentic transactions.

In other words, an AI agent can potentially pay for a service without a person having to manually complete a conventional checkout process.

What Is the Machine Payments Protocol?

The Machine Payments Protocol is designed around a simple idea: software agents should be able to pay for internet services as easily as they can request them.

Stripe and Tempo introduced MPP in March 2026, describing it as an open standard for agents and services to coordinate payments programmatically. The protocol is designed to support microtransactions, recurring payments and other machine-driven payment models.

A simplified MPP transaction can work like this:

  1. An AI agent requests a resource from a service.
  2. The service returns the required payment information.
  3. The agent authorizes the payment.
  4. The payment is processed through the supported payment infrastructure.
  5. The requested resource is delivered to the agent.

The resource could be an API call, data, computing power, AI inference or another digital service.

This is different from traditional online commerce, where a human normally creates an account, enters payment information and confirms a purchase.

MPP is being designed for an internet in which software itself can become an economic participant.

Why XRP Is Being Positioned for AI-Agent Payments

The AI-agent economy creates a different set of requirements for payment systems.

A human may make a few online payments every day. An autonomous software agent could potentially make many smaller payments while completing a larger task.

That makes factors such as settlement speed, transaction costs, wallet controls and programmability particularly important.

Ripple's XRPL AI Starter Kit was originally introduced to help developers build AI-agent applications that can transact using the XRP Ledger. Ripple highlighted use cases including payments for APIs, computing and other digital services.

The latest MPP integration expands the number of payment standards developers can work with when building those applications.

RLUSD Gives AI Agents a Dollar-Denominated Payment Option

XRP is only one part of the strategy.

Ripple is also bringing RLUSD, its dollar-denominated stablecoin, into the AI-payment infrastructure.

The difference matters for businesses that want blockchain settlement without exposing the price of a transaction to the volatility of XRP.

For example, an AI agent could theoretically request an API priced at a fixed dollar amount and settle that payment using a dollar-pegged digital asset.

That could make stablecoins particularly relevant for machine-to-machine commerce, where predictable pricing may be more important than exposure to a volatile asset.

Ripple previously added XRP and RLUSD support for AI-agent payments through the x402 payment standard when it launched the XRPL AI Starter Kit. The latest update adds MPP as another route for developers.

Ripple Is Supporting Multiple AI Payment Standards

One of the more interesting aspects of the update is that Ripple is not relying on a single emerging AI-payment protocol.

The XRPL AI Starter Kit already supports x402, while the new version adds support for MPP.

The Open Wallet Standard Is Also Important

Ripple's update is not limited to payments.

The XRPL AI Starter Kit also adds support for the Open Wallet Standard, which is intended to provide a common way for software to interact with blockchain wallets.

That matters because autonomous agents need more than a payment protocol. They also need a secure way to manage transactions and interact with wallets without receiving unrestricted access to private keys.

Wallet permissions, spending limits and transaction authorization can become critical safeguards when software is allowed to move money automatically.

For AI agents, the challenge is therefore not simply making blockchain payments faster. The infrastructure also needs to determine what an agent is allowed to spend, where it can send funds and under what conditions a transaction can be approved.

Ripple's AI Payment Push Started Earlier This Year

The latest MPP integration is part of a broader Ripple strategy rather than an isolated announcement.

Ripple launched its XRPL AI Starter Kit in June 2026, initially highlighting x402-powered payments using XRP and RLUSD. The company described the toolkit as a way for developers to build applications in which AI agents can transact for APIs, computing resources and other digital services.

The company is now expanding that toolkit to another major machine-payment standard.

That progression reflects a larger change in how the cryptocurrency industry is approaching AI.

Instead of treating AI simply as a tool for analyzing blockchain data or generating trading information, developers are increasingly exploring AI systems that can actually hold value, request services and execute transactions.

Why Machine-to-Machine Payments Could Become Important

AI agents could eventually operate continuously rather than only when a human opens an application.

An autonomous agent might monitor markets, collect data, call specialized AI models, purchase additional computing capacity and send information to another service as part of one workflow.

Every one of those actions could potentially involve a payment.

Traditional financial infrastructure was primarily designed around human users. Stripe itself argues that today's payment systems often require agents to navigate account creation, pricing pages, payment credentials and billing processes designed for people.

Machine-payment protocols attempt to remove those barriers by making payment instructions understandable and executable by software.

If that model scales, blockchain networks and stablecoins could become part of the underlying payment layer for autonomous software.

Ripple's XRP Ledger Is Targeting That Emerging Economy

The XRP Ledger is now being positioned by Ripple as infrastructure for this potential machine economy.

The appeal is not necessarily that AI agents need cryptocurrency for its own sake. Instead, the attraction is the ability to combine programmable software with blockchain-based settlement.

XRP can provide a native asset for transactions on XRPL, while RLUSD can provide a dollar-denominated alternative.

The result is a payment stack in which developers can choose the asset that best fits a particular application.

Technical Integration Does Not Equal Mass Adoption

There is an important distinction between what the new toolkit enables and what is already happening at commercial scale.

Ripple's update gives developers the technical tools to build MPP-based AI payment applications. It does not mean that millions of AI agents are already using XRP or RLUSD to purchase services.

The MPP integration is an infrastructure and developer milestone. Actual adoption will depend on developers building applications, businesses accepting machine payments and users trusting autonomous agents with financial permissions.

That distinction is especially important for XRP investors and the wider crypto market. A new integration can expand the potential utility of an asset without guaranteeing future transaction volumes or token-price performance.

What This Means for XRP and RLUSD

The immediate significance for XRP and RLUSD is that both assets are being positioned for a new category of digital transactions.

Instead of focusing exclusively on human-to-human or human-to-business payments, Ripple is targeting machine-to-machine commerce.

If AI agents become regular economic participants, they will need infrastructure for purchasing data, computing resources, software tools and other services.

Ripple is attempting to make XRP Ledger assets available within that emerging payment environment.

However, the success of that strategy will ultimately depend on real-world usage rather than the existence of the technical integration itself.

What Happens Next?

The next phase will likely be driven by developers experimenting with MPP, x402 and wallet standards across different AI-agent applications.

Ripple's continued development of the XRPL AI Starter Kit will also determine how easily developers can build, test and deploy those applications.

Meanwhile, Stripe and Tempo are continuing to develop MPP as a broader standard for machine payments. Stripe says MPP is intended to support both microtransactions and recurring payments while allowing businesses to use existing payment infrastructure.

If AI agents become widespread users of internet services, the ability to pay automatically could become an important piece of the AI infrastructure stack.

Bottom Line

Ripple has added Machine Payments Protocol support to the XRPL AI Starter Kit, giving developers another way to build AI-agent payment applications around XRP and RLUSD.

MPP was co-authored by Stripe and Tempo and is designed to let software agents coordinate payments with online services programmatically.

For Ripple, the move expands its existing AI-payment strategy beyond x402 and strengthens the XRP Ledger's position in the developing machine-to-machine economy.

The technology is still early, and technical support should not be confused with mass commercial adoption. But the direction is clear: Ripple wants XRP and RLUSD to be usable not only by people, but also by the autonomous software systems that may increasingly transact on the internet.
