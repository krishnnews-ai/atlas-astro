CFTC Launches Frontier Forum for AI and Agentic Finance on October 28

The U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission is launching a new public roundtable series focused on emerging technologies reshaping financial markets, with artificial intelligence and agentic finance set to headline the first session on October 28, 2026.

The initiative, called the Frontier Forum Series, was announced September 21 by the CFTC's Innovation Task Force. The agency said the forums will bring together builders and leaders from the public and private sectors to discuss new financial technologies and evolving market structures.

The first Frontier Forum will focus specifically on AI and agentic finance, putting autonomous and AI-driven financial technology directly into the discussion around the future of U.S. financial markets.

What Is the CFTC Frontier Forum?

The Frontier Forum is designed as a series of public discussions about technologies that are changing how financial markets operate.

According to the CFTC, each forum will provide a venue for public dialogue about financial technologies and evolving market structures. The agency says the discussions are intended to help it examine how it can support responsible innovation while protecting market participants and maintaining the integrity and resilience of U.S. financial markets.

The initiative is being organized by the CFTC's Innovation Task Force, which has been given a broader role in examining emerging areas of financial technology.

First Forum Will Focus on AI and Agentic Finance

The inaugural Frontier Forum is scheduled for Wednesday, October 28, 2026, and will focus on artificial intelligence and agentic finance.

The timing is notable because AI systems are increasingly being developed to perform tasks beyond generating text or analyzing information. Agentic systems can be designed to plan actions, interact with software and execute tasks with varying degrees of human oversight.

In financial markets, that raises questions about how AI systems could interact with trading, market infrastructure, risk management, compliance and other activities.

The CFTC has not yet published the complete agenda or list of speakers. The agency said information about the agenda, speakers and registration will be posted later.

Why Agentic Finance Is Becoming a Regulatory Topic

Traditional financial software generally follows predefined rules and instructions. Agentic AI introduces a different model in which software can potentially interpret information, make decisions within defined parameters and take actions through connected systems.

That creates potential opportunities as well as new regulatory questions.

For example, an AI agent could potentially monitor market information, identify conditions that meet predefined criteria and initiate an action through a connected financial system. The exact level of autonomy would depend on how the system is designed and what permissions it receives.

For regulators, the challenge is determining how existing market rules apply when technology becomes capable of performing increasingly complex activities with less direct human intervention.

CFTC Wants Public and Private Sector Input

The CFTC says the Frontier Forum will bring together people from both the public and private sectors.

That includes technology builders, financial-market participants, researchers and other stakeholders involved in developing or using emerging financial technologies.

The agency's stated objective is to engage directly with people who are building, using and studying these technologies while considering how regulation can support responsible innovation and protect market participants.

CFTC Chairman Michael S. Selig said the technologies transforming financial markets are advancing rapidly and that the agency needs to engage directly with the people building, using and studying them.

AI Could Change More Than Trading

The discussion around AI in financial markets extends beyond automated trading.

Financial institutions are exploring AI for areas such as market analysis, fraud detection, compliance, customer service, risk monitoring and operational processes.

Agentic systems could potentially combine several of these capabilities and perform multi-step tasks with less human intervention.

That also creates practical questions about accountability. If an autonomous system makes an incorrect decision, for example, firms and regulators may need to determine how responsibility is assigned between the technology provider, financial institution, human supervisor and other parties involved in the system.

Market Integrity and Resilience Are Part of the Discussion

The CFTC's announcement does not frame the Frontier Forum as a technology-only initiative.

The agency specifically says the forums will consider innovation alongside the protection of market participants and the integrity and resilience of U.S. financial markets.

That means future discussions could involve issues such as system reliability, cybersecurity, operational controls, market manipulation risks, transparency and oversight of AI-enabled financial activity.

However, the October 28 event itself should not be described as announcing new AI trading rules. The CFTC has announced a public discussion forum, not a final regulatory framework for agentic finance.

CFTC's Broader Innovation Agenda

The Frontier Forum fits into a wider innovation agenda at the CFTC.

The agency's Innovation Task Force identifies three core innovation themes: crypto assets and blockchain technologies, artificial intelligence and autonomous systems, and prediction markets and event contracts.

The CFTC says its innovation work includes public roundtables, written input, the Innovation Advisory Committee and industry engagement. The stated goal is to develop insights that can inform Commission policy, interpretations and potential rulemaking considerations.

This broader structure means the Frontier Forum is not limited to AI. Future sessions can address other technologies and market structures affecting the derivatives and financial markets overseen by the agency.

Public Can Nominate Speakers

The CFTC is also opening the process to public participation.

Members of the public can submit speaker nominations to the CFTC's Innovation Task Force through the email address provided in the agency's announcement.

The agency cautioned that the number of people allowed to attend in person may be limited and that visitors will be subject to security checks.

What the October 28 Forum Could Cover

The CFTC has not yet published the detailed agenda, so specific discussion topics should not be presented as confirmed.

Based on the announced focus on AI and agentic finance, however, areas likely to be relevant include the interaction between autonomous systems and financial markets, the use of AI in market infrastructure, oversight of AI-enabled financial activity and the balance between technological innovation and market protections.

The final agenda and speakers will determine the actual scope of the inaugural session.

Why This Matters for Crypto and Blockchain

Although the first Frontier Forum is focused on AI and agentic finance, the initiative is also relevant to the broader digital-asset industry.

AI and blockchain are increasingly being developed together in areas such as automated financial services, decentralized applications, trading infrastructure and autonomous software agents.

The CFTC's Innovation Task Force separately lists crypto assets and blockchain technologies among its core innovation themes. That creates a regulatory forum where developments across AI, blockchain and financial-market technology can potentially intersect.

Still, the October 28 meeting should be viewed primarily as an AI and agentic-finance discussion. Any future policy consequences will depend on what the CFTC learns from these discussions and any subsequent agency actions.

What Happens Next?

The immediate next step is the October 28 inaugural Frontier Forum.

The CFTC said additional information about the agenda, speakers and registration will be posted at a later date. The agency also invited public speaker nominations, creating an opportunity for industry participants and researchers to contribute to the discussion.

As AI systems become increasingly capable of interacting with financial infrastructure, the questions raised by agentic finance are likely to extend beyond technology into market structure, oversight and operational risk.

Bottom Line

The CFTC is launching the Frontier Forum Series to create a public venue for discussing emerging technologies that are changing U.S. financial markets.

The first session on October 28, 2026 will focus on artificial intelligence and agentic finance, bringing the growing role of autonomous financial technology into a formal regulatory discussion.

The initiative does not itself create new AI trading regulations. Instead, it gives regulators, technology builders, financial institutions and other stakeholders a structured forum to discuss how emerging systems can develop while financial-market integrity, resilience and participant protections remain part of the conversation.
