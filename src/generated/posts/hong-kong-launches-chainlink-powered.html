Hong Kong Launches Chainlink-Powered Tokenized Securities Framework

Top Crypto News 2026-08-06T13:17:08.031Z
Crypto Regulations

A coalition of financial and technology firms has unveiled a comprehensive infrastructure for tokenized securities in Hong Kong, standardizing issuance, distribution, and settlement under the city's regulatory framework.

The Announcement

On August 5, 2026, FORMS Syntron Information (HK), Chainlink, APEX Group, and CSpro officially launched the Tokenized Securities Framework (TSF) within Hong Kong's Blockchain Valley@Cyberport ecosystem . The initiative creates a standardized digital infrastructure covering the full lifecycle of Tokenized Securities Offerings (TSOs)—from regulated issuance and distribution through to final settlement .

The framework is designed to connect digital asset ecosystems with traditional financial infrastructure, enabling institutions to adopt tokenized securities without replacing their core systems .

ERC-3643: Compliance at the Token Level

The framework adopts the ERC-3643 token standard (also known as T-REX), which embeds compliance controls directly into the asset layer rather than applying them externally .

Unlike a standard ERC-20 token that any wallet can receive, an ERC-3643 token checks every transfer against embedded compliance rules and refuses to move to a wallet that fails identity verification . This ensures:

  • Investor eligibility checked on-chain

  • Transfer restrictions enforced automatically

  • Regulatory requirements travel with the asset at all times

"ERC-3643 puts compliance at the token level, ensuring that only eligible investors can hold and transfer tokens." — Chainlink 

The standard was formally accepted as an Ethereum Improvement Proposal in 2023 and is now governed by the ERC-3643 Association, whose members include DTCC, Apex Group, and Invesco .

🇭🇰 Why Hong Kong, Why Now

Hong Kong's regulatory environment has been evolving rapidly to accommodate tokenization:

DateDevelopment
November 2023SFC issues first circular on tokenized securities and retail investor access 
April 2026SFC permits secondary trading of tokenized SFC-authorised investment products on licensed VATPs 
August 2026TSF launches, building directly on this regulatory scaffolding 

The SFC's April 2026 circular was the game-changer. It explicitly permits secondary trading of tokenized products through licensed virtual asset trading platforms (VATPs), opening the door to 24/7 trading of instruments that previously only changed hands during traditional market hours .

The numbers behind the move:

  • 13 tokenized products offered to the Hong Kong public as of March 2026 

  • HK$10.7 billion (~$1.36B) in tokenized product AUM, up roughly sevenfold in a year 

  • 440+ fintech firms housed at Cyberport 

⚡ What Changes: T+0 vs. T+2 Settlement

The clearest way to understand the framework's impact is to follow a single fund purchase:

Traditional Mutual FundTSF-Powered Fund
T+2 settlement (2 business days)T+0 settlement (instant)
Business hours only24/7 availability
Broker → Clearing house → Manual compliance → Fund managerInvestor hits smart contract → Compliance checked on-chain → Token and payment swap at once → Settled

The T+2 gap ties up capital and forces both sides to carry counterparty risk. Removing it is where most of the cost saving lives .

🔗 Live Pilots: The Framework in Action

Two live pilots demonstrate the framework operating beyond theory:

  1. e-HKD Phase Two Programme: ANZ, China AMC, and Fidelity International demonstrated an Australian investor buying a Hong Kong-denominated money market fund natively. CCIP converted between e-HKD, tokenized deposits, and fund shares while ACE ran cross-border compliance checks .

  2. Project Ensemble: Standard Chartered tested tokenizing HKD, offshore renminbi, and USD accounts for Ant International's Whale treasury platform, giving corporate users round-the-clock access to liquidity that normally freezes outside banking hours .

📊 Hong Kong vs. Singapore: Two Tokenization Approaches

FactorHong Kong - TSFSingapore - Project Guardian

Singapore's Project Guardian pioneered institutional tokenization but tends to run on bespoke permissioned ledgers built case by case. Hong Kong is making the opposite wager: standardized, plug-and-play pipeline attracts more issuers than custom builds .
