Grant Cardone Adds 350 BTC to Fort Lauderdale Real Estate Deal

By CoinAINews Staff |

Grant Cardone is taking his Bitcoin strategy deeper into the real estate business.

The entrepreneur and real estate investor says Cardone Capital is adding 350 Bitcoin (BTC) to a Fort Lauderdale, Florida, real estate deal, creating a structure that combines multifamily property exposure with Bitcoin.

Cardone announced the move on X, saying he had been awarded a Fort Lauderdale property and was adding 350 BTC to the transaction.

Cardone Capital is also promoting what it calls a “Real Estate Bitcoin Hybrid,” built around 350 units and 350 BTC. The idea is straightforward: combine an income-producing real estate asset with exposure to Bitcoin in a single investment strategy.

That makes the deal notable. Rather than simply holding Bitcoin as a corporate treasury asset, Cardone is positioning BTC as part of a broader real estate investment model.

A Different Kind of Real Estate Deal

At the center of the announcement is a Fort Lauderdale property paired with 350 BTC.

Cardone Capital's promotional materials describe the opportunity as a 350-unit property combined with 350 BTC, with the property presented as being in a prime location and approximately 30% below replacement cost.

The approach is different from the traditional corporate Bitcoin strategy, where a company buys BTC and holds it on its balance sheet.

In Cardone's model, Bitcoin is being presented as another component of the investment alongside the property itself.

The reasoning behind the structure is easy to understand. Real estate can potentially generate rental income and appreciate over time, while Bitcoin offers exposure to a liquid digital asset with a very different risk and return profile.

Whether that combination ultimately works well for investors, however, will depend on how both sides of the investment perform.

How Much Is 350 BTC Worth?

The value of 350 BTC changes with the Bitcoin market.

One report valued the 350 BTC at approximately $22.3 million, based on a Bitcoin price of about $63,671 on August 4, when the transaction was reported.

Separately, social-media posts and other coverage have referenced a figure of roughly $26.9 million.

Neither figure should be treated as a fixed value for the deal. Bitcoin trades around the clock, so the dollar value of 350 BTC can change significantly within a short period.

For that reason, the 350 BTC figure is the more useful way to describe the Bitcoin component of the transaction, while dollar estimates should be tied to a specific Bitcoin price and date.

Why the Structure Stands Out

The most interesting part of Cardone's move isn't simply the number of Bitcoin involved. It is how the cryptocurrency is being positioned.

A conventional Bitcoin treasury strategy involves buying BTC and holding it as a reserve asset.

Cardone's approach attempts to put two very different assets into the same investment structure:

Real estate + Bitcoin.

The property can potentially provide rental income, while Bitcoin offers exposure to price appreciation but also introduces substantially higher volatility.

That combination could produce a different risk profile from owning either asset independently.

If the property performs well and Bitcoin appreciates, investors could potentially benefit from both sides of the strategy. If Bitcoin suffers a major decline, however, the cryptocurrency component could lose substantial value even if the underlying property continues generating rental income.

Cardone Capital Is Building a Broader Bitcoin Strategy

The Fort Lauderdale deal appears to fit into a larger Bitcoin strategy at Cardone Capital.

Recent reports have said the firm had accumulated more than 2,800 BTC, while its real estate portfolio had grown to more than 15,000 units and assets under management had exceeded $5.4 billion.

Those figures put the Fort Lauderdale transaction into a broader context.

Cardone has increasingly talked about Bitcoin alongside his traditional real estate business rather than treating cryptocurrency as a completely separate investment.

Cardone Capital's own materials also promote the Real Estate Bitcoin Hybrid, showing that the combination of property and BTC is being incorporated into its investment offering.

For investors familiar with Cardone's real estate business, that makes the strategy particularly interesting.

Instead of asking investors to choose between traditional property exposure and Bitcoin, the hybrid model attempts to combine both.

The Real Estate Side Still Matters

Bitcoin may be the headline-grabbing part of the transaction, but the underlying property remains central to the strategy.

Multifamily real estate is influenced by factors such as rents, occupancy rates, financing costs, operating expenses and property valuations.

Bitcoin works very differently.

BTC does not generate rental income, and its market price can move sharply in either direction. A strong Bitcoin market can boost the value of the cryptocurrency allocation, but a major downturn can have the opposite effect.

The hybrid strategy therefore does not remove investment risk. Instead, it combines two assets whose returns are driven by very different factors.

That distinction is important for investors evaluating the concept.

Why Bitcoin Investors Are Paying Attention

Bitcoin adoption has expanded beyond individual traders in recent years, with companies, investment firms and asset managers exploring different ways to gain exposure to the asset.

Cardone's strategy adds another possibility: using Bitcoin as part of a real estate investment structure.

That could appeal to investors who already understand property investing but also want exposure to Bitcoin.

However, the headline numbers do not tell the entire story.

Anyone considering an investment structure like this would need to understand how the BTC is held, who controls the assets, how gains and losses are accounted for, what fees apply, how the property is financed and what happens to the Bitcoin allocation during a major market downturn.

Those details can have a significant impact on the overall risk of the investment.

The Dollar Value Will Keep Changing

The different dollar estimates attached to the 350 BTC figure highlight one of the challenges of reporting cryptocurrency transactions.

At one Bitcoin price, 350 BTC might be worth around $22 million. At a higher price, the same 350 BTC could be worth considerably more.

That is why CoinAINews is treating 350 BTC as the primary transaction figure rather than presenting a single dollar valuation as permanent.

The approximately $22.3 million estimate reflects the Bitcoin price used when the transaction was reported, while the later $26.9 million figure reflects a different market valuation.

Neither changes the underlying fact that the announced Bitcoin allocation is 350 BTC.

Could the Model Spread to Other Real Estate Deals?

The bigger question is whether Cardone's approach becomes more common.

Real estate has traditionally been viewed as a relatively established alternative asset, while Bitcoin represents a newer and considerably more volatile form of investment.

Combining the two could attract investors who want exposure to both markets without treating them as completely separate portfolios.

But the strategy will ultimately have to be judged on performance.

A hybrid structure may look particularly attractive during a strong Bitcoin market. The test comes when BTC falls sharply while property values, financing costs or operating expenses are moving in the opposite direction.

That is where investors will get a better sense of how well the two assets actually work together.

What Happens Next?

The Fort Lauderdale transaction will be worth watching as Cardone Capital develops its Bitcoin-and-real-estate strategy.

The company's promotional materials currently highlight a 350-unit, 350-BTC hybrid, suggesting that Bitcoin is becoming an intentional part of its investment approach rather than simply an occasional purchase.

For Bitcoin investors, the deal provides another example of BTC being incorporated into traditional alternative-asset strategies.

For real estate investors, it raises a different question:

Can Bitcoin become a meaningful component of income-producing property investments?

Cardone appears to believe there is an opportunity to combine the two.

Whether the strategy produces attractive risk-adjusted returns will depend on the performance of both the property and Bitcoin over time.

The Bottom Line

Grant Cardone has announced that 350 BTC is being added to a Fort Lauderdale real estate deal, linking Bitcoin exposure with a multifamily property investment.

Cardone Capital is promoting a 350-unit, 350-BTC Real Estate Bitcoin Hybrid, making the transaction part of a broader strategy that brings cryptocurrency and real estate together.

The 350 BTC was previously valued at around $22.3 million based on Bitcoin's price at the time of the announcement, while other coverage has cited a figure closer to $26.9 million. Because BTC trades continuously, those dollar values can change quickly.

The more important story is the structure itself.

Cardone is attempting to combine Bitcoin and real estate within the same investment strategy, creating a model that could attract attention from investors in both markets.

Whether the approach delivers better returns or diversification than holding the assets separately remains an open question.

For now, the Fort Lauderdale deal represents an interesting development in the growing overlap between Bitcoin and traditional alternative investments.
