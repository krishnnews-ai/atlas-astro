Hyperliquid in Advanced Talks With Kraken Parent to Bring Perpetual Futures to U.S. Traders

Top Crypto News 2026-09-01T09:20:52.557Z
Bitcoin NewsCrypto RegulationsLearn crypto

Hyperliquid could be moving closer to the U.S. market.

Hyperliquid Labs is reportedly in advanced talks with Payward, the parent company of Kraken, over a plan that could bring selected Hyperliquid-linked perpetual futures to U.S. traders through Payward's regulated Bitnomial exchange, according to Bloomberg.

The development is significant because Hyperliquid has become a major venue for crypto perpetual futures outside the United States, while U.S. traders have faced restrictions on accessing the platform's products.

However, there is an important distinction: this is still a reported proposal, not a completed U.S. launch. Regulatory approval remains necessary before the proposed arrangement could move forward.

What Is Hyperliquid Discussing With Payward?

The reported discussions center on allowing U.S. traders to access a selected group of perpetual futures connected to Hyperliquid's ecosystem.

Rather than giving U.S. customers direct access to Hyperliquid's existing platform, the proposed structure would use Bitnomial, a U.S.-regulated derivatives exchange owned by Payward.

Bloomberg reported that Payward has presented the proposed structure to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC). The talks are still ongoing, and the final structure could change during the regulatory process.

Read the Bloomberg report on the reported Hyperliquid-Payward talks.

Why Bitnomial Matters

Bitnomial is an important part of the potential arrangement because it provides the regulated U.S. market infrastructure that Hyperliquid would need for a compliant derivatives offering.

Bitnomial says it operates as a CFTC-regulated exchange offering crypto-settled futures and options, along with perpetual futures and other digital-asset products.

Payward acquired Bitnomial in 2026, bringing the exchange into the broader Kraken parent company structure.

Why Perpetual Futures Are Important

Perpetual futures have become one of the most heavily traded products in the crypto market.

Unlike traditional futures contracts, perpetual contracts do not have a conventional expiration date. Traders can maintain positions for longer periods while paying or receiving funding payments depending on market conditions.

Hyperliquid has built much of its growth around this type of trading.

A regulated U.S. route could therefore give Hyperliquid access to a much larger pool of traders while allowing those customers to interact with the products through U.S. market infrastructure.

But This Is Not a Confirmed U.S. Launch

This is the point traders should pay the most attention to.

The current reporting describes advanced talks. It does not say that Hyperliquid has already received approval to launch perpetual futures for American customers.

The proposed structure still needs to clear the relevant regulatory process.

In other words, the sequence is more accurately described as:

  1. Hyperliquid and Payward discuss a potential arrangement.
  2. Payward presents the proposed structure to regulators.
  3. Regulators review the structure.
  4. If approved, the parties can work toward implementation.
  5. Only then could eligible U.S. traders potentially begin using the products.

That distinction is important because a regulatory proposal can change, take longer than expected or fail to receive approval.

What This Could Mean for Hyperliquid

For Hyperliquid, a regulated U.S. pathway could be a major expansion opportunity.

The United States remains one of the world's largest financial markets, with substantial demand for crypto trading and derivatives.

Access to American traders could increase the potential user base for Hyperliquid-linked products and potentially make the ecosystem more attractive to professional market participants.

But regulatory access also comes with restrictions.

The products available in the United States may have different eligibility requirements, leverage rules and market structures from those available elsewhere.

What This Could Mean for Payward

The talks also highlight the strategic importance of Payward's Bitnomial acquisition.

Payward now has access to U.S.-regulated derivatives infrastructure through Bitnomial, creating another potential channel for bringing crypto-native products into the American market.

Bitnomial currently describes its platform as a CFTC-regulated exchange offering perpetuals, futures, options and other crypto products.

That makes Bitnomial a natural piece of the proposed Hyperliquid strategy: Hyperliquid brings the trading ecosystem and product demand, while Bitnomial provides regulated U.S. market infrastructure.

A Potential Bridge Between Crypto and U.S. Regulation

The broader significance of the talks goes beyond Hyperliquid.

Crypto companies have spent years trying to find ways to serve American customers without running afoul of U.S. derivatives regulations.

A structure involving a decentralized trading ecosystem on one side and a regulated derivatives exchange on the other could become a model worth watching.

If the arrangement eventually receives approval and attracts meaningful trading activity, other crypto platforms could explore similar approaches.

Could the News Affect HYPE?

The potential U.S. expansion is naturally relevant to HYPE, Hyperliquid's native token.

A successful U.S. expansion could increase visibility around the Hyperliquid ecosystem and potentially attract additional users and liquidity.

However, traders should avoid treating the reported talks as an automatic bullish signal.

There is still a long gap between negotiations and actual U.S. trading activity.

For HYPE, the more meaningful milestones would be regulatory approval, an official launch, actual U.S. user adoption and sustained trading volume.

What the Report Does Not Mean

There are several conclusions investors should avoid making at this stage.

It does not mean Hyperliquid has already launched in the United States.

It does not mean every Hyperliquid perpetual will become available to U.S. traders.

It does not mean regulatory approval is guaranteed.

And it does not guarantee a particular price move for HYPE.

The current development is best described as a potentially important regulatory and market-access discussion.

The Bottom Line

Hyperliquid is reportedly in advanced talks with Payward, Kraken's parent company, over a structure that could bring selected perpetual futures to U.S. traders through Payward-owned Bitnomial.

The proposal is notable because Bitnomial provides a CFTC-regulated U.S. derivatives platform, potentially giving Hyperliquid a path into the American market without simply opening its existing platform to U.S. customers.

But the deal is not finalized, there is no confirmed launch date, and regulatory approval remains a critical step.

For now, the most accurate takeaway is simple: Hyperliquid is exploring a regulated route into the U.S. market, and Bitnomial could become the bridge.

If the proposal ultimately receives clearance and attracts meaningful U.S. demand, it could become one of the more significant developments yet in the convergence of decentralized crypto trading and regulated American derivatives markets.
