U.S. Bank Launches USBDC Stablecoin Pilot on Stellar for Cross-Border Payments

Top Crypto News 2026-09-09T13:29:39.781Z
StablecoinTrending NewsBitcoin News



By CoinAINews Staff

U.S. Bank has taken a major step into bank-issued stablecoins, completing a live pilot transaction using its proprietary USBDC dollar-backed stablecoin on the Stellar blockchain.

The transaction moved value between U.S. Bank entities in North America and Europe, marking a shift from the bank's earlier stablecoin experimentation toward a live operational test of blockchain-based money movement.

U.S. Bank said the pilot was designed to demonstrate that it can move funds on a public blockchain while maintaining its existing finance, risk, compliance and operational controls.

The development is significant for the stablecoin industry because the experiment involves a major U.S. bank using its own dollar-backed digital asset on a public blockchain rather than relying solely on an existing third-party stablecoin.

U.S. Bank's USBDC Goes From Testing to a Live Payment

U.S. Bank announced that it successfully executed a live pilot transaction using USBDC for a cross-border payment between its North American and European entities.

The transaction was completed on Stellar, a blockchain that has developed a strong focus on payments, tokenized assets and financial infrastructure.

According to U.S. Bank, the pilot tested several important functions of the stablecoin, including minting, payment execution, redemption, freezing and clawback capabilities.

Those features are particularly important for a regulated financial institution. A traditional bank needs more than the ability to send a token from one blockchain address to another. It also needs controls that allow the institution to manage transactions, meet compliance requirements and respond to operational or regulatory events.

U.S. Bank said the pilot demonstrated how its blockchain infrastructure can work alongside the bank's existing financial, risk, compliance and operations systems.

What Is USBDC?

USBDC is U.S. Bank's proprietary U.S. dollar-backed stablecoin.

Unlike Bitcoin or other volatile crypto assets, a dollar-backed stablecoin is designed to maintain a stable value relative to the U.S. dollar and can be used for digital payments and settlement.

In U.S. Bank's case, USBDC is being developed as part of the bank's broader digital-assets and money-movement strategy.

The bank created its dedicated Digital Assets and Money Movement organization in 2025 to work on areas including stablecoin issuance, cryptocurrency custody, asset tokenization and digital money movement.

U.S. Bank has also been involved in the stablecoin ecosystem as a reserve custodian. In 2025, it announced that it would provide custody services for reserves backing payment stablecoins issued by Anchorage Digital Bank.

Why Stellar Matters to the U.S. Bank Stablecoin

The choice of Stellar is one of the most important parts of the USBDC development.

Stellar was designed with payments and asset issuance in mind, and the network has increasingly attracted financial institutions exploring tokenized assets and stablecoin-based settlement.

Stellar says its infrastructure supports global payments, asset tokenization and on-chain financial applications. The network also hosts established dollar-backed assets such as Circle's USDC.

For U.S. Bank, using a public blockchain allows the bank to test whether blockchain settlement can improve the movement of money while still retaining the controls expected from a regulated financial institution.

That combination—public blockchain infrastructure plus traditional banking controls—is central to the USBDC experiment.

USBDC Could Target 24/7 Money Movement

One of the biggest potential advantages of a bank-issued stablecoin is that blockchain networks do not operate according to the traditional banking calendar.

U.S. Bank said USBDC could eventually support applications involving 24/7 transaction capabilities.

That could be particularly relevant for cross-border treasury operations, where traditional payment systems can involve multiple intermediaries, operating hours and settlement processes.

However, U.S. Bank has not announced that USBDC is already available as a general-purpose customer payment product. The current development is a live pilot and part of the bank's continuing digital-asset strategy.

U.S. Bank Is Building Its Own Digital Asset Platform

The USBDC pilot also tested U.S. Bank's internally developed Digital Asset Platform.

The bank describes the platform as infrastructure for issuing, managing and moving tokenized assets while connecting blockchain networks with its traditional financial systems.

That infrastructure could eventually allow U.S. Bank to use blockchain technology without requiring every part of its existing banking operation to be rebuilt around a public network.

This is an important distinction. The bank is not simply experimenting with a cryptocurrency wallet. It is testing an infrastructure layer designed to connect blockchain-based assets with conventional banking operations.

USBDC Could Go Beyond Cross-Border Payments

U.S. Bank said it is exploring several potential applications for its stablecoin.

These include:

  • Cross-border treasury operations
  • Liquidity management
  • Collateral mobility
  • 24/7 money movement
  • Other institutional settlement applications

These use cases point toward a broader transformation in how financial institutions could move money between their own entities and counterparties.

Instead of using blockchain primarily as a speculative asset market, banks are increasingly examining whether tokenized dollars can function as programmable settlement instruments.

This Is Not Yet a Retail Stablecoin Launch

There is an important distinction between saying “U.S. Bank launched USBDC” and saying that USBDC is now available to everyone.

The bank has completed a live pilot, but it has not announced a broad commercial rollout of USBDC for retail customers.

U.S. Bank also has not provided a specific date for when the stablecoin could become generally available to clients.

For that reason, the most accurate description is that U.S. Bank has launched and executed a live USBDC pilot on Stellar, rather than claiming that the stablecoin is already a widely available payment product.

USBDC Comes as Banks Race Into Stablecoins

U.S. Bank's move comes as traditional financial institutions increasingly explore stablecoins as a new payment and settlement technology.

Reuters reported that several major banks—including Goldman Sachs, Bank of America, Citigroup and Wells Fargo—are planning a company aimed at issuing a dollar-pegged cryptocurrency, with a targeted launch in the first half of 2027.

The growing interest is also being supported by the development of a clearer U.S. regulatory framework for payment stablecoins.

The GENIUS Act established a federal framework for payment stablecoins, while regulators have continued to clarify how qualifying stablecoins should be treated under securities rules.

That regulatory progress could make it easier for banks and other regulated financial institutions to develop blockchain-based payment products.

Why This Matters for Stellar and XLM

USBDC's use of Stellar is also significant for the broader Stellar ecosystem.

A live transaction involving a major U.S. bank provides another real-world example of Stellar being used for institutional financial infrastructure.

However, the USBDC announcement should not be interpreted as a guarantee that XLM will rise in price.

The pilot demonstrates use of the Stellar blockchain, but that is different from announcing a direct investment in XLM or a specific future price impact for the token.

For investors, the more meaningful development is the potential validation of Stellar as infrastructure for regulated financial applications.

What Happens Next for USBDC?

The next stage will be determining whether the pilot can move beyond controlled transactions and into broader institutional use.

U.S. Bank is already evaluating additional applications involving liquidity, collateral and treasury operations.

If those experiments prove commercially useful, USBDC could eventually become part of a wider digital money infrastructure connecting traditional banking systems with public blockchains.

But that transition will depend on regulatory requirements, operational controls, client demand and the economics of blockchain settlement.

The Bigger Shift: Banks Are Bringing Dollars On-Chain

The most important part of the USBDC announcement may not be the name of the stablecoin or even the size of the pilot.

It is the direction of the financial industry.

For years, public blockchains were primarily associated with cryptocurrencies and decentralized finance. Banks are now increasingly testing whether the same infrastructure can support regulated money movement.

U.S. Bank's live USBDC transaction on Stellar is another example of that shift.

If bank-issued stablecoins become widely adopted, blockchain networks could increasingly operate as settlement infrastructure for traditional financial institutions rather than simply as markets for digital assets.

Bottom Line

U.S. Bank has moved its stablecoin strategy another step forward with a live USBDC transaction on Stellar.

The pilot transferred value between U.S. Bank entities in North America and Europe and tested core stablecoin functions including minting, redemption, freezing and clawback.

The announcement does not mean USBDC is already a mass-market stablecoin. Instead, it shows that one of the largest U.S. banking institutions is testing whether a proprietary dollar-backed digital asset can operate on public blockchain infrastructure while remaining connected to traditional banking controls.

For the stablecoin sector, that may be the bigger story: banks are no longer just watching blockchain payments—they are beginning to test their own digital dollars in live environments.
