Coinbase and Robinhood Could Benefit as SEC Opens Door to Tokenized U.S. Stocks

Top Crypto News 2026-09-21T10:19:33.078Z
Crypto RegulationsEditor&apos;s ChoiceMarket News


Wall Street analysts are turning their attention to Coinbase and Robinhood after the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission opened a new regulatory path for trading tokenized U.S. stocks on blockchain networks.

Goldman Sachs analysts said Coinbase could benefit across several parts of its business, including custody, tokenization infrastructure and USDC-related settlement activity. Separately, Citizens analysts expect Robinhood to move quickly to adapt its tokenized-equity products to the new U.S. framework, pointing to the company's offshore traction and its work on Robinhood Chain.

The SEC's move could therefore become an important step in bringing blockchain-based securities closer to traditional U.S. capital markets.

What Did the SEC Announce?

On September 17, 2026, the SEC announced a temporary, conditional "Innovation Exemption" for certain venues seeking to trade tokenized National Market System, or NMS, stocks.

The exemption allows qualifying Tokenized Securities Venues, known as TSVs, to use permissioned automated market makers and liquidity pools for secondary trading of eligible tokenized stocks.

The exemption is scheduled to expire after five years, giving the SEC time to gather information and consider whether additional or more permanent regulatory changes are necessary.

SEC Chairman Paul Atkins described the initiative as a step toward bringing U.S. capital markets further into the digital age while maintaining investor-protection requirements.

Tokenized Stocks Must Preserve Shareholder Rights

The SEC framework does not simply allow any cryptocurrency that tracks the price of a stock to trade under the exemption.

Eligible tokenized stocks must provide holders with the same rights and privileges as the corresponding traditional shares.

Those rights include the ability to receive dividends and exercise voting rights.

The framework also distinguishes eligible tokenized securities from synthetic products that merely provide price exposure to an underlying stock without representing the corresponding ownership rights.

This distinction could become particularly important for companies such as Robinhood, which already offers tokenized stock products outside the United States.

Why Goldman Sachs Sees an Opportunity for Coinbase

Goldman Sachs analysts see several potential ways for Coinbase to benefit if tokenized U.S. equity markets expand.

One area is tokenization infrastructure. Coinbase has been developing services designed to help institutions bring financial assets onchain.

Another opportunity is institutional custody. As tokenized securities become more widely used by financial institutions, demand could increase for regulated infrastructure capable of holding and managing digital representations of traditional assets.

Goldman also highlighted Coinbase's connection to stablecoins and the broader onchain financial ecosystem.

Coinbase has a major relationship with USDC, the dollar-backed stablecoin issued by Circle, and operates Base, its Ethereum layer-2 blockchain network.

If tokenized stocks develop into a larger onchain market, those businesses could potentially create additional activity around trading, custody, settlement and tokenization infrastructure.

Coinbase's Existing Tokenized-Equity Push

Coinbase is already working on tokenized equities, which could give the company a starting point as the U.S. regulatory environment changes.

However, the SEC exemption still contains specific technical and regulatory requirements. A platform seeking to operate under the framework must meet the conditions established by the SEC, including requirements relating to trading venues, tokenized-stock rights, smart contracts and public disclosures.

That means Coinbase's existing infrastructure does not automatically qualify every product for the new exemption.

Instead, the company would need to ensure that individual products and trading arrangements comply with the SEC's conditions.

Why Robinhood Could Move Quickly

Citizens analysts see a different but potentially significant opportunity for Robinhood.

The brokerage already has experience offering tokenized exposure to U.S. equities outside the United States. Citizens analysts believe that international traction could help Robinhood adapt its offering as the U.S. regulatory environment opens.

Robinhood has also been developing Robinhood Chain, a blockchain initiative aimed at supporting onchain financial activity.

That combination of an existing customer base, tokenized-equity experience and blockchain infrastructure could give the company a foundation for expanding its products.

Robinhood Still Has Work to Do

The SEC framework does not mean Robinhood's existing offshore stock tokens can simply be moved into the U.S. market unchanged.

Analysts have pointed out that some existing tokenized-stock products provide economic exposure to shares without giving holders all of the shareholder rights required under the SEC's new framework.

To operate within the U.S. framework, products may therefore need changes involving shareholder rights, including features such as voting and redemption.

The SEC's requirements also give issuers an opportunity to object before certain third-party tokenized versions of their shares can begin trading.

That could make the relationship between companies, tokenization platforms and trading venues an important part of the emerging market.

USDC Could Become Part of the Settlement Layer

The potential impact extends beyond exchanges and brokerages.

Analysts have also pointed to Circle's USDC as a potential beneficiary if tokenized securities trading expands.

Onchain equity markets need digital forms of cash or other settlement assets. A regulated dollar-backed stablecoin such as USDC could potentially be used for settlement, collateral and liquidity in those markets.

This creates an additional connection between tokenized securities and the existing stablecoin ecosystem.

For Coinbase, increased USDC activity could also be relevant because of its broader economic relationship with the stablecoin and its role in the crypto infrastructure surrounding it.

What Makes the SEC Framework Different?

The SEC's approach is significant because it creates a specific regulatory pathway for tokenized securities rather than treating every blockchain-based stock product as if it were identical.

Under the exemption, qualifying venues can use permissioned automated market makers and liquidity pools while operating on public, permissionless distributed ledgers.

The smart contracts used by qualifying venues must be auditable and public.

The framework also requires a tokenized stock to stop trading when trading in the underlying stock is halted on its primary listing exchange.

These conditions are designed to connect the blockchain-based version of a security with the traditional market in which the underlying stock trades.

Trading Limits Could Slow the Initial Market

The SEC's exemption also contains limits on the number of securities that can be listed and the amount of trading activity permitted for certain categories of tokenized stocks.

Those limits mean the initial market is unlikely to immediately replicate the scale of traditional stock exchanges.

For Coinbase and Robinhood, the early opportunity may therefore be less about replacing existing exchanges and more about developing new infrastructure for blockchain-based trading and settlement.

The SEC has said the temporary framework will allow the agency to observe how tokenized securities markets develop before considering additional regulatory action.

Why Tokenized Stocks Matter for Crypto

Tokenized equities represent a potential bridge between traditional financial markets and blockchain infrastructure.

Instead of representing a purely digital asset, a compliant tokenized stock can represent an interest in a traditional security while using blockchain technology for aspects of trading, settlement and ownership records.

Supporters of tokenization argue that blockchain-based markets could eventually offer longer trading hours, faster settlement, programmable financial products and broader access to financial infrastructure.

The SEC's exemption does not guarantee that all of those benefits will materialize. Adoption will depend on market liquidity, investor demand, issuer participation, technology and future regulatory decisions.

SEC Move Comes After CLARITY Act Setback

The SEC announcement also arrives shortly after the Senate failed to advance the CLARITY Act through a September 15 procedural vote.

That makes the SEC's action particularly relevant to the broader U.S. crypto-regulation debate.

Congressional legislation and agency action are not interchangeable. A law can establish statutory authority, while the SEC's Innovation Exemption operates within the agency's existing legal authority and is explicitly temporary and conditional.

The SEC itself has described the exemption as an initial step while it considers further action.

What Happens Next for Coinbase and Robinhood?

The next stage will likely focus on how quickly companies adapt their products to the SEC's conditions.

For Coinbase, the focus could include tokenization infrastructure, custody and the development of compliant tokenized-equity products.

For Robinhood, the challenge is likely to include adapting existing tokenized-equity products to U.S. requirements while expanding the infrastructure behind its blockchain strategy.

For Circle and USDC, the key question is whether tokenized securities generate meaningful demand for stablecoin-based settlement and liquidity.

The SEC's five-year exemption gives the market time to test these models, but the eventual regulatory framework could change as the agency receives public feedback and observes actual market activity.

Bottom Line

The SEC's new Innovation Exemption is creating a regulatory pathway for certain tokenized U.S. stocks to trade through qualifying blockchain-based venues.

Goldman Sachs analysts see potential opportunities for Coinbase across custody, tokenization infrastructure and related stablecoin activity, while Citizens analysts expect Robinhood to move quickly to adapt its tokenized-equity business to the U.S. framework.

The opportunity is not guaranteed. Coinbase and Robinhood will still need to meet the SEC's conditions, while the growth of tokenized equities will depend on issuers, investors, liquidity and future regulatory decisions.

What is changing is the regulatory environment. With the SEC allowing a five-year pathway for qualifying tokenized-stock venues, blockchain infrastructure is moving another step closer to the traditional U.S. securities market.
