Kalshi CEO Says NBA Prediction-Market Deal Could Arrive “Really Soon”

By CoinAINews Staff

A potential partnership between the NBA and prediction-market platforms could be getting closer.

Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour told RotoWire that a deal involving data, market integrity and marketing between the NBA and prediction markets could arrive “really soon.” His comments come as the NBA reportedly moves closer to formalizing relationships with prediction-market companies ahead of the 2026-27 season.

However, there is an important distinction: the NBA has not publicly announced a finalized Kalshi partnership matching those terms. The current reporting points to negotiations and an expected framework rather than a completed deal.

What Did Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour Say?

Mansour discussed the future of prediction markets and their relationship with major U.S. sports leagues during an interview with RotoWire.

When discussing the NBA and other major leagues, he indicated that developments could come very soon. The areas highlighted include three elements that could become important to any eventual agreement:

  • Data: access to official sports information used by prediction markets.
  • Integrity: cooperation aimed at identifying and addressing suspicious activity.
  • Marketing: commercial and promotional relationships between prediction-market companies and sports organizations.

RotoWire's reporting separately indicated that the NBA is close to finalizing a framework related to prediction-market access to league data and integrity systems.

Is the NBA Already Partnering With Kalshi?

Not officially, based on the information currently available.

That distinction is important because the CoinMarketCap post refers to a potential deal that could arrive soon, rather than announcing that an NBA-Kalshi agreement has already been signed.

Front Office Sports has reported that the NBA is expected to announce deals with multiple prediction-market companies as the 2026-27 season approaches. Another industry report said the league wants to finalize partnerships before the new season begins.

That means the eventual arrangement may involve more than one prediction-market operator rather than being exclusive to Kalshi.

Why Does NBA Data Matter?

Prediction markets depend on reliable information to determine the outcome of event contracts.

For basketball-related markets, that can include official game results, player statistics, schedules and other league information used to settle contracts.

An official data relationship can give market operators a clearly defined source of information while giving the league greater visibility into how its data is being used.

For the NBA, the relationship could also provide a formal channel for monitoring prediction-market activity involving its games.

Integrity Could Be the Bigger Issue

The potential partnership is not simply about selling access to NBA statistics.

Market integrity is one of the biggest issues surrounding sports prediction markets.

Sports leagues have an obvious interest in detecting activity that could indicate manipulation, insider information or other behavior that threatens the integrity of competitions.

The NBA has already discussed prediction-market oversight with U.S. regulators. Earlier this year, NBA Commissioner Adam Silver said the league had been talking with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission about an integrity framework for basketball-related prediction-market trading.

That history helps explain why an eventual commercial arrangement could include an integrity component rather than being limited to marketing.

Prediction Markets Are Expanding Across Sports

The NBA is considering these relationships at a time when prediction-market companies are rapidly expanding their presence in professional sports.

Kalshi recently became the official prediction-market partner of the U.S. Open, giving the company another major sports relationship.

Other prediction-market platforms have also been pursuing sports partnerships, while individual professional teams have begun exploring commercial relationships with the industry.

The trend suggests that prediction markets are moving beyond being purely online trading platforms and are increasingly seeking direct relationships with the leagues whose events underpin their markets.

Why the NBA Deal Could Be Important for Kalshi

An NBA partnership would be strategically significant for Kalshi because basketball is one of the largest sports audiences in the United States.

A formal relationship could potentially give Kalshi access to official league data, stronger integrity cooperation and additional marketing opportunities.

It could also help prediction markets present themselves as a more established part of the sports ecosystem.

But the commercial upside comes with regulatory risk.

Kalshi Is Still Facing a Major Regulatory Fight

The timing of the potential NBA partnership is especially significant because Kalshi is fighting a broader legal battle over sports prediction markets.

Kalshi argues that its event contracts fall under federal commodities regulation. Several states and regulators have challenged that position, arguing that sports-related prediction contracts can function like gambling and should be subject to state-level rules.

The legal conflict intensified after the Ninth Circuit ruled against Kalshi in a case involving Nevada regulators. At the same time, a separate federal appeals court had previously sided with Kalshi in litigation involving New Jersey, creating a significant split over regulatory authority.

New Jersey has now asked the U.S. Supreme Court to consider whether states have authority to regulate sports prediction markets such as Kalshi.

That means any major sports partnership will develop alongside an unresolved regulatory debate.

Would an NBA Deal Mean Prediction Markets Are Legal Everywhere?

No.

A commercial agreement between the NBA and a prediction-market company would not automatically settle the legal question over whether sports event contracts are regulated as financial products or gambling.

The legal classification of prediction markets remains under debate, and the outcome of ongoing court cases could have implications for how these products operate across the United States.

In other words, an NBA partnership could be commercially important without resolving the industry's regulatory uncertainty.

Could the NBA Work With Multiple Prediction Markets?

That is a possibility supported by current reporting.

Front Office Sports reported that the NBA is expected to announce deals with multiple prediction markets, rather than necessarily selecting one exclusive platform.

If that happens, companies such as Kalshi and other prediction-market operators could potentially compete for relationships with the league under a common framework.

The final structure, however, will depend on what the NBA officially announces.

What Should Investors and Traders Watch Next?

The most important development will be an official announcement from the NBA or the prediction-market companies involved.

Three details will be particularly important:

  1. Which prediction markets are included?
  2. What data and integrity rights will they receive?
  3. Will the agreements include marketing or sponsorship rights?

The answers could determine how significant the NBA's entry into formal prediction-market partnerships ultimately becomes.

What This Means for the Prediction-Market Industry

If the NBA finalizes agreements with prediction-market operators, it would represent another major step in the industry's push into mainstream sports.

For prediction-market companies, partnerships with major leagues can provide credibility, data access and new customer-acquisition opportunities.

For sports leagues, the appeal is more complicated. Prediction markets can create another form of fan engagement and a new commercial channel, but leagues also have to consider integrity, consumer protection and regulatory concerns.

That balance is likely to shape the NBA's eventual approach.

Bottom Line

Kalshi CEO Tarek Mansour says a prediction-market agreement involving the NBA could arrive “really soon.”

Current reporting supports the idea that NBA negotiations are advanced and that the league could announce prediction-market partnerships before the 2026-27 season begins.

But there is still an important line between “close to a deal” and “deal officially signed.” As of September 7, 2026, the NBA has not publicly confirmed a finalized Kalshi partnership covering data, integrity and marketing.

If an agreement is announced, it could become one of the most important developments yet for the growing U.S. prediction-market industry.
