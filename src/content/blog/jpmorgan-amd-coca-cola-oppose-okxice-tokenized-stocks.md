The push to bring Wall Street stocks onto blockchain networks is facing growing resistance from the companies whose shares are involved. JPMorgan Chase, AMD, Coca-Cola, Dell Technologies, Applied Materials, SanDisk and Adobe have joined the list of companies objecting to the proposed tokenized-stock trading platform operated by OKXICE.

The latest objections bring the reported total to 33 companies, according to an October 28 report from Japanese crypto news outlet New Economy, citing an October 27 notice from OKXICE. The companies span banking, semiconductors, consumer goods, technology hardware and software.

Coin Bureau highlighted the development on X, putting the combined market value of the companies at approximately $17 trillion. That figure is an estimate based on the companies' reported market capitalizations, not the amount of money invested in tokenized stocks or the value of the OKXICE platform.

The key distinction is that these companies have objected to having tokenized versions of their shares offered through a particular proposed trading venue. Their objections should not automatically be interpreted as opposition to every form of asset tokenization.

## Which Seven Companies Have Objected?

The latest notice adds seven prominent names to a list that has expanded rapidly during October.

The newly reported objectors are:

- **JPMorgan Chase (JPM):** One of the largest U.S. banking groups, with extensive operations in traditional finance and blockchain-based financial infrastructure.
- **Advanced Micro Devices (AMD):** A semiconductor company whose products include processors and data-center chips.
- **The Coca-Cola Company (KO):** A global beverage company.
- **Dell Technologies (DELL):** A technology company focused on computers, enterprise infrastructure and related services.
- **Applied Materials (AMAT):** A supplier of equipment and services used in semiconductor manufacturing.
- **SanDisk (SNDK):** A data-storage technology company.
- **Adobe (ADBE):** A software company known for creative, document and digital-experience products.

The seven additions bring the reported objection count to 33. Earlier notices had already named companies including Microsoft, Alphabet, Goldman Sachs, Broadcom, Qualcomm, Wells Fargo and Cisco Systems.

The companies listed in the latest update do not all have the same business model or necessarily share the same concerns. The public objection notices generally do not explain each company's reasoning, so it would be premature to claim that all 33 companies oppose tokenization for one common reason.

## Why Are Companies Objecting to Tokenized Shares?

The central issue is control over how a company's shares are represented and traded on a blockchain-based platform.

Tokenized stocks are digital tokens designed to represent securities or related financial interests. Depending on their structure, they may provide ownership rights, track a stock's price or give holders contractual exposure to an underlying asset.

These arrangements are not interchangeable. A token that represents actual shares with the corresponding legal rights is different from a product that merely follows a company's stock price.

For companies, the distinction matters because an outside platform could offer a tokenized version of their shares without the issuer directly sponsoring the product. Investors might then assume that the company approved or participated in the offering, even when that is not the case.

Other potential concerns include how tokenized trading affects price discovery, liquidity, volatility, shareholder communications and the relationship between blockchain-based markets and conventional exchanges.

One previously reported objector, Applied Optoelectronics, publicly raised concerns about uncertainty surrounding share prices, liquidity and volatility, as well as the possibility of investors confusing a third-party product with an issuer-backed offering.

However, that explanation should not be attributed to JPMorgan, AMD or the other newly listed companies without supporting evidence. Their notices do not, by themselves, establish why they objected.

## What Is OKXICE Trying to Build?

OKXICE is associated with cryptocurrency exchange OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange.

The proposed platform aims to make tokenized versions of U.S.-listed stocks available for blockchain-based trading. Its original plan named 63 stock tickers representing 62 companies, with Alphabet appearing under two share classes.

The proposed model has attracted attention because blockchain-based trading can potentially operate outside the conventional trading day. Depending on the platform's design and applicable controls, tokenized assets may also support programmable transactions and transfers between approved participants.

But continuous trading does not automatically guarantee continuous liquidity, accurate prices or instant settlement in every situation. Those outcomes depend on the market structure, counterparties, technology and rules governing the product.

The growing list of objections presents a practical challenge for OKXICE: the platform cannot simply assume that every company on its original list can be included under the regulatory pathway it plans to use.

## What Does the SEC Framework Say?

The U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission announced an innovation exemption framework for tokenized securities trading venues in September 2026. The framework provides a route for eligible venues to offer certain tokenized U.S. securities under defined conditions.

One important provision gives issuers an opportunity to object to the trading of tokenized versions of their shares on a covered venue.

Under the framework, a venue must provide the relevant issuer with notice. If the issuer submits a qualifying objection within the applicable period, the venue cannot offer that tokenized stock under the exemption.

This gives companies a formal mechanism to challenge third-party tokenized listings without requiring them to oppose blockchain technology in every context.

The distinction is important for interpreting the latest developments. An objection to OKXICE does not necessarily prevent a company from participating in a different tokenization project, working with a regulated financial institution or exploring blockchain-based services for its own operations.

**Official reference:** [SEC statement on the innovation exemption](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/atkins-innovation-exemption-bridge-toward-durable-rulemaking-091726).

## JPMorgan's Position Makes the Story More Complex

JPMorgan Chase stands out because the banking group has invested in blockchain-based financial infrastructure through its Kinexys business and has developed JPM Coin for institutional payment applications.

The bank's involvement in blockchain technology illustrates why opposition to a particular tokenized-stock venue should not be treated as a rejection of blockchain itself.

Banks can support permissioned blockchain networks, tokenized deposits and institutional digital payments while still having separate views about how third parties should issue or trade tokenized versions of publicly listed shares.

Tokenization covers several different activities, including digital representations of deposits, bonds, funds, stocks and other financial assets. Each can involve different legal rights, regulatory obligations and operational risks.

JPMorgan's reported objection therefore does not establish that it has abandoned its blockchain strategy. It concerns the proposed handling of its shares on the OKXICE venue, unless the bank provides a broader explanation.

## Does the $17 Trillion Figure Mean Tokenization Is Failing?

Not necessarily.

The approximately $17 trillion figure cited in the social-media discussion represents the estimated combined market value of the companies involved. It is not a measure of capital withdrawn from blockchain markets, losses caused by tokenization or the size of the tokenized-stock industry.

The growing objection count does demonstrate a meaningful challenge for platforms seeking to bring traditional equities onchain. Issuer participation, legal certainty and investor understanding can all influence whether a tokenized product gains credibility.

At the same time, the market includes multiple approaches. Some projects involve issuers or their authorized partners directly, while others seek to create tokenized representations through third-party arrangements.

The regulatory treatment and rights available to investors can differ between these models. That is why the number of companies objecting to one venue cannot, on its own, establish that the entire tokenization market is losing support.

## What Happens Next?

The next important developments will be whether OKXICE updates its list of eligible stocks, how it responds to the objections and whether more issuers use the same regulatory mechanism.

Companies may also clarify their positions publicly. Until then, it is important to distinguish between what the notices confirm and what market observers believe may be motivating the decisions.

For investors, the practical lesson is to examine the structure of any tokenized stock product before treating it as equivalent to a conventional share. Questions about ownership, voting rights, dividends, custody, redemption and regulatory protection remain essential.

The latest objections show that the future of tokenized equities will depend on more than blockchain technology. It will also depend on issuer rights, regulatory design and whether trading platforms can demonstrate that their products protect investors while working within established securities laws.

**The bottom line:** JPMorgan, AMD and five other major companies have added to the resistance facing OKXICE's proposed tokenized-stock platform. The reported 33-company total is a significant development for this initiative, but it does not mean that $17 trillion has left the market or that those companies have rejected every form of tokenization.
