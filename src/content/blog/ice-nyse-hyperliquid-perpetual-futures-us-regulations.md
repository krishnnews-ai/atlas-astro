A major traditional financial market operator is exploring a connection with one of the cryptocurrency industry's prominent decentralized trading ecosystems.

Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) has updated and reposted its partnership announcement with Trade[XYZ] after removing an earlier post. The revised wording places greater emphasis on the companies' intention to bring Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures to the U.S. market, while making clear that operating regulated U.S. versions remains subject to regulatory approvals.

The wording is important. ICE and Trade[XYZ] are planning a new market built on Hyperliquid technology, but the announcement does not mean that U.S. customers can already access an approved version of these products.

The proposed arrangement could connect traditional financial market infrastructure with blockchain-based derivatives trading. If approved, it could also create a new route for eligible U.S. traders to access perpetual contracts linked to cryptocurrencies and traditional financial assets.

## Why ICE's Updated Announcement Is Getting Attention

The announcement attracted attention after ICE removed its earlier social media post and subsequently published an updated version.

According to Wu Blockchain's report, the revised post specifically emphasizes two points: the companies **"plan to bring"** Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures to the United States, and the operation of **"U.S. regulated versions"** remains **"subject to regulatory approvals."**

That language separates the proposed business plan from the regulatory status of the products.

The companies are not announcing that all approvals have been completed. Instead, they are describing an initiative that requires further regulatory work before a U.S.-regulated market can begin operating.

The change also does not establish that regulators have rejected the proposal or that the partnership has been cancelled. The available announcement describes an intended project whose launch depends on obtaining the necessary permissions.

For the cryptocurrency industry, the development is significant because ICE is not an ordinary crypto startup. It operates major financial market infrastructure, including the New York Stock Exchange.

## What Are ICE and Trade[XYZ] Planning to Build?

The partnership aims to establish a U.S.-regulated market for perpetual futures using Hyperliquid-based infrastructure.

The proposed structure brings together three different areas of expertise.

**ICE** is expected to operate the regulated market and establish dedicated exchange and clearing infrastructure.

**Trade[XYZ]** brings experience in building perpetual markets, including contracts linked to real-world assets.

**Hyperliquid** provides the underlying blockchain-based trading infrastructure on which the proposed markets are intended to be built.

This division of responsibilities is important. The partnership does not mean that ICE is acquiring or taking control of the entire Hyperliquid network. It is a proposed arrangement to use Hyperliquid-based technology within a separately structured U.S. market.

ICE has also announced a strategic investment in Trade[XYZ], connecting the partnership to a broader effort to develop retail-focused perpetual markets.

The planned business is intended to operate separately from ICE's existing institutional futures and clearing operations.

## What Are Perpetual Futures and Why Do Traders Use Them?

Perpetual futures, commonly known as perps, are derivatives contracts that do not have a fixed expiration date.

Unlike traditional futures contracts, which generally expire or settle on a specified date, perpetual contracts are designed to remain open indefinitely, subject to the platform's rules and the trader's margin requirements.

These contracts allow traders to speculate on the price movements of an asset without directly owning the underlying asset.

For example, a trader could open a position linked to the price of Bitcoin, a stock or a commodity without purchasing that asset itself.

Perpetual markets commonly use funding payments between long and short positions to help keep contract prices aligned with the underlying reference price.

Leverage is another important feature. It allows traders to control a larger position with a smaller amount of initial capital, but it also increases the risk of losses and liquidation.

This is one reason the proposed U.S. market needs an appropriate regulatory structure. Offering leveraged derivatives to customers involves more than simply providing access to a trading interface.

## Hyperliquid Could Bring More Than Crypto Trading to the Proposed Market

One of the most interesting aspects of the ICE and Trade[XYZ] arrangement is its potential asset coverage.

The proposed market is not limited to cryptocurrency contracts. The partnership has discussed perpetual products linked to several financial asset categories, including:

- **Cryptocurrencies:** Contracts referencing digital assets such as Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies.
- **Individual equities:** Price exposure linked to publicly traded company shares.
- **Equity indices:** Contracts connected to broader stock-market benchmarks.
- **Commodities:** Derivatives referencing commodity markets.
- **Foreign exchange:** Contracts linked to currency pairs.
- **Other real-world assets:** Additional markets that could be considered as the product offering develops.

The proposed platform is also intended to support around-the-clock trading.

Traditional stock exchanges generally follow established market hours, while cryptocurrency markets operate continuously. A regulated perpetual market offering extended trading access could therefore appeal to traders seeking more flexibility.

However, the proposed asset categories should not be confused with a confirmed list of U.S.-approved contracts. The final products and their availability will depend on regulatory permissions and the market's eventual operating structure.

## How Hyperliquid Fits Into the Wall Street Partnership

Hyperliquid has developed blockchain infrastructure focused on on-chain trading, particularly perpetual derivatives.

Its ecosystem has attracted attention because it allows users to access markets through decentralized trading infrastructure rather than relying exclusively on conventional centralized exchanges.

Trade[XYZ] has built perpetual markets referencing real-world assets on Hyperliquid. The proposed ICE partnership would explore bringing this type of market into a U.S.-regulated environment.

The arrangement could combine the technological flexibility of blockchain-based markets with the exchange and clearing expertise of a traditional financial operator.

That combination is particularly important for derivatives trading. Market access, transaction execution, risk management, clearing and customer protections all need to be addressed when a product is offered within a regulated financial system.

The proposed structure therefore represents a potential bridge between two different approaches to trading infrastructure.

It does not mean that existing decentralized Hyperliquid markets automatically become regulated U.S. products. The proposed U.S. venue would require its own operating arrangements and approvals.

## Could OKX Help Bring These Products to U.S. Traders?

Distribution is another important part of the proposed business.

ICE has identified its joint venture with OKX as an expected route for distributing the planned products through participating brokers and platforms.

The idea is to make the proposed markets accessible through established distribution channels rather than relying only on a standalone trading service.

However, this arrangement also involves regulatory requirements.

ICE has indicated that distribution through its OKX joint venture depends on separate approvals for the relevant U.S. broker-dealer and Futures Commission Merchant entities.

That means the distribution plan is also conditional on regulatory review. The proposed market and its distribution channels would both need to satisfy the applicable requirements before they could operate for U.S. customers.
