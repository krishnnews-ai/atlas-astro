**By CoinAINews Editorial Team**

**Published: October 17, 2026**

Bitget has tightened its compliance controls for cryptocurrency deposits and withdrawals involving a list of 20 named entities, warning that direct transfers connected to those entities may be rejected and the associated accounts terminated.

The latest notice marks a stricter approach than the exchange's earlier measures introduced in August 2026. The updated list includes cryptocurrency platforms, payment-related services and other entities, including HTX (Huobi Global SA), EXMO Ltd, Tokenspot CJSC, BitBank3 and services associated with Xeltox Enterprises.

Bitget's policy also extends beyond transactions sent directly to or received from a named platform. The exchange says it may review the source of funds, sending and receiving addresses, and intermediary services involved in a transaction. Accounts undergoing compliance reviews may face temporary restrictions on deposits, withdrawals, trading or other services.

The update is important for cryptocurrency users who transfer digital assets between exchanges, payment providers and other intermediaries. A transfer's compliance status may depend on the wider transaction path, not just the platform displayed in a user's wallet history.

According to the exchange's published guidance and reporting on the October update, the list may also change as regulatory requirements, sanctions information and internal risk assessments evolve.

## What Has Changed in Bitget's Latest Compliance Policy?

Bitget introduced additional controls for transactions involving named entities in August. Its original English-language announcement outlined a phased implementation, with measures taking effect on August 7, August 13 and August 23, 2026.

Under that earlier policy, transactions involving the listed entities could undergo enhanced compliance reviews, be rejected or result in temporary account restrictions. Violations of the platform's terms could potentially lead to account termination.

The October update goes further by explicitly warning that deposits and withdrawals directly involving the expanded list will be rejected and the relevant accounts terminated.

The updated approach also maintains scrutiny of indirect connections. If a transaction's funding source, destination address or intermediary service is associated with a listed entity, Bitget may conduct further checks or reject the transaction. Temporary restrictions may apply while the review is in progress.

The distinction matters because the exchange does not describe every indirect connection as an automatic account-termination event. For indirect cases, termination may follow if the activity violates its terms of service.

Bitget has also stated that its list is neither exhaustive nor fixed. Users should therefore check the latest official announcement before initiating transfers.

## Why Are HTX and EXMO Relevant to Bitget Users?

HTX and EXMO are among the better-known cryptocurrency trading platforms identified in Bitget's earlier compliance announcement.

For users who move assets between exchanges, the announcement highlights the importance of checking both the immediate sending platform and the wider route through which funds have travelled.

For example, a user may withdraw cryptocurrency from one service, move it through an intermediary and then deposit it into Bitget. If the transaction path is associated with a listed entity, the deposit could trigger additional compliance checks.

That does not mean every transaction involving HTX or EXMO will necessarily be blocked in every context. The applicable outcome depends on Bitget's controls, the transaction details and the circumstances of the review.

Users should not assume that transferring assets through another wallet automatically removes any compliance concerns. A wallet address alone may not establish the full source of funds or the parties involved in a transaction.

## What Is the Significance of Tokenspot, Cryptomus and Heleket?

The expanded list also identifies Tokenspot CJSC and Xeltox Enterprises, with separate references to Cryptomus.com and Heleket.com.

These additions are relevant because crypto transactions increasingly involve multiple services, including exchanges, payment processors and intermediary providers. Compliance checks can therefore extend beyond the exchange where a user initially purchased an asset.

However, users should distinguish between Bitget's decision to restrict transactions involving a named entity and the legal status of that entity under a particular jurisdiction's sanctions regime.

For users, the practical point is straightforward: verify the current restrictions and avoid assuming that a service remains acceptable simply because a transfer worked previously.

## Can Bitget Freeze or Terminate a User's Account?

Bitget's published policy describes several possible outcomes when a transaction is associated with a listed entity.

**1. Rejection of a direct transaction**

Deposits and withdrawals directly involving the listed entities may be rejected under the updated controls. Bitget's October policy warning also specifies account termination for the direct-transfer cases it describes.

**2. Temporary account restrictions during a review**

When a transaction has a potentially relevant indirect connection, Bitget may investigate the source of funds, sending address, receiving address or intermediary service. During this process, deposits, withdrawals, trading and other account functions may be restricted.

**3. Possible termination for a terms-of-service violation**

Indirect exposure does not, according to the available description of the policy, automatically mean that every affected account will be terminated. However, Bitget warns that activity violating its terms may lead to account closure.

**4. Further compliance checks**

The exchange may request additional information or direct affected users to its customer support team. Users should respond through official channels and provide genuine transaction records when requested.

These measures can affect access to exchange services, so users should take transaction warnings seriously rather than repeatedly attempting transfers that have already been rejected.

## What Should Bitget Users Do Before Depositing or Withdrawing Crypto?

Users who regularly move cryptocurrency between exchanges should take a few practical precautions.

- **Review the current policy:** Check Bitget's official support announcement before sending funds.
- **Verify the sending service:** Confirm the platform or provider from which the assets originate.
- **Check intermediary services:** Identify any third-party exchange, payment provider or other service involved in the transfer.
- **Keep transaction records:** Retain transaction IDs, wallet addresses, withdrawal confirmations and relevant account statements.
- **Do not attempt to disguise the transaction path:** Moving assets through additional wallets does not guarantee that a compliance review will be avoided.
- **Contact official support if a transfer is flagged:** Ask which information is required to resolve the review and follow the exchange's documented process.
- **Avoid relying on old information:** Bitget says the list may change without individual notifications.

Users should not interpret these steps as a guarantee that a transfer will be accepted. The exchange retains its compliance controls, and the outcome may depend on the transaction's specific circumstances.

## Is Bitget Itself Sanctioned?

The announcement concerns Bitget's controls on transactions connected to the entities it has named. It should not be read as evidence that Bitget itself has been sanctioned.

Likewise, the appearance of an exchange or service on Bitget's list does not establish that every customer, wallet address or transaction associated with that service is subject to a government-imposed asset freeze.

There is an important distinction between a private cryptocurrency platform's internal compliance policy and an official sanctions designation issued by a government authority.

Users seeking to establish the legal status of a specific business should consult the relevant regulator's current sanctions records and official notices. The rules that apply can differ by jurisdiction.

## Why Crypto Exchanges Are Tightening Transaction Screening

Cryptocurrency exchanges operate across jurisdictions with different financial-crime, sanctions and customer-verification requirements. This creates a need to assess not only the immediate counterparty to a transaction but also potentially relevant connections to other services.

Digital-asset transfers can pass through multiple wallets, exchanges and payment providers before reaching their destination. That complexity can make transaction screening more demanding than checking a single sending address.

Bitget's update illustrates how an exchange can respond by expanding the entities covered by its internal controls and applying restrictions to transfers linked to those entities.

For customers, the consequences can include rejected transactions, requests for additional information and temporary interruptions to exchange services. For the wider industry, these controls highlight the growing importance of transaction provenance and compliance procedures.

However, the announcement alone does not establish the scale of any financial impact, the number of affected users or whether the policy will materially change trading volumes on the named platforms. Those outcomes would require additional evidence.

## What Happens Next?

Bitget says its list may be updated in response to changes in sanctions information, regulatory requirements, risk assessments and internal compliance policies.

Users should therefore treat the published list as a current reference rather than a permanent or exhaustive record of every restricted entity.

Any future changes should be verified through Bitget's official support centre. Customers experiencing rejected transfers or account restrictions should contact the exchange directly rather than relying on unofficial social media claims about the status of their funds.

For cryptocurrency users, the key takeaway is that a transfer's route and associated service providers can matter just as much as the asset being transferred. Checking the current rules before moving funds may help users avoid preventable transaction problems, although it cannot guarantee acceptance.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

### 1. Why is Bitget restricting deposits and withdrawals involving 20 entities?

Bitget says it is strengthening sanctions-related and regulatory compliance controls. Its policy allows it to reject transactions associated with the listed entities and apply account restrictions or termination in the circumstances described in its notice.

### 2. Which major cryptocurrency platforms appear on the list?

The named entities include HTX (Huobi Global SA) and EXMO Ltd, along with other exchanges, trading services and payment-related entities.

### 3. Does a transfer involving a listed entity always result in account termination?

Bitget's updated warning specifies rejection and account termination for direct transfers covered by its policy. Indirect connections may trigger additional review, rejection or temporary restrictions, and may lead to termination if the activity violates its terms. Users should consult the current official notice for the precise conditions.

### 4. Can Bitget restrict trading while investigating a deposit?

Yes. Bitget says account functions, including deposits, withdrawals and trading, may be temporarily restricted during a compliance review.

### 5. Does the list prove that every named entity is officially sanctioned?

No. Inclusion in Bitget's internal transaction-control list does not, by itself, prove that every entity is designated under every government's sanctions regime. Official legal designations must be verified separately.

### 6. Can the list change?

Yes. Bitget says the list is not exhaustive or fixed and may change as regulations, sanctions information and risk assessments evolve.

### 7. What should users do if their deposit or withdrawal is flagged?

Users should contact Bitget through its official support channels, retain their transaction records and provide requested documentation. They should avoid trying to bypass the review by routing the same funds through additional services.
