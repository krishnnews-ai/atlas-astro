The cryptocurrency industry has spent years competing over trading volume, new products, faster transactions and global market share. But Ethereum co-founder Vitalik Buterin believes that the priorities of major crypto exchanges need to change.

In an exclusive interview with Moneycontrol published on November 4, Buterin said the next two years should be about survival rather than winning. His warning comes as major security incidents continue to expose weaknesses across the crypto industry.

The concern is not limited to smart contracts. According to Buterin, recent large-scale attacks have increasingly focused on user interfaces and centralized components — parts of the infrastructure that connect users to blockchain services.

That distinction matters. An exchange can operate on top of sophisticated blockchain technology and still expose customers to serious risks if its websites, internal systems, authentication processes or other centralized services are compromised.

For exchanges, custodians and crypto institutions, the message is becoming difficult to ignore: growth means little if the infrastructure holding customers' assets cannot withstand an attack.

---

## Vitalik Buterin Wants Exchanges to Enter "Bunker Mode"

Buterin described a shift in how crypto businesses should approach the coming years. Instead of treating market expansion and competition as their main priorities, exchanges and custodians need to focus on protecting their systems and customer assets.

He referred to this defensive mindset as "bunker mode."

The idea is straightforward: companies should strengthen security across their entire operations, reduce unnecessary exposure and make resilience a central business priority.

This is particularly relevant because the security of a blockchain protocol and the security of a crypto platform are not necessarily the same thing.

A smart contract may be carefully audited, but users still interact with exchanges through websites, applications, wallets and other services. If one of those components is compromised, attackers may find a way to steal funds or gain access without exploiting the underlying blockchain itself.

Buterin said smart contract hacks have declined significantly, while many of the major attacks over roughly the past 18 months have been concentrated in user-interface layers and centralized components.

His comments suggest that the industry's security challenge is evolving. Improving smart contract code remains important, but it is no longer enough on its own.

---

## Why Crypto Exchange Security Is Becoming a Bigger Concern

Centralized exchanges occupy an important position in the digital asset market. They provide trading services, custody, account management and access to crypto markets for millions of users.

That concentration also creates risks.

A compromised internal system or user-facing service can potentially affect large numbers of customers. Unlike a vulnerability in a single decentralized application, a weakness in a major centralized platform may expose a much broader pool of assets and user information.

The distinction between blockchain security and platform security is therefore critical.

For example, a user may believe that funds are protected because the exchange supports a secure blockchain network. But the process of logging in, approving withdrawals, managing accounts and communicating with the platform can involve additional systems outside that blockchain.

Those systems also need strong protection.

Buterin expects major exchanges, custodians and institutions to take the security-first approach more seriously. He specifically said he expects Binance and other major industry players to move towards this kind of defensive operating model.

His comments were not an announcement of a new security rule or a prediction that particular exchanges will fail. They were a warning about the priorities the industry should adopt.

## Could More Crypto Hacks Bring Stricter Regulations?

Security failures may also create consequences beyond financial losses.

Buterin warned that if hacking continues to be a serious problem, regulators could begin placing greater responsibility on companies to operate more conservatively.

In his view, the industry may face growing pressure to demonstrate that it has taken reasonable steps to protect customer funds and prevent attacks.

This could increase scrutiny of exchange security practices, custody arrangements and internal controls.

His comments also raise a difficult question for crypto businesses: how much responsibility should a company carry when attackers compromise its systems and steal customer assets?

Regulatory approaches differ across jurisdictions, and Buterin did not announce a specific regulatory proposal. However, his warning highlights how repeated security incidents could influence future oversight.

For exchanges, stronger security may increasingly become more than a technical advantage. It could become essential to maintaining customer confidence and meeting regulatory expectations.

## Ethereum's Next Roadmap Will Combine ZK Proofs, AI and Formal Verification

Buterin's comments were not limited to exchange security. He also discussed Ethereum's longer-term development and the role of new technologies in its Lean Ethereum roadmap.

The roadmap aims to make Ethereum more capable and performant while preserving its decentralization and security.

Three technologies are particularly important to this effort:

- **Zero-knowledge proofs:** These cryptographic techniques can help verify information or computations without revealing all the underlying data.
- **Artificial intelligence:** AI could help developers accelerate complex engineering work and examine large amounts of code.
- **Formal verification:** This approach uses mathematical methods to check whether software behaves according to specified rules.

Buterin said the combination of AI and formal verification could significantly accelerate Ethereum's development.

He suggested that work which might have taken around 20 years by the standards of five years ago could now progress much faster with the help of AI.

However, the role of formal verification is especially important. Faster development does not automatically mean safer software. AI can assist with writing and reviewing code, while formal verification can help establish whether specific properties of that code hold under defined conditions.

The broader objective is to avoid a situation where faster development introduces new security weaknesses.

## Vitalik Says Layer 2 Networks Need More Than Faster Transactions

Buterin also challenged the direction of some Layer 2 blockchain projects.

Layer 2 networks are designed to operate alongside a base blockchain such as Ethereum, helping applications handle activity more efficiently. But Buterin argued that simply copying Ethereum and offering another scaling option is not enough to make a project interesting.

Instead, Layer 2 networks should build capabilities that Ethereum does not provide directly.

He highlighted several areas where these networks could develop differentiated applications.

**High-performance DeFi:** Platforms could focus on financial applications that require faster execution and specialized trading infrastructure. Buterin pointed to Lighter as an example of high-performance DeFi.

**Privacy-focused applications:** Privacy protocols such as Aztec and InvalX were mentioned in the interview as examples of projects exploring capabilities beyond basic scaling.

**Enterprise applications:** Businesses could use blockchain infrastructure to manage records involving supply chains, healthcare and manufacturing. Existing business systems could remain in place while blockchain-based proofs or data records help verify that information has been updated according to established rules.

The direction is clear: Layer 2 projects need a distinct purpose rather than competing only on transaction speed or fees.

For the Ethereum ecosystem, that could mean a broader range of specialized applications built around performance, privacy and enterprise requirements.

---

## What Buterin's Warning Means for the Crypto Industry

The interview brings together two developments that are likely to shape the industry's next phase: stronger security expectations and a more specialized blockchain ecosystem.

For centralized exchanges, the immediate challenge is protecting the systems that customers interact with every day. That includes interfaces, internal infrastructure, custody arrangements and the processes used to authorize transactions.

For Ethereum developers, the challenge is different but connected. New technologies such as AI and zero-knowledge proofs may help improve the network, but security and decentralization must remain central to the development process.

Layer 2 networks also face pressure to demonstrate why their products are necessary. Offering another version of Ethereum scaling may not be enough if users and developers are looking for specialized capabilities.

These changes could influence how crypto companies allocate resources. Security teams, infrastructure improvements and verification processes may become more important than aggressive expansion alone.

---

## The Bottom Line

Vitalik Buterin's message is a warning about priorities, not a prediction that the crypto exchange industry is about to collapse.

The next two years could test whether major platforms are prepared to defend themselves against increasingly sophisticated attacks. Exchanges that focus only on growth while overlooking vulnerabilities in centralized systems may find that competition is not their biggest problem.

At the same time, Ethereum's roadmap points towards a future in which AI, zero-knowledge proofs and formal verification play a larger role in blockchain development.

The industry's next stage may not be decided simply by who processes the most transactions or attracts the most users. It could depend on who can build systems that remain secure when they are under attack.

**For crypto exchanges, survival may be the most important competitive advantage of all.**
