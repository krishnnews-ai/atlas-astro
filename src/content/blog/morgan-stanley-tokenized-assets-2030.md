Morgan Stanley has issued a bold projection for the tokenized asset market, forecasting that the sector will expand to between $3 trillion and $10 trillion by 2030. The prediction, delivered by Amy Oldenburg, the bank's Head of Digital Asset Strategy, underscores a growing conviction among major financial institutions that blockchain-based asset representation is moving from experimentation to core infrastructure.

The upper end of Morgan Stanley's range aligns closely with other institutional forecasts. Citi Institute has projected a $5.5 trillion tokenized asset market by 2030, while the broader range of analyst estimates continues to converge around the multi-trillion-dollar mark within the next five years.

## The Infrastructure Shift

Oldenburg's forecast comes as Morgan Stanley itself prepares to operationalize tokenized asset trading. The bank now expects to begin supporting tokenized equities on its Alternative Trading System (ATS) in Q1 2027, a revision from its earlier target of the second half of 2026. The delay, according to Oldenburg, reflects a pragmatic sequencing: "We need a product in order to be able to support it within the ATS. And there really isn't any client demand because the product itself doesn't exist."

The bank is simultaneously developing wallet infrastructure for custodying tokenized assets and has established a Digital Asset Lab to test blockchain applications before commercial rollout. Oldenburg identified private equities, structured notes, and access to private funds as particularly promising use cases, where blockchain could reduce paperwork and administrative costs.

Morgan Stanley's institutional research arm has separately emphasized that digital asset rails are expected to reshape the infrastructure supporting global financial markets over the coming decades. The firm's analysts note that financial institutions building tokenized infrastructure and trusted client gateways could defend or expand market share as client demand evolves.

## Market Infrastructure Convergence

The revision in Citi's forecast from $4–5 trillion to $5.5 trillion was directly linked to major market infrastructure providers moving beyond experimentation. The Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation (DTCC), NYSE, and Nasdaq are embedding tokenization into core issuance, trading, and settlement workflows.

The DTCC's planned support for a broader range of tokenized products, expected to begin in October, has been identified by Morgan Stanley as a critical milestone for market development. The convergence of regulated market infrastructure with tokenization capabilities represents a structural shift rather than a speculative trend.

## What the Data Actually Shows

Current market reality presents a more modest picture. The IMF's October 2026 Global Financial Stability Report valued publicly reported tokenized real-world assets at approximately **$65 billion** as of July 2026, excluding repurchase agreements and stablecoins. Tokenized credit accounted for $30.4 billion, money market funds for $17.5 billion, and tokenized equities for roughly $2.3 billion.

Tokenized repurchase agreements have scaled faster, with 30-day average daily volume reaching $303 billion. However, the IMF characterized tokenized markets as remaining "very small and fragmented" relative to traditional counterparts, noting that the US repo market alone trades about $13 trillion daily.

The IMF identified four interdependent constraints on growth: legal certainty, regulatory clarity, interoperability, and safe, sound, and scalable settlement. The organization urged a technology-neutral approach built on the principle of "same activity, same risk, same regulatory outcome."

## The Institutional FOMO Dynamic

Oldenburg described an observable fear of missing out among institutional players, with attention and participation interest rising noticeably over the past 12 months. This sentiment, she suggested, is mutually reinforcing within the industry.

The IMF's analysis offered early evidence that investors value certain tokenization features. More than 50% of tokenized equity trading occurs outside traditional US market hours, and approximately 80% of trades executed on-chain were for less than one share, indicating retail demand for fractional ownership. Traditional assets absorbed between 87% and 99% of overnight price changes seen in tokenized equities, suggesting that on-chain and traditional markets respond to similar information.

However, the IMF also warned that scaling tokenization could amplify traditional financial risks such as fire sales, liquidity runs, and contagion through greater interconnectedness and leverage. Features that reduce friction—including atomic settlement and continuous trading—also remove buffers that absorb shocks. For now, the fund assessed systemic risks as limited.

## The Path Forward

The gap between current market size and Morgan Stanley's 2030 projection implies a compound annual growth rate that would require sustained institutional adoption, regulatory clarity, and infrastructure maturation. The bank's own product timeline—with tokenized equity trading on its ATS not arriving until 2027—illustrates the measured pace at which even committed institutions are moving.

Oldenburg framed the opportunity in expansive terms, describing a future direction of "securitization of everything" as tokenization becomes more widespread and questions arise about using such assets as collateral. Whether that vision materializes at the $3 trillion floor or the $10 trillion ceiling of Morgan Stanley's range will depend on how quickly the identified constraints—legal certainty, regulatory clarity, interoperability, and settlement infrastructure—are resolved.
