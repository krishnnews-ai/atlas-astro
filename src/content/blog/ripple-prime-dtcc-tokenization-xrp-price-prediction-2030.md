Ripple is gaining attention in institutional finance as Ripple Prime appears among the firms participating in the Depository Trust & Clearing Corporation's (DTCC) tokenization working group. The development comes as DTCC prepares to launch a new service designed to bring eligible traditional securities onto blockchain infrastructure, connecting established financial markets with digital-asset technology.

The initiative has drawn attention because DTCC's depository holds securities valued at more than $114 trillion. Meanwhile, Standard Chartered's long-term outlook for XRP has kept the cryptocurrency in the spotlight, with the bank's analyst Geoffrey Kendrick maintaining a reported price target of $28 by 2030.

Together, the developments highlight two important themes in the digital-asset market: the adoption of tokenization by traditional financial institutions and the debate over whether growing blockchain activity can translate into greater demand for cryptocurrencies such as XRP.

However, institutional participation in tokenization does not automatically create demand for XRP, and a long-term price target remains a forecast rather than a guaranteed outcome.

## Ripple Prime Is Part of DTCC's Tokenization Working Group

DTCC announced its tokenization initiative in May 2026, outlining plans to introduce a service through its subsidiary, The Depository Trust Company (DTC). The company said it intended to begin limited production transactions in July before launching the service in October 2026.

In its official announcement, DTCC listed Ripple Prime among more than 50 financial and technology firms participating in its industry working group. The group includes major banks, asset managers, exchanges, custodians and digital-asset infrastructure providers.

The participation is significant because it places Ripple Prime among organizations contributing to discussions around the infrastructure needed to support tokenized securities.

DTCC's service is designed to represent eligible securities held in its existing depository through blockchain-based tokens while preserving the associated entitlements, investor protections and ownership rights. The initial eligible assets include certain Russell 1000 equities, major index-tracking exchange-traded funds and US Treasury bills, bonds and notes.

Rather than replacing traditional financial markets overnight, the initiative aims to connect existing securities infrastructure with blockchain-based systems.

For institutional investors, tokenization could help improve the movement of assets, support collateral management and create more flexible ways to transfer securities between approved participants.

The October timeline is a planned launch window announced by DTCC. It should not be interpreted as confirmation that every component of the service is already available to all investors.

## Why DTCC's $114 Trillion Figure Matters

DTCC's scale helps explain why its tokenization plans have attracted attention across the financial industry.

According to its May announcement, DTC held securities valued at more than $114 trillion. That figure represents the value of securities held in custody, not the amount of money committed to blockchain transactions or the value of assets already tokenized.

The distinction is important. Tokenizing a portion of existing securities does not automatically create an equivalent amount of new investment demand.

Instead, the opportunity lies in how the infrastructure may change the way eligible assets are represented, transferred and managed.

Traditional securities transactions often involve multiple intermediaries, recordkeeping systems and settlement processes. Tokenization can potentially introduce shared digital records and more flexible transfer mechanisms, although regulatory requirements and existing market safeguards remain important.

DTCC's approach is designed to preserve traditional ownership rights and investor protections while enabling approved participants to use tokenized representations of eligible securities.

If the service gains adoption, it could become part of a broader shift toward blockchain-based financial infrastructure. The pace of adoption will depend on operational readiness, participating institutions, supported blockchain networks and the practical benefits delivered to users.

## Does Ripple Prime's Participation Mean XRP Will Benefit?

This is the central question for XRP investors.

Ripple Prime's inclusion in DTCC's working group strengthens the connection between Ripple's institutional business and the wider tokenization movement. However, Ripple Prime's participation should not be confused with a confirmed agreement to use XRP as the settlement asset for DTCC's tokenized securities.

Institutional tokenization can operate through different technical arrangements. Some systems may use permissioned networks, tokenized deposits or conventional settlement assets, while others may integrate public blockchains and digital currencies.

The economic impact on XRP depends on the specific infrastructure selected and the role the token plays within it.

If a tokenization application uses the XRP Ledger, that could create activity on the network. Whether this activity translates into meaningful XRP demand depends on the application design, transaction requirements and the extent to which XRP is actually used.

By contrast, a tokenized securities service that uses a different settlement asset or operates through a separate network may deliver limited direct benefits to XRP holders.

Investors should therefore distinguish between three separate developments: Ripple's participation in institutional finance, adoption of the XRP Ledger and actual demand for the XRP token.

These developments can be related, but they are not interchangeable.

## Standard Chartered's $28 XRP Forecast for 2030

Standard Chartered has emerged as one of the major financial institutions issuing long-term projections for XRP.

Reports published in 2026 have attributed a $28 target for 2030 to Geoffrey Kendrick, the bank's digital-asset research analyst. The forecast reflects a bullish long-term view of XRP's potential role in digital payments and financial infrastructure.

The target is ambitious and depends on substantial growth in XRP's market value over the coming years. It should be understood as an analyst projection rather than a promise from the bank that XRP will reach that level.

The bank's near-term outlook has also changed during 2026. Reporting in March described a reduction in its 2026 XRP target to $2.80, while the longer-term target remained at $28 for 2030.

This illustrates the difference between a long-term investment thesis and expectations for the immediate market environment. An analyst can remain optimistic about an asset's potential over several years while acknowledging that short-term prices may face volatility, weaker demand or changing macroeconomic conditions.

For XRP to approach the $28 level, it would likely need a combination of sustained market demand, greater adoption of relevant payment and financial applications, supportive market conditions and continued investor confidence.

Institutional partnerships may contribute to that narrative, but partnerships alone are not enough to establish the forecast as achievable.

## How Tokenization Could Change XRP's Long-Term Outlook

Tokenization is increasingly being explored by banks, asset managers, payment providers and securities-market infrastructure companies.

The basic idea is to represent an asset or its ownership rights digitally, allowing certain processes to be handled through blockchain infrastructure. Depending on the implementation, this could support transfers, recordkeeping, collateral management or settlement workflows.

Ripple has pursued institutional blockchain initiatives in several markets. On September 29, 2026, the company announced a partnership with Brazil's CSD BR involving the XRP Ledger and the recording and auditing of financial assets. The initial phase involves investment-fund shares, with the central securities depository's systems remaining the official source of record.

That announcement provides a concrete example of institutional interest in blockchain-based asset records. However, it does not establish that all tokenized assets involved will require XRP for settlement.

For XRP to capture a meaningful share of the economic value created by tokenization, the connection between institutional use and token demand needs to be clear.

Investors should look for evidence of production deployments, transaction activity, the settlement mechanisms actually used and any explicit role assigned to XRP.

Without that information, it is difficult to quantify the potential impact on the token's market value.

## The Difference Between Ripple's Business and XRP's Price

Ripple is a company that develops blockchain-based financial products and services. XRP is a digital asset that operates on the XRP Ledger.

Although the company and the token are closely associated, their commercial developments do not necessarily produce proportional changes in XRP's price.

A financial institution can use Ripple's software or institutional services without necessarily purchasing XRP as a reserve asset. Likewise, a blockchain application can record tokenized assets without requiring the native token to be used in every transaction.

This distinction is important when evaluating institutional announcements.

Ripple Prime's inclusion in DTCC's working group is evidence of participation in an industry initiative. It is not, by itself, evidence that DTCC has selected XRP as its settlement currency or that the token will capture a specific share of the initiative's economic value.

A stronger case for direct XRP demand would require clearer evidence about the role of XRP in actual transactions, settlement flows or liquidity requirements.

## What Could Support XRP Through 2030?

Several factors could influence XRP's long-term outlook.

**Institutional adoption:** Further deployment of blockchain-based financial services could expand the market for digital-asset infrastructure. The effect on XRP would depend on whether applications use the token directly.

**Payment and settlement demand:** Increased use of XRP in cross-border payments or other settlement processes could create additional demand, although actual usage must be distinguished from announcements and pilot projects.

**Regulatory clarity:** Clearer rules governing digital assets could support institutional participation, but regulatory outcomes and their market effects remain uncertain.

**Market liquidity and investor demand:** Exchange liquidity, investment-product flows, macroeconomic conditions and overall cryptocurrency sentiment will continue to influence XRP's price.

**Competition:** XRP faces competition from other digital assets, stablecoins, tokenized bank deposits and institutional settlement networks. The success of tokenization as a technology does not guarantee that one cryptocurrency will dominate it.

These factors will matter more than any single headline when evaluating whether the $28 forecast remains plausible over the longer term.

## Key Risks XRP Investors Should Understand

The bullish case has several limitations.

First, tokenization growth may not translate directly into XRP demand. Institutions can adopt blockchain infrastructure while using other assets for settlement.

Second, DTCC's $114 trillion custody figure represents existing securities held in its depository. It is not a $114 trillion tokenization market forecast.

Third, the planned launch of DTCC's service does not guarantee rapid adoption. Institutions must evaluate integration costs, operational risks, regulatory requirements and compatibility with existing systems.

Finally, XRP remains a volatile cryptocurrency. Its price can be affected by broader market conditions, investor sentiment, liquidity, regulatory developments and competition, regardless of progress in institutional tokenization.

The $28 target should therefore be treated as one long-term scenario rather than an investment outcome that investors can rely on.
