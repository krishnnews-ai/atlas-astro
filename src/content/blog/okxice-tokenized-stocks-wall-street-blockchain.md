What if you could trade shares of Apple, Nvidia, Amazon and Microsoft directly through a crypto platform, even outside traditional stock-market hours?

OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange, want to make that possible. Their joint venture, OKXICE, is launching a blockchain-based platform for tokenized securities on November 23.

But there is a major problem.

Out of the 62 companies originally selected for the platform, **56 have objected to having their shares tokenized through OKXICE**. That represents roughly 90% of the original lineup.

And the list includes some of the biggest names in global business.

## Why Are So Many Companies Saying No?

The main concern is how their shares would behave in a blockchain-based trading environment.

Tokenized stocks could potentially trade around the clock, rather than following conventional stock-market hours. That raises questions about liquidity, price differences, shareholder communication and investor protection.

However, there is an important distinction: these companies have objected to their shares being included through this particular OKXICE arrangement. It does not necessarily mean they oppose blockchain tokenization altogether.

The public notices also do not provide individual explanations from most of the companies.

---

## OKX and ICE Are Moving Ahead Anyway

Despite the objections, OKXICE is proceeding with its launch.

The platform expects to offer more than 70 assets, including securities linked to Coinbase, Circle, Robinhood, Strategy, BitGo, Securitize and Better.com, alongside 59 Direxion exchange-traded funds.

Securitize will provide the tokenization infrastructure. Each token is designed to represent a security entitlement backed one-to-one by an underlying US share, with applicable rights such as dividends and voting.

Initially, access is limited to eligible US-based OKX customers who complete the required verification and onboarding.

## Could Tokenized Stocks Change the Future of Wall Street?

The attraction is easy to understand. Investors could potentially access securities outside conventional trading hours, while blockchain infrastructure could offer a different way to settle and hold financial assets.

But the objections show that traditional companies are not ready to accept every new trading model.

For OKX and ICE, launching the technology may be the easy part. Convincing major corporations and investors to trust it could be much harder.

**The biggest question now is whether tokenized stocks can become the next major chapter of Wall Street — even when 90% of the original companies wanted no part of this platform.**

## The Bottom Line

OKX and ICE are launching OKXICE, a blockchain-based platform for tokenized stocks, on November 23.

But 56 out of 62 major companies — including Apple, Nvidia, Amazon, Microsoft, and Tesla — have objected to having their shares tokenized through this platform.

Despite the backlash, OKXICE is moving forward with over 70 assets, including Coinbase, Circle, and Robinhood.

The big question: Can tokenized stocks succeed when 90% of the original companies want no part of it?
