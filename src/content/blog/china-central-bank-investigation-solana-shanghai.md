China's cryptocurrency crackdown has taken a new turn, and this time Solana is at the centre of attention.

The People's Bank of China (PBOC) has confirmed that authorities are investigating companies and individuals involved in promoting Solana in Shanghai after the blockchain project carried out advertising activities in the city's financial district.

The development adds another layer of uncertainty for cryptocurrency businesses seeking a presence in mainland China, where regulators continue to maintain strict restrictions on virtual currency-related financial activities.

According to a report by Financial Times cited by crypto.news, the central bank said Solana's promotional campaign involved domestic entities and amounted to cryptocurrency marketing, an activity prohibited under China's existing regulatory framework.

Authorities have already coordinated with local financial regulators, police and market supervision departments to stop the promotional activities and the planned Solana event in Shanghai.

The investigation is expected to focus on the domestic organisations and individuals connected to the campaign. Officials have not publicly identified the parties under investigation or announced any penalties.

The latest development is particularly significant because it demonstrates that China's restrictions extend beyond cryptocurrency trading platforms to certain promotional and business activities associated with overseas crypto projects.

---

## Why Has China's Central Bank Started Investigating Solana?

The investigation follows Solana's promotional activities in Shanghai, where advertisements were reportedly displayed in the city's Lujiazui financial district.

One of the reported advertising placements appeared on the exterior of Citigroup Tower, attracting attention because of its location in one of Shanghai's most prominent financial centres.

For a cryptocurrency project, such visibility could have represented an important opportunity to increase brand recognition and attract interest from developers, investors and businesses.

However, China maintains strict restrictions on cryptocurrency-related financial activities.

The PBOC reportedly said Solana had worked with domestic entities to promote its brand through advertising. Regulators considered the campaign virtual currency marketing and determined that it violated existing restrictions.

The authorities subsequently intervened and moved to investigate the organisations and individuals involved.

Importantly, the announcement does not establish that the Solana Foundation itself has been formally charged with a criminal offence. The confirmed investigation concerns parties connected to the promotional activities inside China.

---

## What Happened to Solana's Shanghai Event?

The regulatory action follows the abrupt postponement of Solana Accelerate China 2026 in Shanghai on October 16.

The event was part of a broader series of planned gatherings across several Chinese cities, including Shanghai, Hangzhou, Shenzhen and Beijing.

The programme was expected to bring together members of the blockchain community for discussions involving blockchain infrastructure, artificial intelligence, payments and other technology-related applications.

However, registered attendees were unable to enter the Shanghai venue, and reports indicated that law enforcement officers were present.

At the time, the organisers described the postponement as a logistical issue.

Solana Foundation President Lily Liu also explained that registrations had increased sharply following publicity surrounding the event, reportedly exceeding the capacity approved for the venue.

The central bank's latest comments have now provided a regulatory explanation for the intervention.

Authorities said the promotional activities violated China's cryptocurrency restrictions and confirmed that the planned gathering was stopped through coordination with relevant departments.

This changes the context surrounding the event cancellation. What initially appeared to be an organisational problem is now linked directly to China's enforcement of its cryptocurrency rules.

## Why Is China Treating Solana Advertising as a Regulatory Issue?

China's position on cryptocurrencies has remained restrictive despite the industry's global expansion.

The country has repeatedly stated that virtual currencies do not have the same legal status as the Chinese yuan.

In February 2026, the People's Bank of China and seven other government agencies issued an updated notice addressing risks associated with virtual currencies and real-world asset tokenisation.

The regulatory notice reaffirmed restrictions on activities including:

- Cryptocurrency exchanges involving fiat and virtual currencies.
- Trading services involving different cryptocurrencies.
- Unauthorised token issuance and fundraising.
- Financial services connected to virtual currency transactions.
- Certain activities supporting overseas cryptocurrency platforms.
- Unauthorised promotional and related business activities.

The regulations also cover domestic entities that provide assistance to overseas cryptocurrency businesses when such activities violate Chinese requirements.

This framework is important for understanding the Solana investigation.

Authorities are not simply questioning the use of advertising space. They are examining whether domestic companies and individuals helped promote a cryptocurrency project in a way that falls within prohibited virtual currency-related business activities.

The distinction matters because blockchain technology itself is not universally prohibited in China. The country continues to support certain blockchain and digital infrastructure developments while maintaining restrictions on cryptocurrency-related financial activities.

## Could the Investigation Affect Solana's Global Operations?

At this stage, there is no confirmed indication that the investigation will directly affect Solana's global blockchain operations.

Solana continues to operate as a blockchain network, and the PBOC announcement concerns promotional activities and the parties involved in them within mainland China.

There has also been no official announcement of a worldwide restriction on SOL trading or a technical shutdown of the Solana network.

However, the situation could create additional challenges for the project's efforts to build relationships with businesses and communities in mainland China.

Companies working with international cryptocurrency projects may become more cautious about advertising, sponsorships, public events and other commercial arrangements.

The investigation could also influence how other blockchain foundations approach marketing and community-building activities in countries with strict cryptocurrency restrictions.

For Solana, the immediate challenge is not necessarily its technology. It is navigating the regulatory environment surrounding its attempts to establish a stronger presence in China.

## China's Cryptocurrency Crackdown Is Getting Broader

China's latest action reflects a wider regulatory approach that has developed over several years.

In 2013, authorities began establishing restrictions on the use of Bitcoin within the traditional financial system.

In 2021, regulators intensified enforcement against cryptocurrency trading and related financial services, significantly limiting the activities of domestic exchanges and service providers.

The February 2026 regulatory notice further reinforced the restrictions, addressing virtual currency activities, stablecoins and real-world asset tokenisation.

The latest Solana investigation adds another example of how authorities are applying those restrictions to activities involving overseas cryptocurrency projects and their domestic connections.

For international blockchain companies, the message is increasingly clear: having a globally recognised project does not automatically provide permission to market cryptocurrency-related services inside mainland China.
