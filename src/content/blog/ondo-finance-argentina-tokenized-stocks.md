Argentina is emerging as an important market in the race to bring traditional financial assets onto blockchain networks. Ondo Finance, a company focused on tokenized real-world assets, is looking to expand its presence in the country as local investors and financial platforms explore new ways to access global markets.

The opportunity comes as Argentina records substantial cryptocurrency activity and fintech companies experiment with products linked to traditional investments. For Ondo, the next phase may depend less on attracting crypto traders alone and more on working with established local financial platforms.

The development highlights a broader shift in digital finance: blockchain companies are increasingly trying to connect investors with assets such as U.S. stocks, exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and government securities, rather than relying exclusively on cryptocurrencies.

## Why Argentina Is Attracting Ondo Finance's Attention

Ondo Finance's growing interest in Argentina was highlighted in an October 28 report by Argentine publication iProUP, which covered remarks from Melanie Steiner, the company's regional director for Latin America.

According to the report, Steiner sees considerable potential for tokenized real-world assets in Argentina. She also indicated that Ondo wants to work with more local partners to make these products accessible through established financial services.

Argentina's existing crypto market helps explain the interest. Chainalysis reported that the country recorded approximately **$88.5 billion in cryptocurrency activity between July 2025 and June 2026**, placing it second in Latin America behind Brazil during that period.

That figure measures cryptocurrency activity, however, rather than investment in tokenized stocks specifically. It should be understood as evidence of an established digital-asset market, not proof that every crypto user is ready to invest in tokenized securities.

For Ondo, the opportunity is to build on that existing familiarity with digital assets and introduce products linked to more traditional financial markets.

## What Are Tokenized Stocks, and How Do They Work?

Tokenized stocks are blockchain-based tokens designed to provide exposure to the economic performance of traditional shares. Depending on the product's structure, a token may be backed by underlying securities or other assets and may offer mechanisms for minting and redemption.

Ondo Stocks, for example, provides eligible investors outside the United States with onchain exposure to a range of U.S.-listed stocks and ETFs. The company says its underlying securities and cash are held with U.S.-registered broker-dealers or U.S.-chartered national trust companies, subject to the product's terms.

The blockchain component can make these products transferable through supported wallets and platforms. Certain tokenized assets may also be usable in decentralised finance applications, where permitted.

But tokenized exposure is not automatically identical to directly holding a share through a conventional brokerage account. Investors must examine the legal rights attached to a token, its backing, redemption conditions, fees, platform availability and the rules that apply in their country.

This distinction matters as tokenization moves from a technical experiment toward products aimed at mainstream investors.

## Takenos Shows How Local Partnerships Could Expand Access

One example highlighted in the iProUP report is Takenos, an Argentine fintech platform that has worked with Ondo to offer tokenized ETF baskets.

The report says the products track the performance of the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100 and gold, with a reported minimum investment of $1. Such a low entry point could make these products accessible to users who want exposure to broader markets without committing a large amount of capital.

However, a low minimum investment does not remove market risk, transaction costs or regulatory requirements. Nor does it guarantee that investors will be able to buy, sell or redeem a product at every moment.

The partnership model is strategically important because local platforms already have relationships with users and understand the market in which they operate. Instead of relying only on investors to discover a global tokenization platform directly, Ondo can potentially reach a wider audience through established financial services.

Steiner told iProUP that expanding this partner network is an important objective for 2027. That signals a distribution strategy, rather than confirmation that a broad new rollout has already taken place.

## A Tokenized-Asset Market With Ambitious Growth Forecasts

The potential opportunity extends beyond Argentina.

According to figures cited in the iProUP report from RWA.xyz, tokenized real-world assets excluding stablecoins represented approximately **$38.8 billion** at the time of the report. The report also cited a projection that the market could reach $30 trillion by 2034.

That long-term figure is a forecast, not a confirmed outcome. Reaching it would require substantial growth in investor demand, market infrastructure, liquidity, regulatory clarity and participation from financial institutions.

Tokenization can potentially improve how assets are issued, transferred and integrated with digital financial services. Yet the technology alone cannot guarantee adoption. Investors still need confidence in the underlying assets, the legal structure and the institutions responsible for custody and redemption.

The $30 trillion projection therefore illustrates the scale of the opportunity imagined by industry participants, not the amount of money already invested in tokenized securities.

## Regulation Could Shape Argentina's Competitive Position

Regulatory treatment is likely to be a major factor in determining how quickly tokenized investment products expand across Latin America.

The iProUP report points to Argentina's securities regulator, the National Securities Commission (CNV), and the extension of its tokenization regulatory sandbox through December 31, 2027. A sandbox can provide a framework for testing certain financial innovations under defined regulatory conditions.

That does not mean every tokenized stock or investment product is automatically approved for every Argentine investor. The permissions and obligations depend on the specific product, the participating companies and the applicable rules.

Brazil is also adjusting its approach to crypto-asset service providers. Differences in licensing, compliance and operating requirements can affect where platforms choose to focus their resources, although regulation is only one factor in those decisions.

For Ondo, building relationships with appropriately authorised local partners could be an important way to navigate the region's different financial systems.

## What Could Stop Tokenized Stocks From Going Mainstream?

Despite the potential, several obstacles remain.

**Regulatory uncertainty:** Tokenized securities can fall under different rules depending on their structure and jurisdiction. Access in one country does not establish eligibility everywhere.

**Liquidity and pricing:** A token may be transferable around the clock on supported blockchain networks, but that does not guarantee deep liquidity, tight spreads or uninterrupted access to the underlying traditional market.

**Custody and legal rights:** Investors need to understand who holds the underlying securities, what rights token holders have and what happens if a platform or service provider encounters financial or operational difficulties.

**Market risk:** Tokenized products linked to stocks and ETFs remain exposed to the performance of the underlying assets. Blockchain technology does not protect investors from falling share prices.

**User trust:** Wider adoption will depend on clear disclosures, reliable platforms, accessible customer support and a straightforward process for buying, holding and redeeming assets.

These issues will help determine whether tokenized securities become a mainstream investment option or remain a relatively specialised corner of digital finance.

## What Ondo Finance's Argentina Strategy Means for the Crypto Industry

Ondo's focus on Argentina reflects a wider change in the crypto industry's priorities. Alongside Bitcoin and other digital assets, companies are building products that connect blockchain infrastructure with traditional financial markets.

Argentina offers an established cryptocurrency ecosystem and fintech platforms that may help distribute these products. The challenge is converting that potential into sustainable use by investors who understand both the benefits and the risks.

If local partnerships expand and regulatory requirements can be met, tokenized stocks and ETFs could become more visible in Argentina's digital investment market. But the pace of adoption will depend on investor demand, product design, liquidity and the legal protections available to users.

For now, the important development is not that tokenized stocks are guaranteed to transform Latin American investing. It is that Ondo Finance is treating Argentina as a market where local partnerships could help connect blockchain-based financial products with a much broader audience.
