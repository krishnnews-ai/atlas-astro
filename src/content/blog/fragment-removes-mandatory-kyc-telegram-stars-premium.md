A change reported on Telegram-linked marketplace Fragment could make it easier for users to purchase digital services without completing identity verification. According to reports published on November 2, 2026, Fragment has stopped requesting mandatory Know Your Customer (KYC) verification for purchases involving Telegram Stars, Telegram Premium and certain other services.

The reported change is notable because Fragment previously required users to complete an identity verification process before accessing some purchasing features. That process involved submitting personal information and identity documents through Sumsub, a third-party verification provider.

However, neither Fragment nor Telegram has publicly announced the removal of the requirement. The development should therefore be treated as a reported platform change rather than a confirmed permanent policy decision.

## What Has Changed on Fragment?

Fragment is a blockchain-based marketplace connected to Telegram and The Open Network (TON). It provides access to several Telegram-related digital products and services, including collectible usernames, anonymous phone numbers, digital gifts, Stars and Premium subscriptions.

According to a report by ForkLog and separate reporting by Russian technology publication Kod.ru, Fragment stopped displaying its mandatory identity verification requirement for certain purchases during the night of November 2.

The change reportedly allows users to complete purchases without going through the identity verification process that was previously required.

The disappearance of the verification section from the platform was also reported. Links that previously directed users to the account verification process were said to redirect to Fragment's homepage.

These observations suggest that the platform has changed how it handles verification for certain transactions. However, it remains unclear whether the change applies to every service and every user.

## Why the Previous KYC Requirement Mattered

Fragment introduced mandatory identity verification in November 2024, according to Telegram Info's earlier reporting.

At that time, users purchasing Telegram Stars, Premium subscriptions and certain other services through Fragment were required to complete a verification process.

The process included several steps:

- Email address verification.
- Phone number confirmation.
- Submission of personal information.
- Uploading a government-issued identity document.
- Facial verification.

The identity checks were handled through Sumsub, an external identity verification provider.

For some users, these requirements created an additional barrier to accessing Telegram-related services through the marketplace. Users who were uncomfortable submitting identity documents or completing facial verification had fewer options when using Fragment's purchasing system.

The reported removal of mandatory checks could therefore change the purchasing experience for those users.

## What Is Fragment and How Does It Connect to TON?

Fragment is closely connected to Telegram's digital asset ecosystem. It uses blockchain infrastructure associated with The Open Network, allowing users to interact with Telegram-related digital assets and services.

The marketplace became particularly relevant as Telegram expanded the use of Stars, collectible gifts, Premium subscriptions and blockchain-based usernames.

Telegram Stars are an in-app digital currency used for purchases and payments within Telegram's ecosystem. Telegram Premium provides additional features through a paid subscription.

Fragment provides an alternative purchasing channel for certain Telegram services, with cryptocurrency-based payments forming part of its marketplace model.

This connection makes changes to Fragment's verification requirements relevant beyond the marketplace itself. They may influence how users interact with Telegram's wider digital economy.

## Could the Change Benefit Telegram Users?

If the reported removal remains in place, the most immediate benefit could be a simpler purchasing process.

Users who previously had to submit identity documents and complete facial verification may find it easier to access supported purchasing features.

There are several potential implications.

**1. Fewer barriers to purchases**

Removing an additional verification step could reduce friction for users who want to purchase Telegram Stars or Premium through Fragment.

**2. Greater accessibility**

Some users avoid platforms that require identity documents for relatively small digital purchases. A less restrictive process could make Fragment more accessible to this audience.

**3. Privacy considerations**

Identity verification involves sharing personal information with a platform or its verification provider. Removing a mandatory step could reduce the amount of personal information users need to submit for certain transactions.

However, this does not mean that Fragment purchases are completely anonymous or that no user information is collected. Account access, wallet connections and transaction records may still involve identifying information.

**4. Potential impact on marketplace activity**

A simpler purchasing process could encourage more users to explore Fragment's services. Whether that translates into higher transaction volumes or increased demand for TON-related assets remains uncertain.

## What Could This Mean for the TON Ecosystem?

The development is also relevant to the broader TON ecosystem.

Fragment serves as an important connection between Telegram users and blockchain-based services. Changes that make its marketplace easier to access could potentially support activity across the ecosystem.

However, it would be premature to conclude that the reported KYC change will automatically increase demand for TON or influence its market price.

Cryptocurrency prices depend on several factors, including market conditions, liquidity, network activity, investor sentiment and broader economic developments.

For now, the significance of the reported change is primarily related to platform accessibility rather than any confirmed market impact.

## Why Has Fragment Removed the Verification Requirement?

The reason behind the reported change remains unknown.

Neither Telegram nor Fragment has publicly explained whether the adjustment is temporary, part of a wider policy update or connected to changes in its compliance procedures.

There is also no confirmed information about whether verification requirements could return or remain necessary for specific transactions.

The distinction matters because removing a verification requirement from selected purchasing features does not necessarily mean that all identity checks have been eliminated across the platform.

Users should also avoid assuming that every Fragment service now operates without KYC.

## What Users Should Keep in Mind

Although the reported change could make certain purchases easier, users should continue to exercise caution when interacting with cryptocurrency-based marketplaces.

They should:

- Access Fragment through its official website.
- Verify the transaction details before approving wallet payments.
- Never share wallet recovery phrases or private keys.
- Avoid unofficial services claiming to offer special verification exemptions.
- Check the platform's current requirements before making a purchase.

The removal of a verification step, if confirmed, does not eliminate the risks associated with cryptocurrency transactions, phishing websites or fraudulent accounts.
