Visa is bringing stablecoins deeper into international payments, teaming up with Singapore-based dtcpay to test stablecoin-funded cross-border payouts across Asia Pacific.

The new pilot will allow eligible payment businesses to fund international transfers using supported stablecoins through Visa Direct. The people receiving the money, however, will still get paid in traditional currencies.

That means businesses could use blockchain-based dollars to fund transfers while customers continue receiving money through familiar payment channels.

The move could make international payments more flexible, particularly for businesses dealing with multiple currencies, banking schedules and overseas markets.

Visa's network connects more than 18 billion payment endpoints globally, making the stablecoin integration a significant step toward bringing digital assets into mainstream payment infrastructure.

The pilot follows Visa's stablecoin payment expansion in the United States and highlights how major financial companies are increasingly using blockchain technology behind the scenes.

**The bigger picture:** Stablecoins may not replace traditional payment cards anytime soon—but Visa's latest move shows that they could increasingly become part of the infrastructure powering how money moves around the world.
