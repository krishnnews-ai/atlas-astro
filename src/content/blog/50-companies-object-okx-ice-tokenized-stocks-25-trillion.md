The push to bring Wall Street stocks onto blockchain networks is facing a growing challenge. Fifty companies have now reportedly objected to having tokenized versions of their shares traded through OKXICE TSV, according to a new update shared by Coin Bureau.

The companies named across the reported objections include some of the biggest businesses in the US market, from Nvidia, Microsoft and Micron to Nike, Walmart, JPMorgan Chase and Goldman Sachs.

Coin Bureau says the companies involved have a combined market value of more than $25 trillion. Its latest update also identifies 12 additional companies in the newest group of objections, including Nvidia, Micron, Costco, Palo Alto Networks, CrowdStrike, Nike and Reddit.

The development puts fresh pressure on a project involving crypto exchange OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange.

But the story is bigger than a disagreement over one trading platform. It raises a fundamental question for the future of tokenized securities: can digital versions of traditional shares become mainstream if the companies behind those shares do not want them traded in this way?

## Why Are Companies Objecting to Tokenized Versions of Their Shares?

Tokenization is the process of representing an asset through a digital token recorded on a blockchain.

In financial markets, a token can be designed to represent a traditional security or provide economic exposure linked to it. Supporters believe this could make trading more flexible and allow financial assets to move through blockchain-based infrastructure.

However, a token representing a company's stock is not automatically the same thing as owning that company's shares directly.

The legal structure matters. Investors need to understand whether a token gives them direct ownership, a beneficial interest or a separate contractual claim linked to the underlying stock.

That distinction is one reason issuer consent and clear disclosures matter.

A company may also want to know how its shares are represented, who is responsible for custody, how token supply is managed and whether investors could mistake an unaffiliated token for an issuer-approved product.

The objection notices do not provide a separate explanation from every company. It would therefore be inaccurate to say that all 50 companies share one specific reason for objecting.

Still, the wider debate highlights several issues that traditional businesses and investors may want addressed.

### Shareholder rights and ownership

Traditional shares come with established legal rights and procedures. Depending on the token's structure, holders of a blockchain-based product may not receive the same voting, dividend or ownership rights as conventional shareholders.

Clear information about those rights is essential.

### Trading outside traditional market hours

One of the attractions of tokenized stocks is the possibility of trading outside the usual hours of traditional exchanges.

But extended trading also raises questions about liquidity, price discovery and how a token's price relates to the underlying share when the main stock market is closed.

### Custody and investor protection

Investors need to know who holds the underlying shares, how the tokens are backed and what happens if an issuer, custodian or platform encounters financial or operational problems.

### Corporate control over share representation

Some companies may prefer to have a direct role in any product that represents their shares digitally. An unaffiliated third party issuing a token linked to a company's stock can raise questions about consent, branding, disclosures and responsibility.

These concerns do not mean that blockchain technology cannot be used in securities markets. They show why the structure of a tokenized product matters as much as the technology behind it.

## What Does the SEC's 30-Day Objection Rule Mean?

The US regulatory framework is an important part of this story.

On September 17, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission issued a temporary conditional exemptive order relating to certain distributed-ledger trading venues and tokenized national market system stocks.

Under the framework, covered venues must provide the relevant issuer with notice before offering certain tokenized versions of its shares issued by an unaffiliated third party.

An issuer may submit a written objection within 30 calendar days after receiving the notice.

If a timely objection is submitted, the relevant tokenized version of the company's shares cannot be traded on that venue under the conditions of the SEC order.

This gives companies a formal opportunity to object before the relevant tokenized stock is made available through the covered trading venue.

There is an important limitation: this is not a universal ban on tokenizing the company's shares across every platform.

The objection applies to the relevant venue and process covered by the regulatory framework. It does not automatically prevent a company from exploring other tokenization arrangements, including models involving direct issuer participation.

The SEC's order is also temporary and conditional. It should not be treated as unrestricted approval for every tokenized security or digital-asset trading platform.

## OKX and ICE Face a Bigger Challenge

OKXICE is a joint venture involving crypto exchange OKX and Intercontinental Exchange, the parent company of the New York Stock Exchange.

The proposed initiative aims to bring tokenized versions of traditional equities into a blockchain-based trading environment.

For crypto platforms, this is an opportunity to connect digital-asset infrastructure with one of the world's largest financial markets.

For investors, tokenized stocks could potentially offer additional ways to access traditional assets and interact with financial products through blockchain networks.

But a trading platform needs more than technology and a list of stock tickers. It also needs eligible assets, sufficient liquidity, regulatory clarity and confidence from market participants.

The growing number of objections could make the original stock selection more difficult to deliver.

If major companies remain outside the proposed offering, the platform may have to proceed with a narrower range of eligible stocks, subject to the applicable regulatory requirements.

That could affect the platform's appeal, particularly if investors were expecting access to some of the most widely traded US companies.

## Apple, Tesla and Amazon Have Not Objected So Far, Says Coin Bureau

One detail in Coin Bureau's latest update is particularly interesting.

The account says Apple, Tesla and Amazon have not objected so far.

That is worth watching because all three companies have major positions in the global technology and consumer markets.

However, the absence of an objection should not be interpreted as approval.

It does not establish that these companies have endorsed OKXICE, agreed to have their shares tokenized or authorised a third party to issue tokens linked to their stock.

Their position may become clearer as the relevant notice process develops.

For now, the distinction is simple: not having submitted an objection is not the same as giving formal consent.

## Does This Mean Wall Street Is Rejecting Blockchain?

Not necessarily.

The current objections concern a particular approach to tokenized stocks: the trading of unaffiliated third-party versions of company shares through a covered venue.

That is different from rejecting blockchain technology across the financial system.

Banks, financial institutions and technology companies can explore blockchain for payments, settlement, asset management and other applications without necessarily supporting every form of stock tokenization.

The distinction is important because tokenization is not one single product.

A token issued with the direct participation of a company may have a different legal and operational structure from a token created by an unaffiliated third party.

The future of tokenized securities may therefore depend on which models can provide the clearest ownership rights, reliable custody, investor protection and regulatory compliance.

## What Happens Next?

The next major development will be whether OKXICE can continue its plans with companies that remain eligible under the SEC framework.

Investors will also be watching for further issuer notices, public statements from the companies involved and any clarification about the platform's final selection of tokenized stocks.

The 50-company figure reported by Coin Bureau is a significant development, but it does not establish that the entire tokenization industry has been rejected.

It does show that bringing traditional shares onto blockchain networks is more complicated than simply creating digital tokens.

For tokenized stocks to gain wider acceptance, platforms will need to address the concerns of issuers as well as those of regulators and investors.

Wall Street's move towards blockchain may continue, but the latest objections show that the companies behind the shares want a say in how that transition happens.
