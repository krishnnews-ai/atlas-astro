Amazon may be preparing to take on one of Google's most important roles on the internet: helping people discover what to buy.

The e-commerce giant is reportedly exploring an ambitious initiative to build a database containing information about products sold around the world, including items that are not available on Amazon. The project, internally referred to as **World Product Graph**, could give the company a much broader view of the global retail market.

If the plan moves forward, Amazon could become more than a destination where customers search for products and place orders. It could become a central source of product information, helping shoppers compare brands, understand features and discover alternatives across the internet.

That would put Amazon in more direct competition with Google at a time when artificial intelligence is beginning to change how consumers research and purchase products.

However, the initiative remains a reported plan, not a confirmed global product launch. Its proposed direction was described in an internal document reviewed by Reuters, and Amazon has cautioned that such documents may be outdated or may not reflect its current plans.

---

## Amazon Wants to Build a Product Database Bigger Than Its Marketplace

Amazon's reported World Product Graph initiative is built around a straightforward but potentially powerful idea: collect information about products regardless of where they are sold.

Today, shoppers often move between search engines, retailer websites, brand pages and review platforms before making a purchase. A customer researching a laptop, for example, may compare specifications on several websites, check prices at different retailers and look for independent reviews.

Amazon's proposed database could bring much of that product information into a more unified system.

According to Reuters, the initiative would combine information supplied directly by manufacturers with product details gathered from other retailers' websites. The goal would be to create a more comprehensive picture of the products available across the internet.

This could help Amazon understand not only what it sells, but also what competing retailers and brands offer.

That distinction is important. A marketplace catalogue describes the products available on one platform. A broader product graph could potentially describe the wider retail market.

For Amazon, that information could become a valuable foundation for search, recommendations and AI-powered shopping experiences.

---

## Why Google Is in Amazon's Crosshairs

Google has spent decades becoming one of the main starting points for online product research.

Consumers use Google to discover unfamiliar brands, compare products, find retailers, read reviews and check prices. Even when a purchase eventually happens on Amazon or another shopping platform, the research may begin with a Google search.

That makes product discovery an important competitive battleground.

Reuters cited a 2025 Morgan Stanley research report indicating that 57% of online shoppers began product research on Google, compared with 16% on Amazon.

Those figures help explain why Amazon may want to expand beyond its traditional marketplace experience.

If shoppers can use Amazon to research products across the wider internet, the company could attract customers much earlier in the buying process.

Instead of waiting for someone to arrive with a specific product in mind, Amazon could help them decide what product they should consider in the first place.

That would challenge Google's role in product discovery and potentially change how retailers compete for consumer attention.

---

## AI Shopping Could Make This Database Even More Valuable

The timing of Amazon's reported initiative is particularly significant because artificial intelligence is changing what people expect from online shopping.

Traditional search requires users to enter keywords, open multiple pages and compare information themselves. AI shopping assistants promise a different experience: customers describe what they need, and the system helps identify suitable products.

Imagine asking an AI assistant:

*"Find me a reliable laptop for video editing under $1,200. Compare battery life, performance, display quality and customer feedback. Show me the best alternatives."*

Answering that question properly requires detailed and accurate information about multiple products, not just a list of sponsored links.

A comprehensive product database could help an AI assistant understand specifications, identify similar models, compare features and provide more relevant recommendations.

Amazon already has an AI-powered shopping assistant, now called Alexa for Shopping, which is designed to help customers research products, compare options and make purchasing decisions.

A broader product information system could potentially give such tools a stronger foundation.

The competitive advantage would not simply come from having an AI chatbot. It would come from giving that assistant reliable information about a huge range of products.

---

## Amazon Is Bringing Major Brands Into the Picture

Manufacturers could play an important role in making the reported project work.

Information collected from retailer websites may be incomplete, inconsistent or outdated. Direct contributions from brands could help improve the quality of product specifications and descriptions.

Reuters reported that Amazon had signed up Coca-Cola and Bayer to contribute product information. The document also described a meeting involving senior leaders from Amazon and Procter & Gamble to discuss a potential trial.

This could be important for categories where products have many versions and technical differences.

Consider smartphones, televisions, household appliances and personal care products. Two products may look almost identical but have different specifications, release dates or capabilities.

More accurate product information could help an AI shopping assistant distinguish between them and make better recommendations.

For manufacturers, participation in a major product discovery system could also become increasingly valuable as consumers rely more heavily on AI to research purchases.

---

## Amazon Faces a New Threat From Independent AI Shopping Agents

There is another reason why Amazon may want to strengthen its product information infrastructure.

AI shopping agents are emerging as potential intermediaries between consumers and retailers. Rather than visiting individual shopping websites, users may eventually ask an AI agent to research products, compare prices and complete purchases.

If those agents rely on external product databases, Amazon's marketplace could become just one source among many. A comprehensive product graph could help Amazon remain a central player in that environment.

The shift could also change how retailers compete. If an AI assistant recommends products based on detailed comparisons rather than sponsored placements, the quality and completeness of product information could become an important competitive factor.

That would give Amazon's reported database another strategic purpose: making sure its product information is part of the data that future AI shopping systems rely on.

---

## The Bottom Line

Amazon's reported World Product Graph initiative is an ambitious attempt to expand beyond its own marketplace and become a central source of product information across the internet.

If it moves forward, the project could challenge Google's dominant role in product discovery, strengthen Amazon's AI shopping tools and change how consumers research purchases.

The concept is still in the reported planning stage. No confirmed product launch or global rollout has been announced.

But the direction is clear. Amazon is not just trying to sell products. It wants to help people decide what to buy — wherever those products are sold.

**If Amazon succeeds, the next generation of online shopping may not begin with a search engine. It may begin with Amazon.**
