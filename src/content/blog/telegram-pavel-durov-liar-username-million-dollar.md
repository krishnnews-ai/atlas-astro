Imagine paying $1 million just to own a username that literally calls you a liar.

Sounds like a joke? Well, Telegram founder Pavel Durov has found himself at the centre of exactly this kind of unusual deal.

Someone has offered around $1 million for his Telegram username, **@liar**. And Durov's reaction was almost as entertaining as the offer itself.

He questioned whether the money was real, joking that it was difficult to trust someone who wanted to be known as a liar.

But the story took another turn when the buyer apparently put the money on the table.

---

## Pavel Durov Has a $1 Million Username Problem

On November 24, Durov shared the unusual offer on X.

His message was simple: someone wanted to pay $1 million for his @liar username.

Rather than celebrating the massive offer, Durov focused on the obvious problem with the buyer's chosen identity.

How do you trust someone whose preferred online name is @liar?

The post quickly attracted attention because it combined an extraordinary amount of money with one of the internet's funniest username choices.

But Durov shared another update on November 25.

He said the buyer had actually paid $1 million in Grams for @liar. He also linked to the listing on Telegram's Fragment marketplace.

Durov added another joke, saying he would call the buyer trustworthy but did not want to damage his new brand.

---

## Did Someone Really Pay $1 Million for Four Letters?

There is more to this story than a funny social media post.

The buyer's offer appeared in Telegram's Fragment marketplace, where collectible usernames are traded using blockchain technology.

The reported bid was 1,060,000 GRAM, valued at approximately $1 million when it appeared.

However, there is an important distinction between funding an auction and completing a sale.

Fragment continued to display the @liar auction as in progress, meaning the username had not yet been confirmed as transferred to a new owner.

So, while Durov confirmed that the buyer had paid the amount in Grams, the final ownership transfer remained a separate step.

---

## Why Are Telegram Usernames Worth So Much Money?

For most Telegram users, a username is simply a way for friends, customers or followers to find an account.

But Telegram has created a marketplace where certain usernames can be bought, sold and traded as digital collectibles.

That marketplace is called Fragment.

It uses the TON blockchain infrastructure to record ownership and facilitate transactions involving collectible usernames.

Short usernames, single words and memorable names can attract buyers because they are easy to recognise and can be useful for personal branding, online businesses or digital identity.

A name like @liar is certainly unusual. And apparently, unusual is not always cheap.

---

## Pavel Durov Previously Received a $256,000 Offer

Interestingly, this is not the first time Durov has discussed receiving a huge offer for one of his Telegram usernames.

He previously revealed that someone offered him $256,000 for his @pride username.

Durov joked about that offer too, wondering whether selling the username would mean he had sold his pride.

Now, the reported $1 million offer for @liar has taken the conversation to a completely different level.

The difference is that this time, the buyer appears to have backed the offer with cryptocurrency.

---

## A Million-Dollar Bid Also Raises Questions

The story has attracted another layer of attention.

Blockchain investigator ZachXBT publicly raised concerns about the bidder's alleged involvement in brokering expensive Telegram username deals connected to individuals suspected of using stolen cryptocurrency.

These are allegations made by the investigator, not proof that the bidder committed a crime. The claims should not be treated as established facts.

The development nevertheless shows that high-value digital collectible markets can attract both wealthy collectors and complicated questions about where the money comes from.

---

## The Internet Has Found Its Most Expensive Joke

There is something unusual about this entire episode.

People spend millions on luxury cars, rare watches and expensive digital artwork. But a Telegram username is just a handful of characters.

Yet, in the growing market for digital identities, even four letters can attract a seven-figure offer.

For Durov, the situation has created a rather entertaining dilemma: someone wants to pay a fortune for @liar, and the Telegram founder has to decide how much trust to place in a buyer with that name.

**One thing is certain: this may be the only million-dollar deal where the name itself makes everyone question the buyer's honesty.**
