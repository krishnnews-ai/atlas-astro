What if ordinary investors could access investment opportunities that were traditionally dominated by billionaires, private equity firms and major financial institutions?

That possibility is moving closer to reality as Revolut prepares another major expansion into blockchain-powered finance.

The global financial technology company plans to launch four blockchain-based funds designed to give users access to private markets through fractional investments. The development was announced by Revolut chairman Martin Gilbert at Solana's Breakpoint conference in London on November 17, according to reports.

The announcement is attracting attention because private markets have traditionally been difficult for ordinary investors to access. Investments in private equity, private credit and infrastructure often involve large capital requirements, complex fund structures and limited opportunities to withdraw money.

Revolut believes blockchain technology could help change how these investments are structured and distributed.

Instead of requiring investors to purchase large portions of traditionally illiquid assets, the planned funds would allow investments in smaller, fractional amounts.

However, Revolut has not yet published the complete fund structures, launch dates or eligibility requirements for these four planned products.

The announcement nevertheless signals an important development in the growing relationship between traditional finance and blockchain technology.

## Revolut Plans Four Blockchain-Based Funds: What Has Been Announced?

The latest announcement came from Martin Gilbert, chairman of Revolut, during the Solana Breakpoint conference in London.

According to reports, Gilbert explained that Revolut is planning four blockchain-based funds focused on making private-market investments accessible through smaller investment amounts.

The central idea is to use blockchain technology to divide investments into more manageable and marketable portions.

This could potentially make it easier for customers to gain exposure to financial assets that have traditionally required substantial capital.

Private markets operate differently from publicly traded stock exchanges. Investors generally cannot buy and sell private equity or private credit investments as easily as shares listed on major exchanges.

Blockchain technology could help create a more flexible structure for representing and distributing these investments.

Gilbert also highlighted concerns about liquidity in private markets, arguing that a broader pool of buyers could help address some of the challenges facing the sector.

For Revolut, the opportunity is not simply about launching another investment product. It is about exploring how blockchain infrastructure could change the way traditional financial products reach individual investors.

One important detail remains unknown: Revolut has not yet disclosed the names, underlying assets or complete operating arrangements of the four planned blockchain-based funds.

## Why Revolut Is Turning to Blockchain for Private Markets

Private markets represent a massive part of the global financial system.

Private equity, private credit and infrastructure investments attract capital from pension funds, insurance companies, sovereign wealth funds and wealthy individuals.

However, ordinary investors have historically faced several barriers.

These include:

- High minimum investment requirements.
- Complicated investment structures.
- Limited access to private investment opportunities.
- Longer investment holding periods.
- Restrictions on withdrawing or selling investments.

Blockchain technology could help reduce some of these barriers.

By representing investment interests through digital infrastructure, financial institutions may be able to divide ownership into smaller units and manage transactions more efficiently.

For example, instead of needing a large amount of capital to participate in a private-market fund, an eligible investor could potentially purchase a smaller fractional interest.

This does not automatically make the underlying investment liquid or eliminate financial risks.

However, it could change how investors access and manage their exposure to private assets.

That is the opportunity Revolut appears to be exploring.

## Revolut Already Opened Private Markets to Investors With Just €1

The latest blockchain announcement builds on a private-market investment offering that Revolut introduced in July 2026 for eligible customers in selected European markets.

The company partnered with four major investment managers:

- Apollo Global Management.
- Ares Management.
- Hamilton Lane.
- Partners Group.

These firms manage investment strategies covering private equity, private credit and private infrastructure.

Revolut introduced access through investment funds structured under the European Long-Term Investment Fund 2.0 framework, commonly known as ELTIF 2.0.

One of the most notable features was the reduction in entry requirements.

Eligible customers could access certain private-market investment products with a minimum investment starting at €1 through Revolut's platform.

This was significant because traditional private-market investments have often required much larger commitments.

The existing offering also includes periodic liquidity windows, depending on the individual fund's terms.

However, these windows do not guarantee that investors can withdraw whenever they want.

Private-market investments remain fundamentally illiquid, and redemptions can be restricted or suspended under certain conditions.

The four new blockchain-based funds represent a separate planned development rather than simply a relaunch of the existing products.

## Could Blockchain Make Private Equity Accessible to Everyday Investors?

Private equity has traditionally been associated with wealthy investors and institutional financial organisations.

Large investment firms raise capital, acquire stakes in private businesses and attempt to generate returns over longer investment periods.

Ordinary investors generally gain exposure to these opportunities indirectly through listed companies, certain investment funds or specialised financial products.

Revolut's latest plan raises an important question: Could blockchain change that model?

Fractional investing could allow a larger number of eligible customers to participate in investment structures without purchasing an entire investment position.

Consider a simplified example.

A private-market investment may traditionally require a substantial capital commitment. If a fund can be divided into smaller investment interests, multiple investors could potentially participate with smaller amounts.

Blockchain could provide infrastructure for recording and managing those digital interests.

This approach could make private-market investment products more accessible, although actual participation would still depend on fund rules, regulations and eligibility requirements.

The distinction is important because owning a fractional interest in a fund is not necessarily the same as directly owning shares in a private company.

The underlying legal structure determines what investors actually own.

## Why Liquidity Is Becoming a Major Concern in Private Markets

One of the most interesting parts of Gilbert's announcement was his focus on liquidity.

Private-market investments can be difficult to sell because they are not traded continuously on public exchanges.

Unlike publicly listed shares, private equity and private credit positions may have limited buyers and restrictions on transfers.

This can become a problem when investors want to withdraw capital but cannot easily find someone willing to purchase their investment.

Gilbert suggested that blockchain could help address this challenge by expanding the potential pool of buyers.

The idea is straightforward: if investment interests can be divided into smaller units and distributed more efficiently, more investors could potentially participate.

However, there is an important limitation.

Blockchain does not automatically create buyers or guarantee that an investment can be sold.

A digital investment unit can still be difficult to sell if market demand disappears or the fund restricts transfers.

For that reason, the success of Revolut's proposed funds will depend on their actual structure, investor protections and liquidity arrangements.

## Revolut and the Growing Race to Bring Traditional Finance Onchain

Revolut is not the only financial company exploring the connection between blockchain and traditional investments.

The broader financial industry is increasingly experimenting with digital representations of traditional assets.

Tokenization and blockchain-based fund structures are attracting interest because they could potentially improve the way financial products are issued, recorded and distributed.

Private equity, government securities, money market funds and real-world assets are among the areas attracting attention.

For financial institutions, the potential benefits include more efficient administration, programmable financial products and broader distribution.

Revolut's latest announcement suggests that the company wants to participate in this developing market rather than limit its business to traditional banking and brokerage services.

Its existing private-market partnerships already demonstrate an interest in bringing alternative investments to eligible retail customers.

The four planned blockchain-based funds could take that strategy further.

However, Revolut has not confirmed which blockchain network will support the new funds.

The announcement at Solana's Breakpoint conference should not be interpreted as confirmation that all four products will operate on Solana.

The underlying technology and network arrangements remain important unanswered questions.

## Is Revolut Building a New Investment Opportunity Beyond Stocks and Crypto?

For years, Revolut has expanded its financial services beyond everyday banking.

Its investment offerings include stocks, exchange-traded funds, bonds, money market funds and other financial products, alongside cryptocurrency services.

The company's private-market strategy adds another investment category to that ecosystem.

The direction is particularly interesting because private companies are increasingly remaining private for longer periods before going public.

As a result, some of their growth takes place before ordinary investors can access their shares through public stock exchanges.

Revolut has also announced Revolut Ventures, a separate platform being developed with Carne Group to provide eligible investors with access to private technology companies. The platform is expected to become available in 2027.

This is different from the four blockchain-based funds announced by Martin Gilbert, but both developments reflect the company's broader interest in expanding access to private investments.

Together, these moves suggest that Revolut wants to build a more comprehensive investment platform covering traditional financial products and alternative assets.

## What Investors Should Know Before Getting Excited

Although fractional investing sounds attractive, private-market investments carry risks that should not be ignored.

Smaller investment requirements do not automatically make an investment safer.

Some of the biggest considerations include:

**1. Limited liquidity**

Private-market investments may not be easy to sell. Investors could have to hold their positions for extended periods.

**2. Valuation uncertainty**

Private companies and assets are not necessarily valued continuously like publicly traded stocks. Their valuations may change significantly when new information becomes available.

**3. Capital loss**

Investors can lose part or all of their invested capital if underlying assets perform poorly.

**4. Regulatory restrictions**

Availability will depend on the countries where Revolut operates, local financial regulations and investor eligibility requirements.

**5. Blockchain-related risks**

The use of blockchain infrastructure introduces additional considerations, including cybersecurity, smart-contract risks and the legal treatment of digital investment interests.

Investors should also understand the fees, redemption conditions and underlying assets of each fund before committing money.

The real test will be whether Revolut can combine easier access with transparent investment structures and adequate investor protections.

## The Bottom Line

Revolut's plan to introduce four blockchain-based private-market funds could represent another important step in the evolution of digital finance.

The announcement by chairman Martin Gilbert at Solana's Breakpoint conference highlights the company's interest in using blockchain to make traditionally illiquid investments accessible through fractional amounts.

The move builds on Revolut's existing private-market offering, which already provides eligible European customers with access to funds managed by major investment firms.

However, the four new funds are still in the planning stage, and important details about their launch dates, underlying assets and blockchain infrastructure have not yet been disclosed.

If successfully implemented, the initiative could help bring private-market investment products to a wider audience.

The bigger question is whether blockchain can do more than simply digitise traditional financial products.

Can it fundamentally change who gets access to the investment opportunities that once belonged almost exclusively to institutional investors?

Revolut's next move could provide an important answer.
