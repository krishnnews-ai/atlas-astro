What if the dividends generated by your stock investments could gradually build Bitcoin exposure without requiring you to buy BTC separately?

That is the idea behind two new exchange-traded funds being prepared by Franklin Templeton, the $1.8 trillion asset manager. The proposed funds will combine US equities with a strategy that reinvests stock dividends into Bitcoin-linked investments, offering investors another route to gain exposure to the world's largest cryptocurrency.

The products are the Franklin US Equity Bitcoin DRIP Index ETF, which is expected to trade under the ticker FBEQ, and the Franklin US Innovation Bitcoin DRIP Index ETF, with the proposed ticker FIBT.

According to regulatory filings submitted to the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC), the funds are designed to track separate indexes that combine traditional equity investments with Bitcoin exposure. Their registration is scheduled to become effective on October 27, 2026, bringing the products closer to a potential Nasdaq debut.

However, an effective registration date does not mean trading has already begun. Franklin Templeton has not confirmed the funds' first trading date.

The concept is notable because it connects two investment approaches that have traditionally been handled separately: owning shares in US companies and building exposure to Bitcoin.

Rather than distributing stock dividends to investors as cash, the funds will use those payments to increase their Bitcoin-related exposure according to the rules of their underlying indexes.

For investors who want both equity-market participation and a measured allocation to Bitcoin, the structure offers a different approach. But it does not eliminate crypto risk, guarantee profits or make the funds equivalent to owning Bitcoin directly.

## How Franklin Templeton's Bitcoin Dividend ETFs Will Work

The central idea is straightforward: invest in US stocks, then use the dividends generated by those holdings to accumulate Bitcoin exposure.

Ordinarily, a company may distribute part of its earnings to shareholders through dividends. Investors can receive that money as cash or reinvest it in additional shares.

Franklin Templeton's proposed Bitcoin DRIP funds take a different approach. Their indexes direct regular and special dividends generated by the underlying stock portfolios into Bitcoin exposure rather than treating those payments as cash distributions to investors.

The strategy is built around two funds:

| ETF | Proposed ticker | Investment focus | Annual management fee |
|---|---|---|---:|
| Franklin US Equity Bitcoin DRIP Index ETF | FBEQ | Broad US large-cap equities | 0.09% |
| Franklin US Innovation Bitcoin DRIP Index ETF | FIBT | Innovation-focused US equities | 0.19% |

The fees and fund details are stated in the prospectuses filed with the SEC. Investors should review the final documents for the complete expense disclosures and investment risks.

Both funds are designed to begin with approximately 95% exposure to equities and 5% exposure to Bitcoin-related investments. Their underlying indexes will then use dividend reinvestment and rebalancing rules to manage the portfolio's Bitcoin allocation.

The result is a fund that seeks to combine traditional stock-market exposure with a systematic method of accumulating Bitcoin exposure over time.

One important distinction: the funds are not necessarily required to hold Bitcoin directly. Their prospectuses allow exposure through eligible Bitcoin exchange-traded products and other permitted instruments, including certain derivatives.

That means investors would own shares in an ETF holding a combination of investments, not Bitcoin in a personal wallet.

## Why the Funds Will Start With 95% Stocks and 5% Bitcoin Exposure

The proposed allocation is designed to make equities the dominant part of each portfolio while introducing a smaller Bitcoin component.

The broad-equity fund follows the VettaFi US Large-Cap 500 Bitcoin DRIP Index. The innovation-focused product follows the VettaFi US Innovation 100 Bitcoin DRIP Index.

Both indexes are designed to begin with a 95% allocation to equities and 5% to Bitcoin exposure. This initial allocation provides a reference point for how the products combine traditional investments with a cryptocurrency-related strategy.

The allocation is not a promise that Bitcoin will remain at exactly 5% at all times.

As the price of Bitcoin changes relative to the stock portfolio, its share of the fund's value can rise or fall. Dividend reinvestment can also increase Bitcoin exposure over time.

To manage those changes, the indexes include specific rebalancing rules.

At a quarterly rebalance, if Bitcoin exposure has risen above 5%, the index reduces it to 4.5%. If the allocation is at or below 5%, it can remain unchanged under the stated rules.

The indexes also include an additional cap mechanism. If Bitcoin's share rises above 20% between scheduled quarterly rebalances, the methodology calls for the allocation to be reduced to 4.5% under specified conditions.

These rules are designed to prevent Bitcoin exposure from growing without limit as its market price changes.

They do not remove the risk of losses. A 5% Bitcoin allocation can still affect the fund's performance, especially during sharp cryptocurrency price movements.

## How Stock Dividends Could Gradually Build Bitcoin Exposure

The proposed funds use a dividend-reinvestment mechanism rather than requiring investors to make separate Bitcoin purchases.

Under the index methodology described in the prospectuses, regular and special dividends from eligible stock holdings are reinvested into Bitcoin exposure at the market open on the business day after the relevant ex-dividend date.

The ex-dividend date is the date that determines whether a purchaser qualifies to receive an upcoming dividend. By linking the reinvestment process to that date, the strategy establishes a schedule for directing dividend income into the Bitcoin allocation.

Consider a simplified example.

Suppose a fund holds a portfolio of US stocks and those companies generate dividend income. Instead of distributing that income as cash, the fund's strategy directs it toward eligible Bitcoin-related investments.

Over time, those reinvestments can increase the fund's Bitcoin exposure, depending on the amount of dividends received, the performance of the underlying investments and the index's rebalancing rules.

The actual result will vary. Some companies pay larger dividends than others, and the value of the Bitcoin allocation can change substantially as BTC prices rise or fall.

The strategy also does not mean that the fund receives a special dividend from Bitcoin. Bitcoin itself does not generate corporate dividends. The income comes from the equity holdings, while the fund uses that income to obtain additional Bitcoin exposure.

That distinction is central to understanding the product.

The funds combine an equity portfolio that can generate dividends with a Bitcoin-related allocation that is intended to grow through reinvestment. They do not turn Bitcoin into a dividend-paying asset.

## Will Investors Receive Cash Dividends?

Not in the same way they might from a conventional dividend-focused ETF.

The proposed strategy is designed to reinvest dividends from the underlying stocks into Bitcoin exposure rather than simply distributing those payments to investors as cash.

For an investor, that changes the purpose of the dividend income. Instead of receiving a regular cash payment, the economic value generated by the underlying stocks is used within the fund's investment strategy.

This may appeal to investors who want to build Bitcoin exposure gradually through a single investment product. It may be less attractive to investors who rely on dividends for regular income or want cash distributions from their portfolio.

Investors should also distinguish between the dividends generated by the companies held in the fund and any distributions the ETF itself might make under its governing documents and applicable tax rules.

The final prospectus and subsequent fund disclosures should be consulted for the precise treatment of income, distributions and taxes.

## How These ETFs Differ From Spot Bitcoin ETFs

The new funds offer a different investment structure from a conventional spot Bitcoin ETF.

A spot Bitcoin ETF generally seeks to track Bitcoin's price by holding the cryptocurrency itself, subject to the fund's structure and expenses. Investors purchase ETF shares through a brokerage account rather than managing private keys or transferring Bitcoin directly.

Franklin Templeton already offers the Franklin Bitcoin ETF, which trades under the ticker EZBC.

The proposed Bitcoin DRIP funds have a different objective. They combine US equities with Bitcoin exposure, using stock dividends to increase the cryptocurrency-related portion of the portfolio.

The distinction can be summarised as follows:

| Feature | Spot Bitcoin ETF | Bitcoin DRIP ETF |
|---|---|---|
| Main investment exposure | Bitcoin | US equities plus Bitcoin-related investments |
| Holds individual company stocks | Generally no | Yes |
| Uses stock dividends to build BTC exposure | No | Yes, under the index methodology |
| Tracks Bitcoin alone | Generally designed to do so | No; combines equity and Bitcoin exposure |
| Direct personal ownership of BTC | No | No |

A spot Bitcoin ETF is generally a more direct way to obtain price exposure to Bitcoin through a brokerage account.

A Bitcoin DRIP ETF combines that exposure with stock-market performance, so its returns may differ substantially from Bitcoin's price movements.

The new structure could appeal to investors who prefer a combined portfolio. However, someone seeking the closest possible link to Bitcoin's price would need to compare the fund's methodology, allocation limits, fees and other characteristics with those of a spot Bitcoin product.

## How the Funds Compare With Bitcoin Futures ETFs

Bitcoin futures ETFs use futures contracts to obtain exposure to Bitcoin prices rather than necessarily holding the cryptocurrency directly.

Futures contracts can behave differently from spot Bitcoin, and the process of rolling contracts from one expiry to another can affect performance.

The proposed Franklin Bitcoin DRIP funds have a broader investment structure. They hold US equities and use dividends to build Bitcoin exposure through instruments permitted by their prospectuses, which may include exchange-traded products, futures, options and other eligible investments.

This makes them different from a futures-only strategy.

Investors should not assume that all Bitcoin-linked ETFs produce the same returns. Their performance depends on what they hold, how they obtain exposure, their allocation rules, operating expenses and the market conditions in which they operate.

The fund's name alone does not tell investors whether it will closely track spot Bitcoin, outperform it or provide lower volatility.

## Could Franklin Templeton's ETFs Attract More Traditional Investors?

The proposed structure could appeal to investors who already own US equities but do not want to manage a separate cryptocurrency position.

Buying a single ETF share can be operationally simpler than purchasing stocks, collecting dividends, opening a crypto account and then buying Bitcoin independently.

A dividend-reinvestment strategy may also appeal to investors who prefer a systematic approach rather than deciding when to purchase additional BTC.

However, convenience is not the same as lower investment risk.

The funds would combine exposure to the stock market with Bitcoin-related investments. Their value could fall if equities decline, Bitcoin drops sharply or both markets weaken at the same time.

The reinvestment mechanism also does not guarantee that dividends will offset Bitcoin losses. A relatively small amount of dividend income may be insufficient to compensate for a significant fall in the value of the Bitcoin allocation.

Investors should therefore consider whether the strategy fits their objectives, time horizon and ability to withstand losses.

The proposed products may make it easier to access a combined investment strategy, but they do not remove the need to understand the underlying assets.

## What Are the Main Risks?

The headline idea is simple, but the underlying investment risks deserve attention.

**Bitcoin volatility:** Bitcoin prices can change sharply over short periods. Even a limited allocation can affect the fund's performance.

**Stock-market risk:** The funds will hold US equities, meaning their value can fall during broader market downturns or when the companies in their indexes perform poorly.

**Tracking differences:** The funds seek to correspond to their underlying indexes before fees and expenses. Actual returns may differ because of costs, trading conditions and the performance of the instruments used to obtain Bitcoin exposure.

**Instrument and counterparty risk:** If a fund uses other exchange-traded products or derivatives to gain Bitcoin exposure, it may face additional risks associated with those instruments and their counterparties.

**Allocation rules:** Rebalancing can reduce Bitcoin exposure after strong price gains. This is intended to control the allocation, but it can also mean the fund does not fully participate in every subsequent price increase.

**No guaranteed income or returns:** Dividends from stock holdings do not guarantee a profit, and reinvesting those dividends into Bitcoin does not ensure that the fund's value will increase.

Investors should read the prospectus carefully and understand the fund's full investment policy before making a decision.

## When Will FBEQ and FIBT Start Trading?

Franklin Templeton has registered both funds' shares for listing on the Nasdaq Stock Market.

The SEC filing submitted on October 22 identifies October 27, 2026, as the effective date for the relevant registration statement. That is an important regulatory milestone, but it is not the same as confirmation that investors can begin trading the shares on that date.

The funds are expected to use the tickers FBEQ and FIBT, respectively. However, Franklin Templeton has not announced a confirmed first trading date in the filings reviewed for this article.

The distinction matters because registration becoming effective does not, on its own, establish that an exchange has begun trading the securities.

Investors interested in the products should check Nasdaq's listing information and Franklin Templeton's official fund pages for the latest status.

The prospectuses also make clear that the SEC has not approved or disapproved the securities or passed on the adequacy of the prospectuses. Investors should not interpret the filing as an endorsement of the investment strategy.

## What This Means for the Bitcoin Market

Franklin Templeton's proposed funds add another structure to the expanding range of products that connect traditional investment portfolios with cryptocurrency exposure.

Investors already have several ways to gain exposure without purchasing and managing Bitcoin directly, including spot Bitcoin ETFs, futures-based products, shares in companies that hold BTC on their balance sheets and stocks of businesses operating in the crypto industry.

The Bitcoin DRIP structure introduces a different approach by using income generated by stock holdings to increase Bitcoin exposure.

The product's significance will depend on investor demand, fund performance and how effectively the strategy meets its intended purpose.

The announcement does not prove that new money will flow into Bitcoin on a particular scale. The actual impact will depend on the funds' assets, investment activity, the instruments they use and broader market conditions.

Still, the concept reflects a continuing effort to package Bitcoin exposure in ways that fit more familiar investment structures.

For traditional investors, that may lower some operational barriers. For the crypto market, it creates another potential channel through which investors can obtain exposure to Bitcoin without holding the asset directly.
