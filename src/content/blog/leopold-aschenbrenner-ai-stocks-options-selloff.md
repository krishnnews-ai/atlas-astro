Leopold Aschenbrenner's renewed bets on artificial intelligence-related stocks are facing another difficult period after a sharp market sell-off hit several companies associated with AI infrastructure, semiconductor manufacturing and data-centre demand.

According to a market update shared by Coin Bureau on October 17, six of seven stocks and investment instruments highlighted in connection with Aschenbrenner's reported positions have declined since September 8. CoreWeave recorded the steepest reported drop at 37.4%, followed by SK Hynix at 25.8% and SanDisk at 23.8%. The Roundhill Memory ETF (DRAM), Bloom Energy and Intel were also lower, while Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) was the only name in the group showing a gain.

The seven figures in the post imply an equally weighted average decline of approximately 18.3%. That calculation describes the average price movement of the selected names, not the actual performance of Aschenbrenner's portfolio.

The development comes less than three months after his AI-focused hedge fund, Situational Awareness, suffered a severe setback during July's technology-stock sell-off. In September, financial media reported that the fund had returned to the options market with purchases involving several AI-linked companies.

The latest price movements have renewed attention on the risks of trying to capture the long-term growth of artificial intelligence through financial instruments with fixed expiration dates.

**The central issue is not whether AI will remain important to the global economy. It is whether a particular investment can withstand the timing, volatility and financing risks that come with betting on that future.**

## What Happened to Aschenbrenner's Hedge Fund in July?

To understand the latest developments, it is necessary to look back at the July sell-off.

Situational Awareness, the hedge fund founded by former OpenAI researcher Leopold Aschenbrenner, had built substantial exposure to companies connected to artificial intelligence, computing infrastructure and semiconductor demand.

The fund experienced an extraordinary reversal when several of its investments declined sharply.

According to Reuters, Situational Awareness's portfolio value fell approximately 67% in July 2026, based on an investor letter reviewed by the news agency. The fund subsequently sold most of its publicly traded equity holdings in a transaction involving Ken Griffin's Citadel, according to reporting at the time.

Aschenbrenner acknowledged the severity of the episode in his communication to investors, taking responsibility for the outcome and warning that the fund had come closer to permanent capital impairment than it considered acceptable.

The episode raised questions about concentrated positions, leverage, liquidity and the speed at which losses can compound when several investments move against a portfolio at the same time.

A fund can have a persuasive long-term investment thesis and still face a crisis if its financing arrangements require it to reduce exposure before its investments recover.

That is particularly relevant in the technology sector, where valuations can change rapidly as expectations about future earnings, computing demand and capital spending shift.

## Why Did Aschenbrenner Return to Options Trading?

In September, reports indicated that Situational Awareness had resumed buying options tied to AI-related stocks.

Investing.com, citing CNBC reporting, said Aschenbrenner's fund had purchased options worth hundreds of millions of dollars in premium in late August and early September. The reported names included CoreWeave, SK Hynix, SanDisk, AMD and Bloom Energy, along with other instruments linked to the semiconductor sector.

The reporting did not establish whether the fund had raised additional capital for these trades or disclose enough detail to determine the complete structure of its positions.

Options are financial contracts whose value depends on factors such as the underlying asset's price, the strike price, time remaining until expiration, volatility and the premium paid.

A call option can provide exposure to a rising share price without requiring the investor to purchase the shares outright. But the contract has a deadline, and the stock may need to move far enough in the right direction for the trade to become profitable.

If the expected move takes too long, the option can lose value even if the underlying company remains fundamentally attractive.

For a fund that has recently experienced a major drawdown, options can offer a way to obtain defined-risk exposure when purchased without borrowing against the position. However, they can also produce substantial losses if the underlying assets move unfavourably or fail to move quickly enough.

The key detail is that buying options does not automatically mean a trader is using leverage in the same way as a margin-funded stock position. The risks depend on the contract structure, financing arrangements and whether the fund has other positions offsetting the exposure.

## Why Is CoreWeave Under Pressure?

CoreWeave is a major provider of cloud computing infrastructure designed for demanding workloads, including artificial intelligence training and inference.

Its business is closely tied to the growing need for specialised computing capacity. AI developers require access to large numbers of advanced processors, data-centre facilities, networking equipment and reliable power.

That demand creates an opportunity for infrastructure providers, but it also requires significant capital investment.

Building data centres and securing computing equipment can involve substantial upfront spending, while revenue is generated over time through customer contracts. Borrowing costs, customer concentration, infrastructure utilisation and the pace of new investment can therefore affect how investors value the business.

A decline in CoreWeave shares does not, by itself, establish that demand for AI computing has disappeared. It may reflect changing expectations about the company's future cash flows, financing costs, competitive position or the price investors are willing to pay for growth.

For options investors, however, the size and speed of a price decline can be particularly important. A sharp move in the underlying shares can quickly change an option's value, while an extended period of volatility may make it difficult to predict whether a recovery will arrive before expiration.

The broader lesson is that growth in AI computing demand does not guarantee a smooth share-price trajectory for every company supplying that demand.

## SK Hynix and SanDisk Highlight the Semiconductor Risk

SK Hynix and SanDisk occupy different parts of the memory and storage market, but both are connected to the growing computing requirements of AI systems.

SK Hynix is a major semiconductor manufacturer known for memory products, including high-bandwidth memory used in advanced computing applications.

SanDisk focuses on flash-memory and data-storage products. Its business is exposed to demand for storage across consumer, enterprise and data-centre markets.

The expansion of AI infrastructure has created opportunities for companies supplying memory and storage. At the same time, semiconductor businesses can be affected by pricing cycles, capital expenditure, inventory changes, competition and expectations about future demand.

Investors may pay high valuations when they expect a prolonged period of strong growth. If expectations become less optimistic, share prices can decline even when the underlying companies continue to operate and sell products.

This is why AI-related semiconductor investments can be volatile. The market is not simply evaluating whether AI adoption will increase; it is also estimating how much profit each company can capture, how long strong demand will last and whether current share prices already reflect those expectations.

The reported declines in SK Hynix and SanDisk illustrate the importance of separating a long-term technology trend from the near-term performance of individual securities.

## Why Is the DRAM ETF Included in the List?

The Roundhill Memory ETF trades under the ticker DRAM and provides investors with exposure to companies in the memory sector.

Unlike a single company stock, an exchange-traded fund holds a portfolio of securities according to its investment strategy. Its return depends on the performance and weighting of those underlying holdings, as well as fund expenses and other factors.

The ETF's inclusion in the list is relevant because investors often use sector funds to express a view on a particular part of the AI supply chain without selecting only one company.

However, a sector ETF is not automatically less risky than an individual stock. If its holdings are concentrated in a cyclical industry, the fund may still experience significant declines when that industry sells off.

A decline in DRAM also should not be confused with a fall in the spot price of DRAM memory chips. The ticker refers to an investment product, and its market price reflects the performance of the ETF's holdings.

## Why Options Can Lose Money Even When the AI Thesis Is Correct

One of the most important lessons from the situation is the difference between being correct about a technology's future and making money from a particular trade.

Imagine an investor believes a semiconductor company will benefit from growing AI demand over the next three years.

The investor buys a call option that expires in three months. The company continues to expand its business, but its share price remains flat for the next two months before beginning to rise.

The investor may still lose money if the share-price increase comes too late or is too small to offset the option's premium and other pricing factors.

This happens because options have an expiration date. As time passes, the value associated with the possibility of a favourable future move can diminish, all else being equal. This effect is commonly called time decay.

Options prices are also influenced by implied volatility. If expectations for future price swings decline, an option can lose value even without a major move in the underlying stock.

The effect is especially important for investors purchasing options ahead of anticipated events or market recoveries. Being right about the direction is not always enough; the magnitude and timing of the move matter too.

For options purchased without borrowing, the maximum loss on a conventional long call or put is generally the premium paid, assuming no additional positions or financing obligations. That premium can nevertheless represent a very large amount of money when a fund buys contracts at scale.

Other options strategies can involve significantly different risks, including losses greater than the initial premium.

## Does the Latest Sell-Off Mean the AI Boom Is Over?

Not necessarily.

AI adoption, computing demand and semiconductor investment are long-term themes. A short-term sell-off in AI-related shares does not prove that those trends have ended.

At the same time, long-term growth in a sector does not guarantee that every company will succeed or that every stock purchased at a particular price will generate a positive return.

Investors must distinguish between three separate questions:

1. **Technology:** Will businesses continue adopting AI?
2. **Company fundamentals:** Which suppliers can convert that demand into sustainable revenue and profits?
3. **Investment valuation:** Are the shares priced appropriately for the expected growth and risks?

These questions are related, but they are not interchangeable.

A company can benefit from increasing AI adoption while its shares decline because investors had expected even faster growth. A stock can also rise sharply on improved expectations without an equivalent change in current earnings.

The recent volatility in AI-linked equities reflects the sensitivity of growth-oriented investments to changes in expectations, financing conditions and market positioning.

Broader market reporting has also highlighted concerns about debt-funded AI infrastructure spending and the profitability of major data-centre projects. Those factors can affect sentiment toward companies connected to the same investment cycle.

## The Bigger Lesson: Timing Can Matter as Much as Conviction

Aschenbrenner's experience highlights a challenge that extends beyond one investor or one hedge fund.

The AI industry may offer substantial long-term opportunities, but financial markets do not reward every investor who identifies a promising technology. Returns also depend on entry prices, portfolio concentration, financing arrangements, risk controls and the time horizon of the investment.

The July setback showed how quickly a concentrated portfolio can come under pressure when several related investments decline together. The September options purchases then placed renewed attention on the fund's exposure to short-term market movements.

The latest figures shared by Coin Bureau suggest that several of the highlighted stocks have since moved sharply lower. They do not establish the fund's actual losses, and they do not settle the broader question of AI's long-term prospects.

What they do illustrate is a central principle of options investing: **a compelling long-term thesis can still produce a poor short-term trade when the timing is wrong.**

For investors following the AI sector, the distinction between technological progress, company performance and investment returns remains essential.
