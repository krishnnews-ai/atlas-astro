Switzerland is taking another major step toward bringing traditional banking and blockchain technology closer together. The Swiss National Bank (SNB), UBS and SIX are among nine institutions exploring a shared digital financial infrastructure that could change how financial assets and payments move between banks.

The initiative, called the **Swiss Financial Ledger**, was announced on November 24, 2026, as part of the central bank's ongoing Project Helvetia.

But this is not simply another blockchain experiment. The bigger idea is to explore whether central bank money, commercial bank money and tokenized securities can interact on a common digital infrastructure.

---

## Nine Institutions Are Exploring the Project

The initiative brings together the Swiss National Bank and eight other institutions:

- UBS
- SIX
- Zürcher Kantonalbank
- PostFinance
- Banque Cantonale Vaudoise
- Basler Kantonalbank
- Sygnum Bank
- Seturion

Together, these participants will examine the technical design, potential benefits, risks and governance of a shared financial ledger.

The project also builds on Project Agorá, an international initiative exploring how tokenization could improve cross-border payments.

---

## How Could the Swiss Financial Ledger Work?

One of the most interesting parts of Switzerland's experiment is how payments and financial assets could be settled.

Under Project Helvetia, the central bank is testing two different approaches.

In the first approach, participating financial institutions can use digital Swiss francs issued by the central bank on the SIX Digital Asset Platform.

This allows a tokenized bond and the payment to be transferred together. In simple terms, the buyer receives the bond while the seller receives the money.

The second approach works differently. The tokenized asset changes hands on a digital ledger, while the payment is settled through Switzerland's existing interbank payment infrastructure.

Both approaches are designed to make transactions more coordinated and reduce settlement risks.

---

## Why Does This Matter for Tokenized Finance?

Traditional financial markets often rely on separate systems for trading, payments and settlement.

A shared ledger could potentially make these processes more connected.

For banks, this could mean more efficient settlement of tokenized bonds and other financial assets. It could also open the door to new domestic and international financial services.

The development is particularly important as banks and financial institutions increasingly explore tokenized versions of traditional financial products.

---

## Is Switzerland Launching a New Digital Currency?

Not yet.

The Swiss Financial Ledger is an exploratory project, not a confirmed nationwide blockchain launch.

The SNB has also clarified that the initiative does not mean it has decided to introduce tokenized central bank money permanently.

Project Helvetia is scheduled to continue until at least June 2028, giving participating institutions more time to examine the technology and its practical applications.

**The bigger question now is whether Switzerland can bring traditional banking money and tokenized financial assets closer together without replacing the financial infrastructure that already works.**
