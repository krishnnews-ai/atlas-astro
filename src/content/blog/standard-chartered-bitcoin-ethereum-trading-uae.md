Standard Chartered has launched a cryptocurrency trading service in the United Arab Emirates that allows eligible affluent clients to buy and sell Bitcoin (BTC) and Ether (ETH) through their existing bank accounts. The announcement marks another step in the integration of digital assets into traditional banking services [citation:2].

According to the bank's official announcement on October 14, the offering is designed to give eligible clients access to cryptocurrencies through its existing banking platform, with custody and settlement managed within the bank's service ecosystem. The initial rollout will target Professional Investor clients in the UAE [citation:2][citation:7].

The launch is significant because it brings cryptocurrency trading closer to conventional wealth-management services. Rather than requiring eligible customers to open a separate account with an independent crypto trading platform, the new arrangement allows them to access Bitcoin and Ether through their existing relationship with Standard Chartered [citation:10].

However, the service is not being offered to all retail banking customers, and the announcement does not mean that cryptocurrency trading has become available to every bank customer in the UAE [citation:2].

## How Standard Chartered's New Crypto Trading Service Works

Standard Chartered said its new offering was developed through collaboration between its Wealth and Retail Banking (WRB) and Corporate and Investment Banking (CIB) businesses, alongside Zodia Markets, which is backed by SC Ventures [citation:2][citation:3].

The service brings together client access, liquidity, custody and settlement within an integrated digital-asset framework [citation:2].

Eligible clients can buy and sell Bitcoin and Ether directly through their existing Standard Chartered bank accounts. According to the bank, its closed-loop operating model is designed to reduce exposure to risks associated with third-party wallets by managing custody and settlement within its own service ecosystem [citation:2][citation:10].

This structure differs from the conventional process in which an investor transfers money to a separate cryptocurrency exchange, executes a trade and manages assets through an external wallet or custody provider [citation:10].

The precise account eligibility, applicable fees, transaction limits and other service conditions should be confirmed directly with Standard Chartered. According to product disclosures, the service includes a spread of up to 2%, individual orders range from $50 to $50,000, and there are no additional transaction or custody fees [citation:20][citation:25].

## UAE Professional Investors Will Get Initial Access

The bank said the new service will initially be available to Professional Investor clients in the UAE, with plans to expand into other markets over time [citation:2][citation:7].

Standard Chartered also confirmed that it had received licensing approval from the UAE's Virtual Assets Regulatory Authority (VARA) for the offering [citation:2][citation:14].

This regulatory approval is an important part of the launch. Cryptocurrency services can involve substantial market, operational and custody risks, making the regulatory framework and the specific permissions held by a service provider important considerations for investors [citation:10].

The launch does not establish that every cryptocurrency-related activity is covered by the same approval. Investors should review the service's terms and applicable regulatory protections before trading [citation:10].

Rola Abu Manneh, Standard Chartered's Chief Executive Officer for the UAE, Middle East and Pakistan, described the UAE as a natural market for the initiative, citing its approach to financial innovation and its growing role in digital assets [citation:2].

The bank also identified the UAE as one of its four global wealth hubs, highlighting the market's importance to its broader wealth-management strategy [citation:2].

## Why Bitcoin and Ether?

Standard Chartered's initial offering focuses on Bitcoin and Ether, the native assets of the Bitcoin and Ethereum networks, respectively [citation:2].

Bitcoin is widely known as the largest cryptocurrency by market capitalization, while Ether is used within the Ethereum ecosystem for transaction fees, network activity and a range of blockchain-based applications. As reported in our coverage of [Morgan Stanley's tokenized asset projections](https://www.coinainews.com/morgan-stanley-tokenized-assets-2030/), institutional interest in digital assets continues to expand across major financial institutions.

Both assets are widely traded, but their prices can fluctuate sharply. Their inclusion in a bank's wealth offering does not remove the risks associated with owning or trading cryptocurrencies [citation:10].

The initial focus on these two assets also reflects the scope of the service announced by the bank. Standard Chartered has not stated in this announcement that the new UAE wealth-client offering includes every cryptocurrency or token available in the wider digital-asset market [citation:2].

## A Broader Expansion of Standard Chartered's Digital-Asset Business

The October 14 launch builds on Standard Chartered's existing digital-asset capabilities, which include cryptocurrency custody, trading and tokenisation-related initiatives [citation:2][citation:22].

In September 2026, the bank announced a separate expansion of its institutional Bitcoin and Ether spot-trading services in the UAE through Standard Chartered DIFC, its Dubai International Financial Centre entity regulated by the Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA) [citation:1][citation:4].

That earlier service was designed for eligible institutional clients seeking spot trading in Bitcoin and Ether. As we reported in our article on [BlackRock's US stock outlook](https://www.coinainews.com/blackrock-us-stocks-outlook-ai-market-volatility/), institutional adoption of digital assets continues to evolve alongside broader market dynamics.

The October announcement addresses a different client segment: eligible affluent and Professional Investor clients accessing cryptocurrencies through the bank's wealth offering [citation:2].

Distinguishing the two launches is important. Institutional spot trading and wealth-client cryptocurrency access are related parts of the bank's digital-asset strategy, but they are not necessarily identical products or subject to identical account conditions [citation:10].

Standard Chartered has also offered clients access to cryptocurrency exchange-traded funds through its cash-equities trading platform, alongside certain structured products and over-the-counter derivatives linked to cryptocurrency-related investments [citation:2].

Taken together, these activities show how a traditional international bank can expand its digital-asset services across several areas without making every product available to every customer [citation:10].

## What the Launch Means for the Crypto Industry

The new service represents a further step in bringing cryptocurrency exposure into established banking and wealth-management relationships [citation:2].

For eligible investors, using an existing banking platform may simplify access to Bitcoin and Ether. An integrated custody and settlement arrangement may also reduce the need to manage certain operational processes independently, although it does not eliminate market risk or guarantee the safety of an investment [citation:10].

For the wider cryptocurrency industry, participation by established financial institutions can provide an additional channel through which qualified investors access digital assets [citation:2].

The development may also increase competitive pressure on cryptocurrency exchanges, brokers and specialist custody providers to demonstrate the value of their trading infrastructure, liquidity, security and customer services [citation:10].

Nevertheless, one bank's product launch is not evidence that all banks are adopting the same strategy. Institutions differ in their risk policies, regulatory permissions, client requirements and willingness to offer direct cryptocurrency trading [citation:10].

The service's longer-term impact will depend on factors such as client demand, product availability, trading costs, regulatory developments and any future expansion into additional markets [citation:2].

## What Investors Should Know Before Trading

Although the service is offered through an established banking relationship, Bitcoin and Ether remain volatile assets [citation:10].

Potential users should confirm whether they meet the bank's Professional Investor eligibility criteria and whether the service is available for their particular account. According to reports, eligibility requires UAE residency, an age above 21, and a banking relationship of at least AED 4 million (approximately $1.1 million), excluding primary residence [citation:17][citation:20].

They should also review transaction fees, custody arrangements, withdrawal or transfer restrictions, tax obligations and the legal protections that apply to their specific circumstances [citation:10].

The bank's integrated model may simplify some aspects of accessing digital assets, but it should not be interpreted as a guarantee against losses, cyber incidents or other operational risks [citation:10].

Investors should also distinguish between direct cryptocurrency ownership and exposure through exchange-traded funds, structured products or derivatives, as these products can have different costs, risks and legal characteristics [citation:10].
