Donald Trump is preparing to meet some of the biggest holders of his $TRUMP memecoin again. But this time, the excitement surrounding the exclusive dinner appears to have faded — and the political questions are getting louder.

The latest gathering is scheduled for November 22 at Trump National Golf Club in Virginia. It is the third event offering major token holders a chance to attend a dinner featuring the US president.

The difference this time is striking: the memecoin has lost almost all of its value since its 2025 peak, while participation in the contest has dropped sharply.

---

## From a $1,200 Cost to a $6 Profit

Crypto trader Morten Christensen told Bloomberg that getting into the earlier dinners came at a cost. He estimated that qualifying for the first event cost him around $1,200, while the second cost approximately $500.

This time, however, he says he managed to turn the strategy into a roughly $6 profit.

His approach involved buying $TRUMP tokens while also taking a separate position designed to benefit if the token price fell. The hedge helped offset the risk of holding the memecoin long enough to qualify.

It is an unusual situation: a trader says he effectively earned money while securing a place at a dinner linked to a cryptocurrency that has suffered a major decline.

---

## $TRUMP Has Fallen Nearly 99% From Its Peak

The market performance tells a very different story from the excitement around the first dinner.

The $TRUMP token reached a high above $74 in January 2025. By November 2026, it was trading around the $1 level, representing a decline of roughly 99% from its peak.

Interest in the latest contest has also weakened. Reuters reported that the leaderboard closed with 130 wallets holding approximately $8.6 million in combined token value — around 94% lower than the amount recorded for the first dinner contest.

The figures suggest that the event is no longer generating the same level of buying activity that accompanied its earlier editions.

---

## Why Are Lawmakers Raising Questions?

The dinner has become controversial because access to a sitting president is being offered through ownership of a cryptocurrency associated with him.

Democratic lawmakers argue that wealthy investors, including potentially foreign participants, could use token purchases to gain access to Trump. They have raised concerns about conflicts of interest, transparency and whether private financial activity could create opportunities for political influence.

In October, Senator Adam Schiff and other Democratic senators asked the White House for answers about the event and the safeguards surrounding it.

These are allegations and concerns raised by lawmakers, not a finding that attendees have received political favors.

---

## White House Says It Is a Private Event

The White House has pushed back against treating the gathering as an official government function.

Spokesperson Davis Ingle told Bloomberg that the president's attendance at a privately organized event does not make it a White House event or government function.

That response addresses the administration's position on the event's status, but it does not resolve the wider debate over whether a president's private crypto-related business interests should overlap with access to the presidency.

---

## A Memecoin Dinner Facing a Different Reality

The latest dinner highlights a sharp contrast between the early excitement around Trump's memecoin and its current position.

In 2025, the promise of meeting the president helped attract significant attention from crypto investors. Now, the token is trading far below its peak, participation has weakened and political scrutiny has intensified.

The dinner may still attract prominent guests, but the bigger question is whether the $TRUMP project can regain investor confidence — or whether its most valuable selling point has become access to the president rather than the token itself.

For the crypto market, the episode is another reminder that celebrity-linked memecoins can be driven by attention and political headlines, but that attention does not guarantee lasting value.
