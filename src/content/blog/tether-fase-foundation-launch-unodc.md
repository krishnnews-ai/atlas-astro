The cryptocurrency industry is taking a broader role in financial safety and humanitarian assistance. Tether, the company behind the USDT stablecoin, has joined forces with major digital-asset businesses and nonprofit organisations to establish the Financial Access and Systemic Empowerment Foundation, known as FASE.

Announced on October 28, 2026, the Swiss-based nonprofit brings together 11 founding stakeholders, including Tether, Bybit, Kraken, Chainalysis and the Solana Foundation. The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) will participate as a strategic partner, providing technical advice.

The foundation's initial priorities include educating young people about online scams, supporting organisations that assist human trafficking survivors and exploring the use of stablecoins to distribute humanitarian aid.

Tether says one youth education initiative aims to reach up to 10 million young people, while a proposed humanitarian aid pilot could potentially reach up to 1 million people. These are announced targets, not confirmed beneficiary numbers.

The launch raises an important question for the crypto sector: can blockchain-based financial tools deliver measurable benefits to vulnerable communities beyond their existing role in digital-asset markets?

## What Is the FASE Foundation?

The Financial Access and Systemic Empowerment Foundation is a Swiss nonprofit established by Tether and a group of industry and organisational partners.

According to Tether's official announcement, FASE is organised around four pillars:

- **Financial Safety:** Helping protect people from financial harm, including scams and fraud.
- **Financial Freedom and Inclusion:** Expanding access to financial services for underserved communities.
- **Access to Resources:** Supporting access to connectivity, energy and healthcare.
- **Responsible AI and Robotics:** Encouraging ethical, transparent and human-centred technological development.

The initiative brings together companies from different parts of the digital-asset ecosystem, alongside organisations with experience in financial services, technology and humanitarian work.

However, the foundation's establishment does not automatically mean that every proposed programme is already operating at scale. Its future impact will depend on implementation, funding, partnerships and publicly reported results.

The United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime is a strategic partner rather than a member of the listed founding stakeholder group.

That distinction is important. UNODC's involvement provides a connection to the United Nations' work on crime prevention, but the announcement does not mean the foundation is itself a United Nations agency.

The organisations bring different capabilities to the initiative, ranging from digital-asset infrastructure and blockchain analytics to financial services and humanitarian programmes. Whether those capabilities translate into effective collaboration will become clearer as individual projects develop.

## FASE Plans to Reach Up to 10 Million Young People With Scam-Prevention Education

One of the foundation's first initiatives is a global youth alliance with Goodwall, a youth engagement platform.

The programme aims to provide up to 10 million young people with digital education and tools to recognise and avoid online scams. Tether says the initiative will prioritise communities in Africa, Latin America and Southeast Asia.

Online fraud has become an important concern across the digital economy. Scammers can use fake investment platforms, impersonation, phishing messages and fraudulent cryptocurrency offers to persuade victims to transfer money or reveal sensitive information.

Education can help people recognise suspicious messages, verify websites and avoid sharing wallet credentials. However, awareness programmes are only one part of the response; effective protection also requires reporting mechanisms, platform safeguards and law-enforcement cooperation.

FASE has not yet disclosed a detailed programme timetable, a complete curriculum or independently verified participation figures in the launch announcement. Its 10 million figure should therefore be understood as an intended reach target.

## Humanitarian Aid Pilot Could Use Stablecoins to Reach Up to 1 Million People

Another planned initiative involves Prosper Global, formerly known as Mercy Corps.

FASE says the collaboration will explore stablecoin-based humanitarian aid distribution, with an initial pilot that could potentially reach up to 1 million people.

Stablecoins are digital tokens designed to maintain a relatively stable value, often by tracking a currency such as the US dollar. They can be transferred across supported blockchain networks and may offer an alternative to certain conventional payment arrangements.

For humanitarian organisations, digital payments could potentially make some transfers faster or easier to track. But the practical benefits depend on the circumstances in which aid recipients live and the infrastructure available to them.

Access to a suitable device, internet connectivity, a compatible wallet, local cash-out services and reliable merchants can all affect whether a digital payment is useful. Transaction costs, local regulations and the stability of the payment infrastructure also matter.

The announcement does not identify the pilot's initial countries, launch date or detailed delivery arrangements. It also does not confirm that 1 million people have already received or are guaranteed to receive aid.

The key measure of success will be whether the programme can deliver assistance reliably and transparently to the people it is intended to support.

## Foundation Will Support Organisations Helping Human Trafficking Survivors

FASE also plans to support civil society organisations that provide assistance and protection to human trafficking survivors.

The initiative will work with the United Nations Trust Fund for Victims of Human Trafficking, according to Tether's announcement.

This part of the programme focuses on support for people affected by exploitation rather than on cryptocurrency adoption itself. The foundation has not yet publicly detailed the initial recipient organisations, the amount of funding committed or the number of survivors expected to benefit.

Those details will be important for evaluating the programme's reach and effectiveness.

FASE also says it will build on existing cooperation between Tether and UNODC to expand cybercrime-prevention activities across Africa, Asia and Central America.

The announcement presents these efforts as part of a broader financial-safety and inclusion strategy. Specific outcomes will need to be assessed as programmes are implemented and results become available.

## Why Is Tether Involved in Financial Inclusion?

Tether is best known for issuing USDT, a stablecoin widely used in cryptocurrency trading and digital payments.

Stablecoins can provide access to digital transfers and dollar-linked value in some circumstances, but access is not universal. Users may still face restrictions involving local laws, banking services, wallet providers, internet access and conversion into local currency.

FASE gives Tether and its partners a formal structure for coordinating initiatives that extend beyond the direct operation of cryptocurrency markets.

For the broader industry, the launch also demonstrates how blockchain companies are seeking partnerships with nonprofits and international organisations to address social and financial challenges.

Still, the foundation's stated mission should be separated from evidence of actual impact. The existence of a nonprofit, a list of partners and ambitious reach targets do not independently prove that a programme will be effective.

Public information about funding, implementation and independently measurable results will be necessary to judge the initiative fairly.

## What FASE Could Mean for the Crypto Industry

The launch reflects a growing effort among digital-asset companies to demonstrate practical uses for their technology beyond trading and investment.

Stablecoins may offer useful payment options in certain humanitarian settings, while blockchain analytics and digital education can contribute to efforts against financial crime. Yet these tools work best when combined with effective institutions, safeguards and services that meet local needs.

For FASE, the next stage will be more important than the announcement itself. Clear programme schedules, transparent funding information, reliable partnerships and independently measurable results would help establish whether its ambitions translate into meaningful outcomes.

The foundation has brought a notable group of crypto and technology organisations into a common initiative with UNODC as a strategic partner. Its real significance will ultimately depend on what those participants deliver.

For now, FASE is a newly launched initiative with ambitious plans—not proof that millions of people have already benefited from its programmes.
