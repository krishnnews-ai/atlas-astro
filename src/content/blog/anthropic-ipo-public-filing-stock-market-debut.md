The company behind Claude is approaching one of the biggest tests in its history. Anthropic is reportedly preparing to make its initial public offering (IPO) registration filing public on October 26, a move that could bring the artificial intelligence company one step closer to a stock market debut expected as early as November.

According to reporting by Bloomberg and other financial news outlets, Anthropic planned to publicly file its IPO paperwork at around 9 a.m. Eastern Time on Monday. The company is reportedly targeting an offering that could rival or exceed the scale of SpaceX's record-setting public market debut. However, the final offering size, valuation, share price and listing date have not been confirmed.

The planned filing matters because it could give public-market investors a clearer look at the business behind Claude, including its financial performance, infrastructure commitments and the risks involved in building advanced AI systems.

For Anthropic, the challenge is no longer simply convincing customers to use its AI products. It is demonstrating that the business can support the enormous costs of developing and operating increasingly powerful models while competing in a crowded market.

## Why Anthropic's IPO Could Become a Landmark Market Event

Anthropic has emerged as one of the major companies competing to build and commercialise advanced AI systems. Its Claude family of models is used for tasks ranging from software development and research to enterprise productivity and automated workflows.

A public listing would give investors a new way to gain exposure to the commercial AI industry through a company whose products are already competing for business customers.

The timing is particularly important. AI companies have attracted enormous investment, but public-market investors are increasingly asking whether the revenue generated by AI services can justify the costs of computing infrastructure, model development and long-term capacity commitments.

Anthropic's IPO could therefore become a test of investor confidence in the economics of frontier AI—not simply another technology stock debut.

Bloomberg previously reported that Anthropic was aiming to begin formal IPO marketing as early as the week of November 9, potentially allowing shares to start trading before Thanksgiving on November 26. That schedule remains subject to change.

The company's public registration filing, if released as planned, would be an important step in that process. It would not, by itself, mean that the IPO had been completed or that shares were already available to trade.

## What Does the Reported $75 Billion Figure Mean?

Reports comparing Anthropic's ambitions with SpaceX have attracted attention because of the extraordinary scale associated with the expected offering.

However, investors should distinguish between the amount a company raises in an IPO, the valuation assigned to the business and the total market value of its shares after listing. These are different financial measures.

The comparison with SpaceX reflects expectations for a potentially historic offering, but Anthropic's final deal size and valuation remain unconfirmed. The reported ambition should not be interpreted as a guaranteed $75 billion fundraising total.

Before the offering can be assessed properly, investors will need to see the final registration documents, including the number of shares offered, the proposed price range and the ownership structure.

Until those details become available, comparisons with previous record-breaking IPOs remain an indication of the potential scale—not confirmation of the final outcome.

## Anthropic's Financial Performance Will Be Under the Microscope

One of the most important consequences of a public filing would be greater visibility into Anthropic's financial position.

Reuters reported in September that it had reviewed confidential IPO materials containing information about the company's financial performance. According to that reporting, Anthropic generated approximately $4.6 billion in revenue in 2025, while recording a substantial net loss.

Other financial reports have highlighted the company's rapid revenue growth alongside its significant spending commitments for computing capacity and infrastructure.

Those figures need to be interpreted carefully. Revenue growth can demonstrate strong customer demand, but it does not automatically establish profitability. Similarly, a reported net loss can include accounting effects that do not directly reflect day-to-day operating performance.

The public registration statement should help investors examine the details more closely, including the company's revenue sources, expenses, risk factors, share structure and financial obligations.

The central question will be whether Anthropic can turn growing demand for Claude into a sustainable business while continuing to invest heavily in new AI models.

**Revenue growth is only one part of the story. Investors will also want to know how much it costs to generate that revenue and how much additional capital the company may need.**

## The AI Infrastructure Bill Could Be Just as Important as Revenue

Developing advanced AI models requires significant computing resources. Training, serving and improving those models can involve large commitments to specialised chips, cloud infrastructure, data centres and energy.

Anthropic operates in a market where technological competition can require continued investment even when a company is already attracting customers.

That creates a difficult balance. Spending too little could weaken a company's ability to compete, while spending too aggressively could put pressure on margins and cash requirements.

Anthropic's relationships with major technology and cloud providers are therefore an important part of the business. The terms of those relationships, the scale of future infrastructure commitments and the company's ability to use computing resources efficiently could all affect its long-term financial outlook.

A public prospectus would give investors an opportunity to examine the risks disclosed by the company rather than relying only on headline revenue figures or private-market valuation estimates.

The broader AI sector is also facing questions about whether the revenue generated by AI applications will ultimately justify the substantial cost of developing and operating them. Anthropic's offering could become an important test of how public investors value that trade-off.

## Anthropic Is Not Entering an Empty Market

Anthropic faces competition from established technology companies, specialist AI developers and open-source models that can provide alternative ways for businesses to deploy AI.

Its ability to retain enterprise customers, attract developers and deliver useful model improvements will be central to its long-term prospects.

Claude has gained attention in areas such as coding and professional workflows, but competitive advantages in AI can change quickly. New models, lower prices, improved tools and changes in customer preferences can all influence demand.

The company must also manage the safety and reliability concerns associated with increasingly capable AI systems. Those issues can affect customer trust, regulatory scrutiny and the pace at which organisations adopt AI tools.

For public investors, the challenge will be to determine whether Anthropic's current position can translate into durable advantages rather than a temporary lead in a rapidly changing industry.

## What Happens After the Public Filing?

If Anthropic publishes its registration statement as planned, several details will become especially important.

- **IPO price range:** The proposed price range will help establish the initial valuation sought by the company.
- **Shares offered:** The number of shares being sold will clarify the size of the offering.
- **Financial disclosures:** Investors will be able to examine reported revenue, losses, expenses and other financial information.
- **Ownership and voting rights:** The share structure will help explain how control is distributed between public shareholders and existing stakeholders.
- **Risk factors:** The prospectus should describe material risks involving competition, infrastructure costs, regulation, AI safety and business execution.
- **Expected timetable:** The filing and subsequent updates may clarify the path towards a potential November listing.

None of these steps guarantees that the IPO will proceed on the original timetable. Market conditions, regulatory review and the company's own decisions could still affect the schedule.

A public filing is an important milestone, but it is not the same as final IPO pricing or the start of stock trading.

## Can Retail Investors Buy Anthropic Shares Immediately?

Not necessarily.

Even if Anthropic makes its IPO filing public on October 26, ordinary investors should not assume that shares will become available immediately. The company must progress through the relevant registration and offering process before the public listing can take place.

The final prospectus and official listing announcements should establish the timetable and relevant details. Until then, any claimed ticker, confirmed trading date or guaranteed access to IPO shares should be treated cautiously unless supported by reliable official information.

Investors should also be wary of websites and social media accounts claiming to offer early Anthropic shares in exchange for cryptocurrency deposits, wallet connections or upfront payments. A reported IPO does not make unofficial offers legitimate.

## The Bigger Question: Can the AI Boom Deliver Public-Market Returns?

Anthropic's planned IPO arrives at a moment when investors are trying to separate long-term AI opportunities from the financial risks of building the technology.

A successful debut could strengthen confidence in the commercial AI market and create a new benchmark for valuing companies developing advanced models. A weaker reception, however, could highlight investor concerns about high costs, uncertain profitability and the enormous investment required to remain competitive.

Neither outcome is predetermined. Strong customer demand can coexist with large expenses, while substantial infrastructure spending can create future opportunities without guaranteeing financial success.

The public filing, if released as planned, should provide a more concrete basis for evaluating those questions than private-market estimates or speculation about a record-breaking valuation.

For now, Anthropic's reported IPO preparations represent a major development to watch across the technology and financial markets. The key event is not simply whether the company can attract a headline-grabbing valuation, but whether the documents show a business model that can sustain growth in one of the world's most expensive and competitive industries.
