A major traditional investment fund has taken another step towards blockchain-based finance. Aviva Investors, the asset management arm of British insurance group Aviva, has launched a tokenised share class of its Sterling Liquidity Fund on the XRP Ledger (XRPL), expanding its partnership with Ripple.

The move brings blockchain technology into a fund with approximately £22 billion in assets under management. However, there is an important distinction behind the headline: the entire £22 billion fund has not been moved onto the blockchain. Aviva has introduced a new digital share class and invested £50 million in that offering.

The launch, announced on October 26, 2026, follows the introduction of a tokenised share class for Aviva Investors' US Dollar Liquidity Fund in July. Across its two tokenised share classes, the firm now reports more than £86 million in investment.

The development highlights a growing institutional interest in representing conventional financial assets on public blockchain networks. It also raises a more practical question: can tokenisation make investment funds easier to distribute and manage without changing the underlying investments or removing existing regulatory requirements?

## What Exactly Has Aviva Launched on XRP Ledger?

Aviva Investors has introduced a tokenised share class of its existing Sterling Liquidity Fund. In simple terms, eligible investors can access their fund holdings in a digital format, with blockchain records supporting the representation and management of those shares.

The fund itself remains a conventional regulated money market fund. It invests in high-grade, short-term debt instruments denominated in British pounds and is designed to provide daily liquidity while targeting low-risk returns.

The new arrangement uses what Aviva describes as a "digital twin" model. Rather than replacing the existing fund with a cryptocurrency, the structure adds a blockchain-based access layer to the established investment product.

This distinction matters. The token represents an interest in a fund; it is not the same as buying XRP, and it does not mean that the fund has converted its underlying portfolio into cryptocurrency.

Aviva Investors said the tokenised share class maintains the conventional fund's investment objective, risk profile, liquidity characteristics and regulatory protections. Investors must still meet the applicable eligibility requirements to access it.

The original Sterling Liquidity Fund dates back to 2002 and is domiciled in Ireland under the UCITS framework for investment funds.

## The £22 Billion Headline Has an Important Catch

The size of Aviva's existing fund has attracted attention, but the figures need to be separated carefully.

| Figure | What it represents |
|---|---|
| Approximately £22 billion | Assets under management in the existing Sterling Liquidity Fund |
| £50 million | Aviva Investors' investment in the new tokenised sterling share class |
| More than £86 million | Combined investment across Aviva's two tokenised share classes |

These figures come from Aviva Investors' October 26 announcement.

The £22 billion figure describes the entire existing fund, not the value of assets newly issued or transferred onto the XRP Ledger. The £50 million investment is the amount Aviva disclosed for the new tokenised share class.

That difference is central to understanding the development. The launch is significant because it extends blockchain-based access to a major established investment product, not because the whole fund has suddenly become an on-chain asset.

The distinction also provides a useful measure of where institutional tokenisation currently stands. Large financial firms can begin introducing digital share classes while the majority of their conventional investment operations continue to function through existing systems.

## Why Is Aviva Working With Ripple?

Aviva Investors and Ripple announced their tokenisation partnership in February 2026. The stated objective was to explore ways of bringing traditional investment funds onto the XRP Ledger.

The sterling launch represents the second tokenised fund share class under that initiative. The first was the US Dollar Liquidity Fund share class, launched in July.

For Ripple, the partnership is another example of its effort to position the XRP Ledger as infrastructure for institutional financial applications, including the issuance and management of tokenised assets.

The network supports transactions recorded on a public blockchain, allowing digital representations of assets to be issued and managed through on-chain processes. Ripple says the network's transaction costs, speed and energy efficiency make it suitable for such applications.

For Aviva Investors, the potential benefits are more operational than speculative. Tokenisation could help improve how fund shares are recorded, distributed and transferred, depending on how the product is implemented and how investors access it.

However, the launch does not by itself establish that every transaction will settle instantly, that investors will be able to trade at all hours, or that the product will be available to everyone with an XRP Ledger wallet.

Those outcomes depend on the fund's legal structure, operating arrangements, eligible participants and applicable rules.

## Who Can Actually Invest in the Tokenised Fund?

The tokenised sterling share class is available to eligible investors using digital wallets, according to Aviva Investors.

That does not mean it is an unrestricted retail product. A publicly accessible blockchain can support digital records while the financial product represented by those records remains subject to investor eligibility requirements.

In other words, having a wallet address is not sufficient on its own to establish the right to invest.

Potential investors must still consider the fund's distribution rules, jurisdictional restrictions and any account approval or identity checks required by the providers involved.

This is an important point in the wider tokenisation debate. Blockchain technology can make digital asset records accessible across a network, but it does not automatically remove the legal requirements attached to securities and investment funds.

Aviva's announcement does not establish that the tokenised sterling share class is available to all retail investors globally, nor does it announce an unrestricted public exchange listing for the fund's shares.

## BNY Mellon, Komainu and Licuido Support the Structure

The tokenised share class involves several financial and technology providers.

BNY Mellon remains the custodian of the fund's underlying assets. Komainu, an institutional digital asset custodian, supports the tokenised offering, while Licuido provides digital infrastructure designed to help asset managers issue and manage tokenised real-world assets.

The division of responsibilities illustrates how institutional tokenisation can combine conventional financial services with blockchain infrastructure.

The underlying portfolio continues to operate within the existing fund structure, while the digital share class uses blockchain-based records. Custody and infrastructure arrangements help connect those two layers.

For financial institutions, this hybrid model can provide a way to experiment with digital distribution without immediately replacing their entire operating framework.

The long-term benefits will depend on whether these arrangements reduce administrative friction, improve investor access or make fund operations more efficient in practice.

## What Does This Mean for XRP?

The announcement is relevant to the XRP Ledger because it demonstrates another institutional use case for the network. However, the distinction between network adoption and demand for the XRP token should not be overlooked.

The tokenised fund shares are represented on XRPL, but that does not mean investors are buying XRP as their investment exposure. The underlying product remains a sterling money market fund.

Nor does the announcement establish that the launch will produce a specific amount of new demand for XRP. Any effect on the token would depend on the network's actual use, transaction activity and how the relevant infrastructure operates.

For XRP holders, the development is best understood as a signal of institutional engagement with the ledger rather than proof of an imminent price increase.

That is a more useful way to assess adoption: examine what the product does, who uses it and whether usage grows over time, rather than assuming every institutional blockchain announcement automatically translates into higher token prices.

## Tokenised Money Market Funds Are Becoming a Bigger Institutional Trend

Aviva's launch is part of a wider movement towards tokenising traditional financial assets.

Money market funds are a natural area for experimentation because they hold short-term financial instruments and are often used for cash management. Tokenised share classes can introduce a digital representation of ownership while the fund continues to invest through conventional markets.

Other major asset managers have also explored tokenised funds, including BlackRock and Franklin Templeton. These initiatives use different products and blockchain infrastructure, but share a broader objective: connecting established financial assets with digital systems.

The potential advantages include more automated recordkeeping, new distribution channels and improved integration with blockchain-based financial services. Yet tokenisation does not remove investment risk, eliminate the need for custodians or guarantee greater liquidity.

A tokenised share can still be subject to restrictions on transfers and ownership. The underlying fund's investment strategy and risk profile also remain important, regardless of how ownership is recorded.

For institutional investors, the test will be whether tokenisation delivers measurable operational benefits and attracts sustained demand.

## What Happens Next?

Aviva Investors has indicated that it intends to continue exploring tokenisation across additional funds and asset classes, although money market funds will remain an initial focus.

The next developments to watch include whether the firm introduces more tokenised share classes, whether the amount invested in its existing offerings grows, and whether the new distribution model attracts a broader group of eligible investors.

It will also be important to see how tokenised funds interact with other financial infrastructure, including custody systems, settlement processes and regulated investment platforms.

For Ripple, the partnership provides another opportunity to demonstrate how the XRP Ledger can support institutional applications beyond cryptocurrency trading.

For investors, however, the key question is not simply whether more assets appear on a blockchain. It is whether the new structure improves access or efficiency while preserving the safeguards associated with the underlying financial product.

Aviva's sterling launch is a concrete step in that direction, but its long-term significance will depend on adoption, operating results and further product development.

**The bigger story is not that £22 billion has moved into XRP. It is that a major asset manager is expanding blockchain-based access to an established investment fund—and testing whether tokenisation can become a practical part of mainstream finance.**
