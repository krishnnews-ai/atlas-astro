One of Wall Street's biggest institutions is moving closer to bringing stock markets onto blockchain infrastructure.

The New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) plans to use the Avalanche blockchain exclusively for settling trades on its upcoming tokenized securities platform, according to a November 9 report by *The Wall Street Journal*.

The development could give Avalanche a significant role in the infrastructure supporting tokenized U.S. stocks and exchange-traded funds (ETFs).

For the cryptocurrency industry, the bigger story is not simply another partnership. It is the growing connection between traditional financial markets and blockchain networks.

---

## Why Did NYSE Choose Avalanche?

NYSE reportedly evaluated multiple blockchain providers before selecting Avalanche for its planned settlement infrastructure.

According to the WSJ report, NYSE Chief Product Officer Kevin Tyrrell pointed to Avalanche's transaction speed, throughput and ability to support controlled environments with compliance requirements.

These capabilities matter because traditional securities markets operate under strict requirements involving transaction processing, ownership records, settlement and regulatory oversight.

Avalanche's customizable blockchain infrastructure could help financial institutions build systems that combine blockchain settlement with institutional controls.

The selection follows approximately a year of testing and discussions between NYSE and Ava Labs, the company behind Avalanche.

---

## NYSE Wants Stock Trading to Run 24/7

The Avalanche development is part of a much larger project.

In January 2026, NYSE parent company Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) announced plans to develop a digital platform for trading tokenized U.S. equities and ETFs.

The proposed platform aims to introduce several major changes:

- **24/7 trading:** Investors could trade eligible tokenized securities beyond traditional stock market hours.
- **Instant settlement:** Blockchain infrastructure could reduce the time needed to complete transactions.
- **Stablecoin funding:** Transactions could use dollar-pegged digital currencies.
- **Fractional ownership:** Investors could potentially purchase shares in dollar-denominated amounts rather than whole shares.

The platform is designed to combine NYSE's existing Pillar trading technology with blockchain-based settlement infrastructure.

Its launch remains subject to regulatory approval, with the latest report pointing to a potential fourth-quarter 2026 rollout.

---

## What Does This Mean for AVAX?

For Avalanche, the development represents a major institutional validation opportunity.

NYSE is one of the world's most recognized stock exchanges. Its potential use of Avalanche for tokenized securities settlement could strengthen the network's position in the rapidly expanding real-world asset (RWA) market.

Tokenization allows traditional assets, including stocks, bonds and funds, to be represented digitally on blockchain networks.

This market has attracted growing interest from major financial institutions because blockchain technology could improve how assets are transferred, recorded and managed.

However, there is an important distinction for investors: NYSE's reported selection of Avalanche does not automatically mean every transaction will create direct buying demand for the AVAX token. The exact settlement design, fee structure and institutional arrangements will determine how much economic value flows to the token.

---

## Wall Street's Blockchain Race Is Accelerating

NYSE is not the only major financial institution exploring tokenized securities.

Other market infrastructure companies are also developing blockchain-based systems to modernize traditional financial markets.

The race involves more than cryptocurrency exchanges. Banks, asset managers, transfer agents and securities infrastructure providers are increasingly exploring ways to bring financial assets onchain.

NYSE's growing network of partners includes Securitize, tZERO, Broadridge, Computershare, Equiniti and Superstate.

Together, these developments point toward a financial system in which traditional securities and blockchain infrastructure increasingly operate alongside each other.

---

## The Bigger Picture

The potential NYSE-Avalanche integration could become an important milestone for institutional blockchain adoption.

Bitcoin introduced a new form of digital money. Ethereum expanded the possibilities of programmable finance. Now, blockchain networks such as Avalanche are competing for a role in the infrastructure of traditional capital markets.

If the NYSE platform receives regulatory approval and launches as planned, Avalanche could become part of the technology powering a new generation of tokenized stock trading.

**Wall Street spent decades building the world's largest financial markets. Now, some of its biggest institutions are looking to blockchain to rebuild how those markets settle transactions.**

## The Bottom Line

NYSE's reported selection of Avalanche for tokenized stock settlement is a major validation for blockchain infrastructure in traditional finance.

If approved, the platform could bring 24/7 trading, instant settlement and fractional ownership to U.S. equities and ETFs.

But for AVAX investors, the key question is how much economic value actually flows to the token — not just whether the partnership makes headlines.

The bigger story: Wall Street is no longer just watching blockchain. It is starting to build on it.
