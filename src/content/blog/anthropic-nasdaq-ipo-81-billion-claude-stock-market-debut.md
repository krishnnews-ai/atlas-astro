**Anthropic has just delivered one of the biggest moments in the history of the artificial intelligence industry.**

The company behind Claude, one of the world's most closely watched AI assistants, has officially entered the public stock market, and Wall Street wasted no time reacting.

On November 19, 2026, Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol **ANT**. Its initial public offering raised approximately $81.3 billion, making it the largest IPO on record and putting the company in the middle of an extraordinary financial milestone.

But the opening day was even more dramatic.

Anthropic shares started trading at $111.50, significantly above the $83 IPO price. The stock briefly climbed as high as $114.99 before investors began taking profits. By the end of the session, shares closed at $97.17, still representing a gain of approximately 17.1% from the offering price.

For a company that built its reputation through artificial intelligence rather than traditional consumer products, this was a remarkable entrance into the world of public markets.

And the message from investors was difficult to ignore: Wall Street wants exposure to the next generation of AI companies.

---

## Anthropic's $81.3 Billion IPO Has Changed the Conversation on Wall Street

The scale of Anthropic's public offering is what makes this event particularly important.

The company initially indicated an IPO price range of $70 to $78 per share. However, strong investor demand allowed it to increase the final price to $83.

Anthropic ultimately offered approximately 980 million shares, raising around $81.3 billion through the public offering.

The company also arranged a separate $26 billion private placement. Together, these transactions brought the overall fundraising total to approximately $107.3 billion.

That puts Anthropic's market debut ahead of the record-setting SpaceX IPO earlier in 2026, which raised approximately $75 billion.

For the technology industry, the comparison is significant. A company built around AI models and software has now demonstrated that investors are willing to commit enormous amounts of capital to the future of artificial intelligence.

---

## Claude's Popularity Is Giving Investors a Reason to Pay Attention

Anthropic is not simply another technology company trying to benefit from the AI boom.

Its Claude family of AI models has become an important part of the company's business, particularly among developers and businesses looking for advanced AI tools.

Claude Code has also attracted considerable attention because it helps developers work with software, understand codebases and automate parts of programming workflows.

Businesses are increasingly experimenting with AI systems that can do more than answer questions. They want tools capable of completing complicated tasks, assisting employees and improving productivity.

That shift has created a significant commercial opportunity for Anthropic.

According to the company's IPO disclosures, it had approximately 11,900 customers generating at least $100,000 in annual revenue over the trailing 12-month period as of September 30, 2026.

That figure highlights how deeply AI tools are entering the corporate market.

For investors, the important question is whether Anthropic can turn that growing demand into sustainable profits.

## The Stock Jumped 34% at the Opening, Then Reality Set In

Anthropic's first trading session offered a clear picture of how excited investors are about AI, but it also showed how quickly sentiment can change.

The stock opened at $111.50, roughly 34% above its $83 offering price. It later reached an intraday high of $114.99.

At those levels, the company's market value briefly moved beyond $2 trillion on a fully diluted basis, according to market reports.

However, the rally did not continue at the same pace throughout the session.

Some investors decided to lock in their early gains, bringing the share price down to $97.17 by the closing bell.

Even after that pullback, Anthropic finished its first day approximately 17.1% above its IPO price.

There is another interesting detail here: the broader US stock market was under pressure during the same session. The Nasdaq fell around 2.3%, while the Dow Jones and S&P 500 also recorded significant declines.

Anthropic's positive debut despite the wider market weakness made its performance particularly notable.

But the first day should not be treated as proof that the stock will continue rising. IPO shares can experience substantial volatility as the market works out what investors are actually willing to pay.

## Behind the Excitement, Anthropic Has a Massive Financial Challenge

The market celebration does not mean Anthropic has solved every financial problem.

Building and operating advanced AI models requires enormous computing resources. Powerful chips, data centres, electricity, networking equipment and cloud infrastructure all come with significant costs.

Anthropic's financial disclosures reveal just how expensive this business can become.

The company spent nearly $9 billion on computing capacity and infrastructure during the first half of 2026, according to Reuters' reporting on its IPO disclosures.

It also reported an additional $5 billion in computing costs during the third quarter.

The company has made computing commitments approaching $900 billion with infrastructure partners, including Amazon and Google, over the coming years.

These commitments highlight the enormous scale of the infrastructure required to compete in the global AI market.

The opportunity is huge, but so is the financial responsibility.

Anthropic will need to continue attracting customers, increasing revenue and managing infrastructure expenses if it wants to justify the expectations surrounding its stock.

## Why Anthropic's IPO Could Matter Beyond the Stock Market

The significance of this listing extends well beyond one company's share price.

For years, advanced AI companies were primarily valued through private funding rounds. Their valuations were determined by private investors, venture capital firms and strategic technology partners.

A Nasdaq listing changes that equation.

Anthropic will now face the expectations of public-market investors, who will closely examine its financial performance, customer growth, operating expenses and long-term business strategy.

It also creates a major reference point for other AI companies considering public listings.

Investors will be watching whether Anthropic can maintain its valuation after the excitement surrounding its IPO fades.

The listing may also influence how the market values other businesses involved in AI infrastructure, chips, cloud computing and enterprise software.

In other words, Anthropic's public-market debut could become an important test of how much investors believe the AI industry is worth.

## The Biggest Question: Can Anthropic Turn AI Demand Into Long-Term Profits?

Anthropic has already demonstrated that it can attract extraordinary investor interest.

Its $81.3 billion IPO, strong opening price and 17% first-day gain show that the appetite for major AI businesses remains substantial.

However, the next stage will be considerably more difficult.

Public investors will want to see consistent revenue growth, stronger financial discipline and evidence that the company's massive infrastructure investments can produce meaningful returns.

Competition is another major challenge.

Anthropic operates in a market where companies are racing to build more capable AI systems, attract business customers and secure access to computing resources.

The company must keep improving Claude while managing the cost of staying competitive.

That is why its IPO should be viewed as the beginning of a new chapter rather than the final victory.
