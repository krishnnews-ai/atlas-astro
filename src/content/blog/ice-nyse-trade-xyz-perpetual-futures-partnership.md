Wall Street's push into crypto-style trading is gathering pace. Intercontinental Exchange (ICE), which owns the New York Stock Exchange, is partnering with Trade.xyz to bring perpetual futures products into regulated U.S. markets, according to a Bloomberg report highlighted by Tree News on October 29.

The reported move could mark another step toward bringing financial products popular on crypto trading platforms into the U.S. regulatory system.

## Why Perpetual Futures Matter

Unlike conventional futures contracts, perpetual futures have no fixed expiration date. Traders can maintain positions for as long as they meet the contract's margin requirements, with funding mechanisms typically helping keep prices aligned with the underlying market.

These contracts have become popular in crypto markets because they allow traders to speculate on price movements without directly holding the underlying asset. Depending on the product, they can also involve significant leverage and the risk of rapid losses.

## ICE Is Expanding Beyond Traditional Exchanges

ICE operates major financial-market infrastructure, including the NYSE. It has also been expanding its digital-asset strategy through partnerships and investments, including its previously announced strategic relationship with crypto exchange OKX.

Trade.xyz, meanwhile, is associated with perpetual derivatives linked to traditional financial assets on blockchain-based trading infrastructure. In March 2026, S&P Dow Jones Indices licensed the S&P 500 to Trade[XYZ] for a perpetual contract on Hyperliquid, demonstrating how established financial benchmarks are reaching crypto-native trading venues.

The reported ICE partnership could extend this convergence, although the exact products, launch timeline and regulatory structure require further confirmation.

## What Happens Next?

The key questions are which assets the proposed contracts will track, which U.S. customers will be eligible to trade them and how the products will operate within the regulatory framework.

If the partnership proceeds as reported, it would highlight a broader trend: traditional financial companies are exploring ways to combine established market infrastructure with trading formats developed in crypto markets.

**The takeaway:** ICE's reported move with Trade.xyz could bring perpetual futures closer to regulated U.S. markets. The significance will depend on the final product design, regulatory approvals and the assets made available to traders.
