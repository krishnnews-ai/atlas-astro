Polygon Labs has completed its acquisition of Coinme, adding regulated US money-transmission infrastructure to its Polygon Open Money Stack and strengthening its push into stablecoin-based payments for businesses .

Announced on October 14, the completed acquisition brings Coinme's US payment infrastructure into Polygon's broader platform, which combines fiat on-ramps and off-ramps, digital wallets, cross-chain payment routing and blockchain settlement. The company says its US payments business now has coverage across 48 states through 37 state money transmitter licenses and 11 applicable exemptions. It is also registered with the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) as a money services business .

The development marks a significant step in Polygon's strategy to move beyond blockchain infrastructure and provide businesses with integrated tools for transferring money between traditional financial systems and blockchain-based assets .

Polygon also identified Tazapay, Mesh, Toku and Kira among the first customers adopting Open Money Stack for use cases spanning international business payments, account funding, global payroll and access to US dollar payment services .

The announcement follows Polygon's January 2026 agreement to acquire Coinme and wallet infrastructure firm Sequence as part of a broader effort to build an integrated stablecoin payments business .

## What Polygon's Coinme Acquisition Brings to Open Money Stack

Coinme gives Polygon access to established US money-transmission infrastructure, connecting traditional payment methods with digital assets .

For businesses building payment products, the challenge is often not simply transferring value on a blockchain. They also need ways to accept dollars, onboard customers, manage wallets, satisfy compliance requirements and deliver funds to recipients through usable payment channels .

Polygon Open Money Stack is designed to bring several of these functions together through a single integration .

Its infrastructure includes:

- **Fiat on-ramps and off-ramps:** Tools connecting traditional currencies with supported digital assets.
- **Wallet infrastructure:** Systems for holding and moving funds.
- **Cross-chain routing:** Technology for directing transactions across supported blockchain networks.
- **Settlement infrastructure:** Blockchain-based rails for transferring value.
- **Banking and payout connections:** Infrastructure supporting the movement of money into and out of existing financial systems.

The objective is to reduce the number of separate providers and integrations that businesses need to manage when building stablecoin payment services .

For example, a fintech company processing an international payment may need to collect money from one party, convert it into a supported stablecoin, transfer the value and deliver the funds to a recipient in local currency. Open Money Stack aims to connect these stages within a more unified system .

Actual availability, settlement times, costs and supported payment routes depend on the relevant product configuration, partners and jurisdictions .

## Polygon Reports Coverage Across 48 US States

One of the most important elements of the acquisition is Coinme's licensing infrastructure .

Polygon says its US payments business has coverage across 48 states, comprising 37 state money transmitter licenses and 11 applicable exemptions. The business is also registered with FinCEN as a money services business .

These figures describe different parts of the regulatory framework and should not be interpreted as 48 identical licenses. State licensing requirements and exemptions vary according to applicable law and the activities being conducted .

FinCEN registration is also distinct from state money transmitter licensing. Registration as a money services business does not, by itself, constitute a general endorsement of a company or replace other applicable state and federal obligations .

For payment providers, this infrastructure can be important because moving money between bank accounts, fiat currencies and digital assets involves regulatory and operational requirements beyond blockchain transaction processing .

Polygon's acquisition therefore adds more than another crypto product. It brings an existing payments business and regulatory infrastructure into the company's broader stablecoin strategy .

However, coverage across 48 states does not mean every service is available in every state or that every business can access all features without additional checks. Companies using the platform must assess their own regulatory obligations and the precise permissions applicable to their activities .

## Tazapay, Mesh, Toku and Kira Become Early Customers

Polygon's announcement also identifies four early customers, illustrating how the infrastructure can support different types of financial services .

### Tazapay: Cross-border business payments

Tazapay provides payment infrastructure for businesses, marketplaces and fintech companies operating across international markets .

The company is using Open Money Stack's on- and off-ramp and wallet infrastructure to connect stablecoins with local currencies. This can provide another route for collecting, holding and paying out funds across markets alongside the traditional payment channels Tazapay already uses .

The potential benefit is a more direct connection between blockchain-based settlement and local-currency payment services, although actual costs and delivery times will depend on the payment corridor and the services involved .

### Mesh: Funding digital-asset payments

Mesh connects wallets, cryptocurrency exchanges and financial services through its digital-asset payments network .

Its integration with Open Money Stack adds another on-ramp option, enabling participating fintechs and payment platforms to expand how users fund accounts from existing wallets and exchanges .

The integration is intended to reduce the need for businesses to build separate connections to numerous digital-asset providers .

### Toku: Global payroll and stablecoin payouts

Toku provides global payroll and employer-of-record services, helping businesses employ and pay workers across jurisdictions .

Through Open Money Stack, Toku is connecting stablecoin balances with compliant payroll and local-currency payout processes. Its offering combines payment infrastructure with employment-related requirements, including tax withholding, benefits and local filings .

This is relevant to companies operating internationally that want to use stablecoins for parts of their payment operations without treating blockchain transfers as a substitute for local employment and payroll compliance .

### Kira: US dollar access and banking connections

Kira uses AI agents to help fintechs, over-the-counter trading desks and import businesses access US banking and stablecoin services .

Its integration with Open Money Stack is intended to expand the stablecoin options available to clients using US dollar accounts and payment channels .

Kira's offering combines account-related workflows, payment tracking and compliance processes with connections to traditional banking infrastructure. Human review remains part of the company's described decision-making process .

Together, these integrations demonstrate that Polygon's payments strategy is aimed at business infrastructure rather than only consumer cryptocurrency trading .

## Why Polygon Is Focusing on Stablecoin Payments

Stablecoins are digital assets designed to maintain a relatively stable value against a reference asset, commonly the US dollar. They are used for cryptocurrency trading, blockchain settlement and certain cross-border payment applications .

Their usefulness in payments, however, depends on more than the blockchain itself .

A business may still need access to banking systems, identity checks, custody arrangements, local payout networks and the ability to convert funds into a currency a recipient can use .

These requirements can create friction when companies assemble a payment service from multiple providers .

Polygon's Open Money Stack is intended to bring more of those functions into one integrated offering. By combining Coinme's regulated US money-movement infrastructure with wallet, routing and settlement capabilities, Polygon aims to make it easier for businesses to build payment products using stablecoins .

The model could be relevant to remittance providers, payroll companies, marketplaces, fintech applications and businesses managing international supplier payments .

Nevertheless, stablecoin payments are not automatically faster or cheaper in every circumstance. Network fees, conversion spreads, banking cut-off times, compliance reviews and local payment infrastructure can all affect the final result .

The benefits therefore need to be assessed against the specific payment route and the needs of each business .

## How the Acquisition Fits Polygon's Broader Strategy

Polygon announced definitive agreements to acquire Coinme and Sequence in January 2026 .

Coinme brings licensed US money-movement capabilities, while Sequence contributes wallet infrastructure and technology designed to simplify on-chain transactions. Together with Polygon's blockchain and payment-routing infrastructure, these businesses form the foundation of Open Money Stack .

Completing the Coinme acquisition moves that strategy forward by bringing the regulated US payments business into Polygon's operating structure .

The company is positioning itself as a payments infrastructure provider that can generate business from actual money movement, rather than relying exclusively on activity associated with blockchain infrastructure .

That shift places greater emphasis on enterprise customers, transaction flows, payment integrations and the practical requirements of businesses moving money across borders .

It also puts Polygon in a competitive market that includes specialist stablecoin payment companies, fintech providers, banking infrastructure businesses and other blockchain networks seeking enterprise payment activity .

The acquisition alone does not establish how quickly Polygon can grow this business. Its longer-term performance will depend on customer adoption, transaction volumes, pricing, operating costs and the ability to maintain regulatory compliance across supported markets .

## Important Regulatory Context Following Coinme's Settlement

The acquisition announcement comes shortly after a separate regulatory development involving Coinme .

On October 8, 2026, the Georgia Department of Banking and Finance announced a consent order involving Coinme. The order followed a coordinated effort by 34 state financial regulatory agencies and addressed concerns about compliance with state and federal laws applicable to money transmitters .

According to the regulators, Coinme agreed to pay a $2.5 million settlement and terminate its virtual-currency kiosk operations by January 1, 2027, under the terms of the order. Coinme was also required to retain an independent consultant to review its Bank Secrecy Act and anti-money-laundering compliance program .

The order stated that Coinme could continue providing other money-transmission services to Georgia residents .

This distinction matters when assessing the acquisition. The regulatory action concerned Coinme's compliance and virtual-currency kiosk operations; it should not automatically be treated as a determination that every service within Polygon Open Money Stack is prohibited or unavailable .

At the same time, the settlement is material context for a business strategy built around regulated payments. Compliance controls, monitoring and regulatory obligations remain important as Polygon integrates Coinme and expands its services .

The early customer integrations also provide examples of how the platform may be used in practice, from cross-border payments to payroll and dollar access .

Adoption will depend on whether the infrastructure meets the requirements of businesses and regulators, whether customers receive a clear operational benefit, and whether the service can scale reliably .
