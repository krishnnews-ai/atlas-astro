Hong Kong is moving closer to bringing crypto transactions into its international tax-reporting system. A major legislative development reported on November 4 could require crypto-asset service providers with a Hong Kong reporting connection to collect customer and transaction information from 2027, with cross-border information exchanges expected to begin in 2028.

The development matters because Hong Kong is aligning its crypto reporting rules with the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development's (OECD) Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework, better known as CARF.

Wu Blockchain reported on November 4 that Hong Kong's Legislative Council had passed the *Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework and Amended Common Reporting Standard) Bill 2026*. However, the government's publicly available pages reviewed for this article had not yet reflected a final passage confirmation. The legislative outcome should therefore be treated as reported rather than independently confirmed.

---

## What Hong Kong's New Crypto Reporting Rules Could Mean

The proposed framework is designed to bring crypto-asset transactions into the international system used by tax authorities to exchange financial information.

Under CARF, qualifying crypto-asset service providers would have due-diligence and reporting responsibilities. These can include collecting customer identification details, determining tax residency and recording relevant crypto transactions.

The rules are not limited to Bitcoin. Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Department describes relevant crypto-assets broadly, covering digital representations of value that use cryptographically secured distributed ledger technology and can be traded or transferred.

The framework covers reportable exchanges between crypto-assets and fiat currencies, exchanges between different crypto-assets, and certain transfers of crypto-assets.

That means the change could affect more than conventional cryptocurrency purchases and sales. Transfers involving external wallet addresses may also fall within the reporting framework, depending on the applicable rules and reporting requirements [citation:1].

## The Timeline: 2027 for Collection, 2028 for Information Sharing

The proposed implementation schedule is one of the most important parts of the development.

| Date | Expected development |
|---|---|
| January 1, 2027 | CARF reporting and due-diligence requirements begin for qualifying providers |
| 2028 | Hong Kong plans to begin automatic exchanges of crypto-related tax information with participating jurisdictions |
| January 1, 2028 | Amended Common Reporting Standard provisions are expected to take effect |
| 2029 | First automatic exchanges under the amended CRS are planned |

The CARF and amended CRS timelines are separate. CARF focuses on crypto-asset transaction information, while the amended CRS updates the existing framework for exchanging financial-account information.

The international exchanges will depend on the relevant legal arrangements and participating jurisdictions meeting the necessary requirements.

## Which Crypto Platforms Could Be Affected?

The framework focuses on reporting crypto-asset service providers that have the required connection, or nexus, with Hong Kong.

This can include businesses that facilitate crypto-to-fiat exchanges, crypto-to-crypto transactions or operate trading platforms for customers.

The rules also establish due-diligence responsibilities. Providers may need to obtain customer self-certifications and use available documentation, including relevant anti-money-laundering and know-your-customer records, to determine a customer's tax-residency status.

For existing customers, providers may also need to obtain updated tax-residency information within the prescribed period.

Importantly, this does not mean every individual crypto holder must directly submit transaction reports to Hong Kong's tax authority. The reporting obligations described under CARF primarily fall on qualifying service providers [citation:1].

## What Information Could Be Shared?

The framework is intended to give tax authorities a clearer picture of reportable crypto activity.

Depending on the applicable reporting rules, information can include:

- Customer names and addresses.
- Tax-residency jurisdictions.
- Taxpayer identification numbers, where applicable.
- Relevant crypto-asset transaction values.
- The number of units acquired, disposed of or transferred.
- The number and type of reportable transactions.

Reporting providers would submit the required information to the relevant tax authority, which could then exchange it automatically with eligible partner jurisdictions.

Hong Kong has said that international exchanges will operate with appropriate partners and subject to data confidentiality and security requirements.

## Does This Mean Hong Kong Is Introducing a New Crypto Tax?

No. The distinction is important.

CARF is a tax-transparency and information-reporting framework. It is designed to help tax authorities identify reportable crypto activity and improve cross-border tax cooperation.

It does not, by itself, introduce a new cryptocurrency tax rate or establish that every crypto transaction is taxable.

Whether an individual owes tax depends on the tax laws and circumstances applicable to that person. Reporting information and calculating tax liability are two different processes.

For crypto investors, the practical implication is that relevant transactions may become more visible to tax authorities through information collected by regulated or otherwise qualifying service providers.

## Why Hong Kong Is Adopting the OECD Framework

The OECD introduced CARF in 2023 to extend international tax transparency standards to crypto-assets.

Traditional financial institutions already operate under international information-exchange arrangements. CARF is intended to address the growing use of digital assets by establishing a comparable system for collecting and exchanging relevant crypto transaction information.

Hong Kong's Financial Services and the Treasury Secretary, Christopher Hui, has previously described the initiative as part of the city's efforts to strengthen tax transparency and combat cross-border tax evasion.

The move also reflects a wider international trend: governments are increasingly seeking consistent reporting standards for digital assets rather than relying exclusively on conventional banking disclosures.

## What Crypto Investors and Exchanges Should Watch Next

For crypto businesses operating in or connected to Hong Kong, preparation could involve reviewing customer onboarding procedures, tax-residency checks, transaction records and reporting systems.

Investors may also be asked by relevant service providers to provide additional tax-residency information or update existing customer details.

The next important steps are the publication of final implementation guidance, confirmation of the legislation's enactment and clarification of the jurisdictions that will participate in automatic information exchanges.

The practical impact will depend on how the rules are implemented and how widely international partners participate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When will Hong Kong start collecting crypto transaction information?

The CARF timetable provides for reporting and due-diligence requirements to begin from January 1, 2027 for qualifying crypto-asset service providers with a Hong Kong reporting connection.

### When will Hong Kong share crypto tax information internationally?

Hong Kong has planned to begin automatic exchanges under CARF in 2028 with relevant partner jurisdictions, subject to the necessary legal and implementation arrangements.

### Will Bitcoin transactions be included?

Bitcoin can fall within CARF's definition of relevant crypto-assets. Reportability depends on the transaction, the service provider and the applicable rules.

### Does CARF mean crypto investors will pay a new tax?

No. CARF is an information-reporting framework, not a new tax rate. Individual tax obligations continue to depend on applicable tax laws.

### Will every self-custody wallet be reported?

The framework primarily places reporting and due-diligence obligations on qualifying service providers. It does not mean that every self-custody wallet automatically reports directly to the tax authority.

## The Bigger Picture

Hong Kong's CARF initiative is part of a broader shift toward international transparency in digital-asset markets. As reporting systems develop, crypto platforms may face greater compliance responsibilities, while tax authorities could gain access to more consistent information about cross-border crypto activity.

For investors, the key dates to watch are 2027 and 2028. For the industry, the immediate priority is understanding the final legislation and preparing systems before the reporting requirements take effect.
