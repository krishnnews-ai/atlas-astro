For years, Kalyan Krishnamurthy was one of the most important faces behind Flipkart's growth. He helped steer the company through fierce competition, a massive Walmart acquisition and the rapid expansion of India's online shopping market.

Now, his journey as Flipkart's CEO is coming to an end.

On November 25, 2026, Flipkart officially announced that Krishnamurthy would conclude his tenure, marking a major leadership transition at one of India's biggest e-commerce companies.

But the timing is interesting.

The company is still working toward its long-awaited stock market debut, competition in online retail is getting tougher, and Walmart remains focused on Flipkart's long-term growth in India.

So, what happens to Flipkart now?

---

## The Man Who Led Flipkart Through Its Biggest Transformation

Krishnamurthy's association with Flipkart began in 2013. After a period at investment firm Tiger Global, he returned to the company in 2016.

He became CEO in January 2017 and later took over as group CEO in 2018.

His leadership coincided with one of the most important moments in Indian e-commerce history.

In 2018, Walmart acquired a controlling stake in Flipkart in a deal valued at approximately $16 billion.

The acquisition brought Flipkart under the ownership of one of the world's largest retailers and gave it significant financial backing to compete in India's rapidly expanding digital market.

According to Flipkart, its business grew more than fivefold during Krishnamurthy's leadership.

The company also credited him with expanding its reach, strengthening its seller ecosystem and turning The Big Billion Days into a major event in India's festive shopping season.

---

## Why Is He Leaving Now?

This is where the story becomes particularly interesting.

Flipkart has confirmed Krishnamurthy's departure, but the company has not publicly disclosed a specific reason for the decision.

However, a report by The Economic Times linked his exit to differences with Walmart over the company's IPO plans and a potential liquidity event for employees and senior executives.

The report said discussions over these issues had broken down.

Flipkart has not officially confirmed that these disagreements caused the leadership change.

The distinction matters because the company's official announcement focused on Krishnamurthy's contribution and the business's future rather than explaining the circumstances behind his departure.

---

## The $38 Billion Question: When Will Flipkart Go Public?

For years, Flipkart's potential IPO has been one of the most closely watched developments in India's startup industry.

A public listing could give investors an opportunity to realise returns on their holdings and allow the company to access public capital markets.

But the process has not moved according to earlier expectations.

The Economic Times previously reported that Flipkart had put its IPO plans on hold. Earlier expectations included filing draft documents with India's market regulator, SEBI, in late 2026 or early 2027.

For employees holding company stock options, a delayed listing also means waiting longer for a potential liquidity opportunity.

However, there is no confirmed IPO date at present.

And Krishnamurthy's departure does not automatically mean the IPO has been cancelled.

---

## Who Will Run Flipkart Now?

Flipkart has confirmed that Balaji Thiagarajan, its current Chief Product and Technology Officer, will lead the organisation alongside the company's board on an interim basis.

The company says it will announce a permanent successor in the coming weeks.

This appointment will be important because Flipkart is entering a period where technology, customer experience and operational efficiency could play an even bigger role in its growth.

The next CEO will inherit a business with more than 500 million registered users and over 1.4 million sellers, according to company figures.

That is a massive responsibility.

---

## Amazon Is Not the Only Challenge Anymore

Flipkart's competitive environment has changed dramatically.

Amazon remains a major rival, but the company is also facing pressure from newer players changing how Indians shop online.

Quick-commerce platforms such as Blinkit, Zepto and Swiggy Instamart have made rapid deliveries a major part of consumer expectations.

Flipkart has responded with its own quick-commerce offering, Flipkart Minutes.

At the same time, value-focused platforms such as Meesho are competing aggressively for customers who are particularly sensitive to prices.

The next leadership team will have to decide how Flipkart balances traditional e-commerce, faster deliveries, technology investments and profitability.

---

## Walmart Is Still Betting on India's Future

Despite the leadership change, Walmart has publicly reaffirmed its commitment to Flipkart and its long-term opportunity in India.

That is an important signal.

India remains one of the world's most attractive digital commerce markets, with a huge consumer base and growing adoption of online shopping.

Flipkart also operates a wider business ecosystem that includes Myntra, Flipkart Wholesale, Cleartrip and super.money.

A new CEO will therefore be taking charge of much more than an online shopping website.

The challenge will be to turn this large ecosystem into a stronger and more sustainable business.

---

## What Happens Next?

Krishnamurthy's departure closes an important chapter in Flipkart's history, but the company's bigger story is far from over.

The immediate focus will be on three things: finding a permanent CEO, strengthening its position against competitors and bringing greater clarity to its long-awaited IPO ambitions.

For customers, the Flipkart experience may continue as usual.

For investors, employees and the wider startup industry, however, the coming months could be far more significant.

**The man who helped take Flipkart through its Walmart era is leaving. Now the big question is whether the company's next leader can take it all the way to the stock market.**

---

## The Bottom Line

Kalyan Krishnamurthy is stepping down as Flipkart's CEO after leading the company through its $16 billion Walmart acquisition and massive growth.

His exit comes amid reports of disagreements with Walmart over IPO plans and employee liquidity.

Balaji Thiagarajan will lead the company on an interim basis while Flipkart searches for a permanent CEO.

The company still faces questions about its IPO timeline, growing competition from quick-commerce platforms, and its next chapter under Walmart's ownership.
