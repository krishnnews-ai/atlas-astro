Who are the 500 Muslims having the greatest influence on the world today? A new international publication has brought together some of the most recognised names in global politics, religion, business, sports and culture.

The 2027 edition of *The Muslim 500: The World's 500 Most Influential Muslims* has been released, putting influential personalities from across the Muslim world and beyond under the spotlight.

Among the prominent names featured are Pakistan's Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif, Field Marshal Asim Munir, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan and Saudi King Salman.

But the list goes far beyond presidents, military leaders and royal families. Football superstar Mohamed Salah, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and prominent religious scholars are also featured.

Published by the Royal Islamic Strategic Studies Centre (RISSC) in Jordan, the annual publication examines individuals whose work, positions and public influence have an impact on Muslim communities around the world.

---

## Five Major Leaders From Three Countries Feature in a Historic Joint Recognition

One of the biggest talking points in the 2027 edition is the recognition of five leaders from Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Türkiye.

The names highlighted together are:

- King Salman bin Abdul-Aziz Al-Saud – Saudi Arabia
- Mohammed bin Salman – Saudi Arabia
- Shehbaz Sharif – Pakistan
- Field Marshal Asim Munir – Pakistan
- Recep Tayyip Erdoğan – Türkiye

The publication connects this recognition to the Makkah Joint Defence Agreement, signed on August 7, 2026.

The agreement represents an important development in security cooperation between the three countries.

According to the publication, the partnership brings together Saudi Arabia's financial strength, Türkiye's defence technology and Pakistan's military capabilities.

The development has attracted attention because it reflects changing diplomatic and security relationships across the Muslim world.

## Why Is Zohran Mamdani Included?

One name that may surprise international readers is Zohran Mamdani.

The New York City mayor has attracted considerable attention in American politics and public life.

His inclusion reflects the growing visibility of Muslim public figures in major international cities.

Mamdani's presence also demonstrates that the publication is not limited to leaders from Muslim-majority countries.

It considers influence across geographical boundaries, including individuals whose work affects Muslim communities in Europe and North America.

## The Other 450: Scholars, Business Leaders, Scientists and Public Figures

The publication contains 500 individuals, but they are not all presented as one numbered ranking.

The first 50 receive individual rankings, while the remaining 450 are organised into categories according to their areas of influence.

These 13 categories cover:

1. Scholarly
2. Political
3. Administration of Religious Affairs
4. Preachers and Spiritual Guides
5. Philanthropy, Charity and Development
6. Social Issues
7. Business
8. Science and Technology
9. Arts and Culture
10. Qur'an Reciters
11. Media
12. Influencers and Sports
13. Extremists

This structure brings together people from very different backgrounds, including religious educators, entrepreneurs, scientists, artists, journalists, athletes and humanitarian workers.

The complete publication also includes Honourable Mentions, recognising additional individuals for their contributions.

## How Does The Muslim 500 Select Its Names?

The publication says its selection process combines social indicators, public opinion, expert assessments and the achievements of individuals over their lifetimes.

It also considers people whose influence may be particularly important within their own communities, even if they are not internationally famous.

This means the publication is not simply measuring wealth, political office or social media popularity.

Its stated approach also considers long-term contributions, leadership and the ability to influence society.

Importantly, being included does not mean that every individual shares the same political, religious or social views.
