Wall Street spent years betting that artificial intelligence would become one of the biggest investment opportunities in financial history. Billions of dollars poured into chipmakers, cloud infrastructure, data centres and companies developing increasingly powerful AI systems.

But something is changing.

Some of the world's biggest institutional investors are beginning to question whether technology companies can continue spending at the extraordinary pace that helped fuel the AI boom.

Bank of America's latest fund manager survey has revealed one of the sharpest changes in investor positioning in nearly two decades. Fund managers reduced their net overweight exposure to technology stocks from 37% in October to just 12% in November.

According to the November 17 report, this was the biggest monthly reduction since June 2006.

The development comes as concerns grow about whether major cloud companies will eventually slow their artificial intelligence infrastructure investments.

A record proportion of surveyed investors also believe companies are spending too much on capital expenditure.

This does not mean the AI industry is collapsing or that investors have abandoned technology stocks. But it does reveal a major change in confidence.

And for companies supplying the chips, servers and infrastructure behind the AI revolution, that shift could become increasingly important.

## Wall Street Just Made Its Biggest Technology Retreat Since 2006

The most important signal from Bank of America's November fund manager survey is the dramatic decline in technology positioning.

In October, fund managers held a net 37% overweight position in technology stocks.

By November, that figure had fallen to 12%.

That represents a decline of 25 percentage points in just one month.

It is the sharpest monthly reduction in technology exposure since June 2006, highlighting how quickly institutional sentiment has changed.

However, there is an important distinction.

The figures do not mean investors sold 25% of their technology shares. They measure the difference between investors who are overweight and those who are underweight the technology sector.

In other words, fund managers still favour technology overall, but their conviction has weakened significantly.

This is particularly interesting because the AI industry continues to attract enormous investment.

The concern is no longer simply whether artificial intelligence will transform the global economy.

Investors increasingly want to know whether the financial returns will justify the enormous cost of building it.

## 52% of Fund Managers Expect Big Cloud Companies to Cut AI Spending

One of the most concerning findings involves expectations for future capital expenditure.

According to Bank of America's November survey, 52% of fund managers expect at least one major AI hyperscaler to announce a reduction in capital spending within the next six months.

That figure was just 30% in October.

The rapid increase suggests that investors are becoming less confident about the sustainability of the current AI infrastructure spending cycle.

Major cloud companies have been investing heavily in computing infrastructure to support AI model training, inference and enterprise applications.

These investments require enormous amounts of capital.

They include advanced processors, high-performance servers, networking equipment, electricity infrastructure and specialised data-centre facilities.

The problem for investors is that spending more money does not automatically guarantee higher profits.

Technology companies must eventually demonstrate that AI services can generate enough revenue to justify their infrastructure costs.

If companies begin slowing their expansion plans, suppliers across the AI ecosystem could feel the consequences.

## Record 38% Say Companies Are Spending Too Much on AI

Another major warning comes from fund managers' assessment of corporate investment decisions.

A record net 38% of respondents believe companies are overinvesting.

This is particularly significant because the AI industry has been built around expectations of continuously expanding infrastructure demand.

For several years, the market rewarded companies that announced larger data-centre investments and ambitious AI expansion plans.

But investors are now asking whether the industry is building capacity faster than customers can generate profitable demand.

There are several reasons behind these concerns.

First, advanced AI infrastructure is expensive to build and operate.

Second, companies need to convert AI adoption into sustainable revenue.

Third, competition is increasing, making it harder for individual businesses to maintain pricing power.

If infrastructure spending continues rising faster than AI-related revenue, investors could begin questioning the returns on those investments.

That does not mean AI demand has disappeared.

It means the financial market is becoming more selective about which companies can turn AI investment into actual profits.

## Only 16% Want Companies to Increase Capital Spending

Perhaps the clearest sign of changing investor sentiment is the declining appetite for additional corporate investment.

Only 16% of fund managers want companies to increase capital expenditure, the lowest reading since May 2020.
