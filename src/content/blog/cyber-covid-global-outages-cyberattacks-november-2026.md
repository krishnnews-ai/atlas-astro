A week of unexpected technology failures and cybersecurity incidents has raised a serious question: how safe is the digital infrastructure the world depends on every day?

Between November 12 and November 21, major disruptions affected Microsoft, US aviation systems, JPMorgan Chase, United Airlines, Apple and several other organizations. Meanwhile, separate cyber incidents involving financial companies and government systems have added to growing security concerns.

The unusual sequence has prompted some observers to describe the situation as a possible **'Cyber COVID' timeline**.

But there is an important distinction: authorities have not established that these incidents are connected or part of one coordinated attack.

---

## Microsoft Outage Was Just the Beginning

The trouble began on November 12, when Microsoft 365 experienced a major global disruption.

Users faced problems accessing Outlook and other services, affecting businesses and people who depend on Microsoft's cloud infrastructure.

The following day, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) ordered a nationwide ground stop in the United States, temporarily disrupting flight departures.

The incidents highlighted how quickly technical problems can affect services used by millions of people.

---

## Banks and Airlines Faced Another Wave of Problems

November 16 brought more disruption.

JPMorgan Chase experienced a software failure that delayed certain banking transactions. Customers of other banks, including Bank of America and Wells Fargo, also reported payment-related problems.

United Airlines separately experienced a system outage that delayed departing flights for approximately 90 minutes.

Apple services also suffered disruptions around November 17, affecting products and services including iCloud, Apple Pay and the App Store.

The National Security Agency (NSA) reported temporary degradation of its internal network during scheduled maintenance.

These incidents had different reported causes, but their timing attracted considerable attention.

---

## A Cyberattack Reached 178 Organizations Through Optimizely

One of the more serious cybersecurity incidents involved Optimizely's Web Experimentation platform.

Attackers exploited a vulnerability to inject malicious JavaScript into affected customer projects.

Security researchers identified 188 compromised scripts associated with 178 organizations.

The malicious code could expose website visitors to cryptocurrency wallet-address manipulation and payment-card information theft.

This incident demonstrated an important weakness in modern internet infrastructure: even when a company's own servers remain secure, compromised third-party software can put its website visitors at risk.

---

## JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard Disclose Separate Cyber Incidents

The concerns grew further on November 19.

JPMorgan Chase, Visa and Mastercard disclosed separate third-party security incidents involving information held by outside service providers.

JPMorgan said attackers may have accessed marketing information, potentially including customer names and email addresses.

Mastercard reported exposure of a limited number of email addresses, while Visa said its payment systems and payment data were not affected.

Importantly, these security incidents were separate from the earlier banking service disruptions.

---

## FBI Investigates a Suspected Breach of a Sensitive Database

Another worrying development emerged on November 21.

According to a Reuters report citing CNN, the FBI is investigating a suspected breach involving its National Crime Information Center (NCIC).

The database is used by law enforcement agencies across the United States to access information involving criminal histories, warrants and background checks.

The investigation is ongoing, and the suspected breach has not been independently confirmed by the FBI.

---

## Is This One Coordinated Cyberattack?

Despite the alarming sequence, there is currently no verified evidence that all these incidents are connected.

Some disruptions have been attributed to software bugs, technical failures, internal updates or scheduled maintenance. Other events involved genuine cybersecurity incidents.

The bigger concern is the growing dependence on interconnected digital infrastructure.

A failure at one technology provider can affect banks, airlines, businesses and millions of ordinary users.

For organizations, the latest incidents underline the importance of stronger cybersecurity, reliable backup systems and better protection of third-party software.

**The world may not be facing a 'Cyber COVID', but this extraordinary week is a reminder that digital infrastructure can be far more fragile than it appears.**

---

## The Bottom Line

A wave of outages and cyber incidents hit Microsoft, JPMorgan, Apple, airlines, and the FBI between November 12 and 21, 2026.

Authorities have not confirmed these events are connected or part of a coordinated attack.

The incidents highlight the fragility of global digital infrastructure and the growing risk from third-party software vulnerabilities.

For businesses and governments, the message is clear: stronger cybersecurity and backup systems are no longer optional.
