For more than a century, financial indices have helped investors understand what is happening across markets. Now, one of the companies behind the world's best-known benchmarks is exploring a new question: can the same idea be used to measure expectations about future events?

S&P Dow Jones Indices and prediction-market platform Kalshi announced a strategic partnership on October 26, 2026, bringing traditional financial benchmarks and event-contract data into a shared framework. The agreement will allow Kalshi to offer event contracts tied to S&P Dow Jones Indices benchmarks, while S&P Dow Jones Indices will use Kalshi's market data to help construct new indices.

The potential applications extend beyond stocks. The companies have identified economics, politics and sports as areas where new indices could help users track expectations about real-world outcomes.

The agreement does not mean these new indices are already available. The companies have not announced a complete product list, launch dates or detailed methodologies for the proposed benchmarks.

Nevertheless, the partnership signals an important development in financial data: prediction-market prices could become inputs for a new generation of indices designed to summarise what market participants expect to happen next.

## From the Dow Jones to the S&P 500: Why Indices Changed Investing

Financial indices transformed how investors measure markets.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dates back to 1896, while the S&P 500 was introduced in 1957. Instead of forcing investors to assess every company individually, these benchmarks offered a way to understand the broader performance of a selected group of stocks.

The concept eventually supported an extensive ecosystem of investment products. Index funds and exchange-traded funds made diversified exposure easier to access, while futures and options gave market participants additional ways to trade or hedge broad market movements.

The scale is substantial. More than $20 trillion is indexed or benchmarked to the S&P 500, according to the figures cited in the partnership discussion.

The proposed collaboration explores whether a similar framework can help people interpret a different kind of market information: prices reflecting expectations about future events.

Rather than measuring the performance of a portfolio of stocks, an event-based index could combine information from multiple prediction-market contracts into a single indicator. Depending on its methodology, that indicator could show how expectations change as new information becomes available.

It would not replace traditional stock indices. Instead, it could create another category of benchmark focused on the expectations reflected in event-contract markets.

## What Did S&P Dow Jones Indices and Kalshi Announce?

The agreement covers two main areas.

**1. Event contracts linked to established financial indices**

Kalshi becomes a preferred prediction-market platform licensed for event contracts tied to S&P Dow Jones Indices benchmarks. The forthcoming contracts will settle against official index values published by S&P Dow Jones Indices, according to the announcement.

This provides a defined reference point for determining contract outcomes. However, S&P Dow Jones Indices has explicitly stated that Kalshi's contracts based on its indices are not sponsored, sold or endorsed by the index provider.

**2. New indices built using prediction-market data**

S&P Dow Jones Indices will also become a preferred provider for constructing indices using Kalshi event-contract data.

The proposed approach combines Kalshi's market information with S&P Dow Jones Indices' experience in benchmark design. Potential areas include economic developments, political outcomes and sporting events.

The exact indices, calculation rules and release schedule have not yet been disclosed.

The distinction between these two initiatives is important. One connects event contracts to existing financial benchmarks. The other explores using prediction-market data to create entirely new indicators.

## How Could a Prediction-Market Index Work?

Imagine an indicator designed to track market expectations around a group of economic events.

A prediction market may offer contracts tied to specific outcomes. As participants trade those contracts, prices change in response to new information, revised expectations and trading activity.

An index methodology could combine prices from a defined group of contracts into a single measure. The resulting indicator could help analysts observe how expectations shift over time instead of following every contract separately.

The same general concept could be applied to selected political or sporting events, provided the underlying contracts and calculation methodology support a meaningful comparison.

These are potential applications, not confirmed index products announced by the companies.

Building a reliable index would require clear rules. Its creators would need to determine which contracts qualify, how much weight each receives, how prices are handled when trading is limited and what happens if a contract's outcome becomes disputed.

Those decisions matter because an index is only as useful as the data and methodology behind it.

S&P Dow Jones Indices brings experience in developing and maintaining benchmarks. The partnership creates an opportunity to apply that expertise to a new category of market data, although the resulting indices will need to demonstrate their usefulness in practice.

## Why Prediction Markets Are Attracting Financial Attention

Prediction markets allow participants to trade contracts tied to defined future events. Depending on the contract, its price can provide a signal about how participants assess the likelihood of a particular outcome.

For example, prices may change when economic data is released, a political development occurs or new information alters expectations about an event.

These markets can aggregate views from participants with different information, assumptions and incentives. That can make their prices useful to monitor alongside polls, surveys, economic forecasts and conventional financial data.

But a prediction-market price is not a guarantee of what will happen.

Prices can be affected by limited liquidity, trading costs, concentrated positions and the characteristics of individual contracts. A contract may also have settlement conditions that require careful interpretation.

An index built from several contracts could make the information easier to follow, but combining market prices does not automatically eliminate weaknesses in the underlying data.

The challenge for any new benchmark will be to turn those prices into a transparent and consistent measure that users can interpret correctly.

## Could Businesses Use These Indices to Understand Risk?

If the proposed benchmarks are developed successfully, they could offer another way for businesses and analysts to monitor changing expectations.

A company concerned about economic conditions might follow an indicator constructed from relevant event contracts. A broadcaster or advertiser could be interested in measures associated with specific sporting outcomes. Financial analysts might compare prediction-derived indicators with conventional market data.

These are illustrative use cases rather than confirmed products or services.

For an index to become useful, it would need to measure a clearly defined subject and provide information that users cannot easily obtain from existing sources. Its methodology would also need to be transparent enough for users to understand what the indicator represents.

The partnership could eventually help make prediction-market information more accessible in a familiar benchmark format. Whether that happens will depend on the products the companies develop and the demand they attract.

## What Does This Mean for Kalshi?

For Kalshi, the agreement creates a connection with an established financial benchmark provider.

Contracts tied to recognised indices can use official index values as their settlement reference, while the broader collaboration creates a potential route for Kalshi's market data to inform new financial indicators.

The partnership could also increase institutional interest in prediction-market data. Financial firms often require clearly defined methodologies and reliable reference information when assessing new products.

However, the agreement does not resolve every question surrounding prediction markets. Regulatory treatment, contract design, market integrity and consumer protection remain important considerations.

The companies will need to translate the agreement into clearly specified products before users can judge their practical value.

## The Risks: Expectations Are Not Certainties

Prediction-derived indices could be useful, but they also raise several challenges.

- **Liquidity:** Thinly traded contracts may produce prices that do not reflect a broad range of views.
- **Methodology:** Contract selection, weighting and calculation rules can materially affect an index's value.
- **Settlement:** Contracts require clear definitions of the outcomes being measured and how those outcomes are determined.
- **Regulation:** The rules governing event contracts can vary according to the product and jurisdiction.
- **Interpretation:** An index summarising market expectations should not be mistaken for a definitive forecast or an objective probability of what will happen.

Transparent methodology and reliable data will be essential if the proposed indices are to earn the confidence of financial institutions and other users.

## What Happens Next?

The next milestone is the release of concrete product details.

The companies have not yet specified which index-linked contracts will appear first, when the new indices will launch or how the proposed benchmarks will be calculated.

Users should watch for official announcements covering contract listings, index methodologies, data quality controls and the rules used to maintain the indicators over time.

Adoption will be another important test. Even a well-designed index needs to provide practical value to analysts, businesses or investors if it is to become widely used.

The partnership is therefore a starting point for a new category of financial information—not evidence that prediction-market indices will immediately become as influential as the S&P 500.

## The Bigger Picture: Financial Benchmarks May Expand Beyond Stocks

The collaboration between S&P Dow Jones Indices and Kalshi reflects an effort to apply established benchmark techniques to information generated by prediction markets.

Traditional indices help people understand the performance of financial assets. Prediction-derived indices could instead summarise expectations about defined future events.

If the new benchmarks are designed transparently and supported by reliable market data, they could provide additional context for understanding economic, political and sporting developments.

For now, the significance lies in the companies' agreement to explore this approach. The exact products and their commercial impact remain to be seen.
