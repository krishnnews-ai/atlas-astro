Traditional finance is taking another step toward blockchain-based investing. Janus Henderson has launched a tokenized fund linked to the Nasdaq-100 Index, bringing exposure to one of the best-known US equity benchmarks into an onchain investment structure.

The new product, called **JQQQ**, is managed by Janus Henderson and powered by blockchain tokenization infrastructure from Centrifuge. The fund is designed to track the Nasdaq-100, an index comprising 100 of the largest non-financial companies listed on the Nasdaq exchange.

The launch marks another development in the growing market for tokenized financial products. Instead of relying exclusively on conventional fund distribution and recordkeeping systems, tokenization represents fund interests through blockchain-based digital tokens.

However, the launch comes with important limitations. Initial access is intended for eligible non-US professional investors, with a reported minimum investment of $100,000. Janus Henderson's approximately $500 billion in assets under management refers to the asset manager's broader business—not the size of the new Nasdaq-100 fund.

## What Is the JQQQ Tokenized Nasdaq-100 Fund?

JQQQ is a tokenized investment fund designed to provide exposure to the Nasdaq-100 Index through blockchain-based infrastructure.

The index includes major technology and growth-oriented businesses, with constituents such as Nvidia, Apple and Tesla. Although technology companies play a significant role in the benchmark, the index is not limited to technology stocks.

The product combines traditional index-based investing with blockchain-enabled fund infrastructure. Janus Henderson brings investment management capabilities, while Centrifuge provides the technology used to bring the fund onchain.

According to reporting published on October 27, 2026, Janus Henderson secured a licence from Nasdaq to use the Nasdaq-100 Index for the product. The launch follows a broader expansion of index licensing beyond the arrangements that historically shaped access to Nasdaq-100 investment products.

The distinction is important: **JQQQ is a tokenized fund linked to the Nasdaq-100, not a new cryptocurrency representing direct ownership of all 100 companies.** Investors' rights depend on the fund's legal structure and offering documents.

## How Does Tokenization Change Fund Investing?

In a conventional investment fund, ownership records, subscriptions, transfers and other administrative processes are handled through established financial intermediaries and systems.

Tokenization represents eligible fund interests as digital tokens recorded on a blockchain. Depending on the product's structure, blockchain infrastructure can support ownership records, transfers and interactions with compatible financial applications.

The technology may offer several potential advantages:

- **Digital ownership records:** Blockchain-based records can help track tokenized fund interests.
- **More efficient settlement:** Programmable infrastructure may streamline some transfer and administrative processes.
- **Integration with digital finance:** Eligible tokenized assets may be designed to interact with compatible wallets and financial applications.
- **New distribution models:** Fund managers can explore blockchain-based channels alongside traditional investment infrastructure.

These benefits are not automatic. Actual transferability, settlement arrangements, redemption procedures and investor protections depend on the specific product and its governing documents.

Tokenization also does not eliminate the need for asset management, custody, compliance or regulatory oversight. Instead, it changes how certain parts of the investment process may be organised.

## Why Is the Nasdaq-100 Being Brought Onchain?

The Nasdaq-100 is a widely followed benchmark, making it a significant candidate for experiments involving tokenized investment products.

Its constituents include companies involved in artificial intelligence, cloud computing, consumer technology and digital infrastructure. Investors use the index to gain diversified exposure to a collection of large, Nasdaq-listed non-financial businesses.

By bringing a fund linked to the benchmark onchain, Janus Henderson is extending its tokenization strategy into equity-market exposure.

The move also reflects a broader shift in the financial industry. Asset managers are exploring whether blockchain technology can support investment products that traditionally depend on multiple layers of administration, distribution and settlement.

For investors already operating in digital-asset markets, tokenized funds may offer a way to access certain traditional financial strategies through blockchain-based infrastructure.

That does not necessarily mean the product is available to every crypto investor. Eligibility requirements and minimum investment thresholds can be substantial, and a tokenized fund may have restrictions that do not apply to ordinary publicly traded exchange-traded funds.

## Stellar and Centrifuge's Role

The fund is expected to use the Stellar blockchain for its initial onchain availability, according to reporting on the launch. Centrifuge is providing the tokenization infrastructure supporting the product.

These roles are distinct.

Janus Henderson is the investment manager behind the product, while Centrifuge supplies technology for tokenized funds. Stellar provides the blockchain network on which the tokenized product is initially expected to be available.

Using a blockchain does not change the underlying investment benchmark. The fund's exposure remains tied to its investment structure and the Nasdaq-100 Index, rather than to the price of Stellar's native cryptocurrency, XLM.

Investors should therefore distinguish between the performance of the fund, the performance of its underlying benchmark and the market price of any cryptocurrency associated with the blockchain infrastructure.

The launch should not, by itself, be interpreted as evidence that XLM will rise in value or that the fund will generate returns superior to conventional Nasdaq-100 investment products.

## Is JQQQ a $500 Billion Fund?

No. This is an important distinction in interpreting the announcement.

Janus Henderson oversees approximately $500 billion in total assets, according to the latest reporting. That figure describes the asset manager's broader business, not the amount invested in JQQQ.

The fund's own assets under management should be assessed separately as data becomes available.

The reported minimum investment of $100,000 also indicates that the initial product is aimed at eligible professional investors rather than being automatically accessible to ordinary retail investors. Availability and eligibility requirements should be confirmed against the official offering documents before anyone attempts to invest.

For that reason, describing the announcement as a $500 billion Nasdaq-100 fund launch would be inaccurate. The significance lies in the product's structure and the participation of a large traditional asset manager—not in an assumption that $500 billion has moved onto a blockchain.

## How Does This Compare With Other Tokenized Funds?

Janus Henderson has already participated in tokenized investment products, including funds linked to US Treasury bills and other credit strategies.

In September 2025, Janus Henderson, Anemoy and Centrifuge announced the Janus Henderson Anemoy S&P 500 Index Fund Token, known as SPXA. That product brought exposure to the S&P 500 into a tokenized fund structure.

The new Nasdaq-100-linked product extends that direction into another major US equity benchmark.

The wider industry is also exploring tokenized Treasury funds, credit products and other regulated financial instruments. The goal is to combine familiar investment strategies with infrastructure that can support digital ownership and blockchain-based financial applications.

Nevertheless, different tokenized funds are not interchangeable. Their underlying assets, fees, legal structures, investor eligibility rules and redemption arrangements may differ significantly.

Investors should review the individual fund documents rather than assuming that all tokenized products offer the same rights or level of liquidity.

## What Are the Risks for Investors?

Tokenization can modernise parts of financial infrastructure, but it does not remove investment risk.

**Market risk:** A fund tracking the Nasdaq-100 remains exposed to movements in the underlying equity market. If the index falls, the fund may also lose value.

**Liquidity risk:** A token's presence on a blockchain does not guarantee that it can be sold immediately at a fair price. Liquidity depends on the product's arrangements and available counterparties.

**Access restrictions:** The reported initial offering is intended for eligible non-US professional investors. Not every investor or jurisdiction will necessarily qualify.

**Technology and operational risk:** Blockchain networks, digital wallets, smart contracts and service providers can introduce technical and operational risks.

**Legal and ownership risk:** Holding a tokenized fund interest is not necessarily the same as holding the underlying shares directly. The legal rights attached to the token depend on the product's structure and documentation.

Investors should also distinguish between a fund that tracks an index and a token that merely claims to mirror the index's price. The legal and economic protections can be very different.

## What the Launch Means for the Future of Tokenized Finance

The launch of JQQQ adds another major equity benchmark to the developing tokenized-fund market.

For traditional asset managers, the opportunity is to explore blockchain-based distribution and administration while retaining established investment strategies. For digital-asset infrastructure providers, partnerships with large financial institutions can help demonstrate how tokenization may extend beyond stablecoins and crypto-native assets.

But the long-term impact will depend on adoption, costs, regulatory clarity, liquidity and investor protection—not simply on the number of products announced.

The key question is whether tokenized funds can provide practical advantages over existing investment channels while preserving the safeguards investors expect.

For now, Janus Henderson's Nasdaq-100-linked fund is a notable development in the convergence of traditional asset management and blockchain infrastructure. Its performance, accessibility and long-term adoption will become clearer as the product develops.
