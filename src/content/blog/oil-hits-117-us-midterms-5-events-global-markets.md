Global markets are heading into a politically and economically sensitive week. Oil prices have climbed to their highest levels in six months, the United States is preparing for midterm elections, and several major central banks are expected to make important interest-rate decisions.

Between November 2 and November 6, investors will be watching developments across energy markets, government policy, monetary policy and employment data. Each could influence expectations for inflation, interest rates and economic growth — with potential consequences for stocks, bonds, currencies and cryptocurrencies.

Brent crude futures settled at $117.64 a barrel on October 30, gaining 2.4% during the session. The benchmark also recorded a 13.6% increase over October, as concerns about the Iran conflict and risks to Middle East supply routes added pressure to energy markets.

At the same time, the US midterm elections could reshape the political balance in Congress, while rate decisions in Australia, Sweden and the United Kingdom will offer fresh signals about how central banks are responding to inflation.

Here are five developments investors will be watching closely this week.

## 1. Oil Surges to $117 as Middle East Supply Risks Return

Oil is entering November with a significant geopolitical risk premium.

Brent crude for December delivery settled at $117.64 per barrel on Friday, October 30, its highest level in roughly six months. US West Texas Intermediate crude also advanced, settling at $104.12.

The latest move followed renewed concerns about the security of energy infrastructure and shipping routes in the Middle East. Reuters reported that a tanker was struck by explosive devices in the Strait of Hormuz, while regional tensions and threats to oil infrastructure added to supply concerns.

The Strait of Hormuz is one of the world's most important energy shipping routes. Any sustained disruption could affect the movement of crude oil and increase costs for import-dependent economies.

The implications extend well beyond the energy sector.

Higher oil prices can raise transportation, manufacturing and electricity costs. If those increases persist, businesses may pass some of the additional expenses on to consumers, adding pressure to inflation.

That creates a difficult situation for central banks. Policymakers may have less room to reduce interest rates if energy costs keep inflation elevated.

**What investors should watch:** Any new developments involving Iran, the Strait of Hormuz, regional shipping and oil infrastructure. A further rise in crude could influence inflation expectations and market sentiment across multiple asset classes.

## 2. US Midterm Elections Put Congress and Economic Policy in Focus

The US midterm elections on Tuesday, November 3, will be another major event for financial markets.

All 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 Senate seats are being contested. The outcome will determine the balance of power in Congress and could affect the direction of fiscal policy, legislation, government spending and regulatory priorities.

For investors, the central question is not simply which party gains seats. It is how the election result could change the government's ability to pass economic and financial legislation.

Congress has an important role in decisions involving taxation, public spending, government borrowing and the federal debt limit. Changes in political control can influence expectations about those policies.

Markets may also respond to the possibility of divided government. If the presidency and Congress remain under different political control, major legislative changes could become more difficult to pass.

The impact may be felt across several sectors. Companies exposed to government contracts, financial regulation, energy policy and healthcare could attract particular attention as investors assess the implications.

Currency and bond markets will also be watching the result. Political uncertainty can affect expectations about future government borrowing and economic policy, although the market reaction will depend on the final composition of Congress and subsequent policy decisions.

**What investors should watch:** Results from competitive House and Senate races, the likely balance of power in Congress and any early indications of how the election could affect fiscal and regulatory policy.

## 3. Three Central Bank Decisions Could Change Interest-Rate Expectations

Monetary policy will be another major theme during the week, with decisions expected from the Reserve Bank of Australia, Sweden's Riksbank and the Bank of England.

The decisions come as policymakers face the challenge of balancing inflation risks against economic growth.

### Reserve Bank of Australia

The Reserve Bank of Australia is scheduled to announce its policy decision on Tuesday, November 3.

Markets are watching whether the central bank will raise interest rates again as it responds to inflation pressures. A rate increase would affect borrowing costs for households and businesses and could influence expectations for the Australian dollar.

Investors will pay close attention not only to the decision itself but also to the central bank's explanation of inflation and its outlook for future policy.

### Sweden's Riksbank

Sweden's Riksbank is scheduled to announce its decision on Wednesday, November 4.

The announcement will provide another indication of how European policymakers are assessing inflation and economic conditions. Investors will examine the policy statement for clues about whether further tightening could be considered.

### Bank of England

The Bank of England's decision is scheduled for Thursday, November 5.

Markets widely expect the central bank to raise interest rates by 25 basis points to 4.00%, according to recent market reporting. If delivered, the move would mark a significant shift in the UK's monetary-policy direction.

However, the accompanying statement and voting breakdown could matter just as much as the rate decision. Investors will want to understand whether policymakers see further increases as necessary or whether they expect to proceed more cautiously.

**What investors should watch:** The size of any rate changes, voting patterns, inflation assessments and guidance on future policy. Differences between central banks could also influence currency movements and international capital flows.

## 4. US Jobs Report Could Influence the Federal Reserve's Next Move

Friday's US employment report will provide one of the week's most important economic signals.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics is scheduled to release October nonfarm payrolls data on November 6. Market expectations cited in the week-ahead outlook point to approximately 55,000 new jobs, compared with 29,000 in September. The unemployment rate is expected to remain around 4.2%.

The report will help investors assess whether the US labour market is stabilising or continuing to lose momentum.

A stronger-than-expected employment figure could reinforce concerns that the economy remains resilient enough to withstand tighter monetary policy. If inflation also remains elevated, markets may reconsider expectations for the Federal Reserve's next interest-rate decision.

A weaker report could produce a different reaction. It may increase concerns about slowing economic activity and strengthen expectations that policymakers will avoid additional tightening.

Wage growth will also matter. Faster increases in average earnings can support household spending but may add to concerns about services inflation.

The employment figures will arrive after the US elections and several central bank decisions, making the timing particularly important for investors assessing the broader economic outlook.

**What investors should watch:** Nonfarm payrolls, the unemployment rate, average hourly earnings and any revisions to previous employment figures.

## 5. Major Corporate Earnings Could Test Investor Confidence

Alongside political and economic developments, investors will be assessing results from major companies.

Apple, Palantir, AMD and Circle are among the companies highlighted in the week's earnings calendar. Their results and management commentary could provide useful information about consumer demand, artificial intelligence spending, semiconductor markets and the digital-asset economy.

Technology earnings are especially important as investors continue to evaluate whether heavy spending on artificial intelligence is translating into sustainable business growth.

AMD's results and outlook will be watched for signals about demand for data-centre chips and competition in the AI hardware market. Palantir's performance could attract attention around enterprise and government demand for AI-related software.

Apple's results may offer insight into consumer spending and demand for its products. Meanwhile, Circle's earnings could provide additional information about the business environment for stablecoins and digital-asset infrastructure.

Investors will be looking beyond headline revenue and profit figures. Forward guidance, operating margins, customer demand and capital expenditure plans could all influence market reactions.

**What investors should watch:** Company guidance, AI-related spending, semiconductor demand, stablecoin business developments and signs of weakness or resilience in consumer activity.

## Why This Week Matters for Bitcoin and the Crypto Market

Although the week's biggest events are centred on traditional financial markets, their effects could extend into cryptocurrencies.

Bitcoin and other digital assets are sensitive to changes in liquidity, interest-rate expectations and broader investor risk appetite.

If oil prices remain elevated, inflation concerns could intensify. That may make central banks more cautious about easing monetary policy, potentially limiting the liquidity conditions that have supported risk assets.

The US midterm elections could also influence expectations around financial regulation and digital-asset legislation. However, the implications will depend on the election results and any policy action that follows.

The employment report is another important variable. Stronger labour-market data could strengthen expectations for tighter monetary policy, while weaker figures may raise concerns about economic growth.

None of these developments automatically determines Bitcoin's direction. Crypto markets have their own drivers, including institutional flows, exchange activity, regulation and developments within blockchain networks.

Still, traders will be watching the relationship between Bitcoin, the US dollar, Treasury yields and broader equity-market sentiment throughout the week.

## The Bigger Picture: Inflation, Politics and Interest Rates

The main theme connecting this week's events is inflation.

Higher oil prices could make it harder for central banks to bring inflation under control. At the same time, employment figures and economic surveys will help policymakers assess whether higher borrowing costs are slowing demand.

The US elections add another layer of uncertainty because changes in Congress could affect fiscal policy and the regulatory environment.

For investors, the challenge will be separating short-term market reactions from developments that could change the longer-term economic outlook.

Oil prices, central-bank decisions, employment data and corporate guidance will each provide a different piece of the picture. Taken together, they could make the first week of November an important test for global financial markets.
