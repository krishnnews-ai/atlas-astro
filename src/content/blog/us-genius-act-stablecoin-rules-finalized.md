The United States has taken another significant step toward establishing a formal regulatory framework for payment stablecoins. On October 21, the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) announced a final rule implementing key provisions of the Guiding and Establishing National Innovation for U.S. Stablecoins Act, widely known as the GENIUS Act.

The rule establishes requirements for permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers that fall under the OCC's jurisdiction. It also addresses certain custody activities conducted by OCC-supervised entities, bringing greater regulatory clarity to an industry that connects cryptocurrency markets with traditional financial services.

However, there is an important distinction between finalizing the OCC's rule and completing every regulation required under the GENIUS Act. The OCC confirmed that anti-money-laundering, Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions-related requirements will be addressed through separate rulemaking in coordination with the US Treasury Department.

The final rule is scheduled to take effect on January 18, 2027. That date gives stablecoin issuers a defined compliance milestone as the United States moves toward implementing its new payment-stablecoin framework.

**Key points at a glance**

- The OCC announced its final GENIUS Act implementation rule on October 21, 2026.
- The rule covers payment-stablecoin issuers and certain custody activities under OCC jurisdiction.
- Reserve backing, redemption and risk management are central areas of the regulatory framework.
- The GENIUS Act establishes a requirement for permitted issuers to maintain identifiable reserves at a minimum one-to-one ratio.
- The final rule is scheduled to become effective on January 18, 2027.
- Separate rulemaking remains necessary for certain anti-money-laundering and sanctions provisions.

## What Is the GENIUS Act?

The GENIUS Act is a US federal law designed to establish a regulatory framework for payment stablecoins. President Donald Trump signed the legislation into law on July 18, 2025.

Payment stablecoins are digital assets designed to maintain a relatively stable value and be used for payments. Many are pegged to the US dollar, although a stablecoin's stated peg does not guarantee that it will always trade at exactly one dollar.

Stablecoins play an important role in cryptocurrency markets. Traders use them to move between digital assets, exchanges rely on them for settlement, and payment companies are exploring their use for transfers and other financial services.

Before a comprehensive federal framework was introduced, stablecoin issuers operated within a complicated environment involving different state-level requirements and federal rules applicable to particular activities or institutions.

The GENIUS Act aims to establish clearer requirements for eligible issuers, including reserve backing, redemption, supervision and financial crime compliance. The law also addresses how federal and state regulatory frameworks interact.

The White House said when the legislation was signed that it was intended to strengthen consumer protections and support responsible innovation in digital finance.

The OCC's October 21 announcement represents a further implementation milestone. It does not mean that every stablecoin product, cryptocurrency exchange or digital asset company is automatically approved under the new framework.

## What Does the OCC's Final Stablecoin Rule Cover?

The OCC, which supervises national banks and certain other financial institutions, announced the final rule after receiving hundreds of comments on its earlier proposal.

According to the agency, the rule establishes requirements for permitted payment-stablecoin issuers and foreign payment-stablecoin issuers under its jurisdiction. It also addresses certain custody activities performed by OCC-supervised entities.

The rule focuses on several areas that matter directly to issuers and the institutions providing services to them.

### 1. Reserve Backing and Financial Safeguards

Reserve backing is one of the most important elements of the GENIUS Act.

Under the law, permitted payment-stablecoin issuers must maintain identifiable reserves backing their outstanding payment stablecoins at a minimum one-to-one ratio. The permitted reserve assets include specified forms of US currency, eligible deposits, short-term US Treasury securities and certain other qualifying assets.

The objective is to ensure that an issuer has qualifying assets corresponding to its outstanding payment-stablecoin obligations.

For example, an issuer with $1 billion in outstanding payment stablecoins would generally need at least $1 billion in qualifying reserves under the statutory requirement.

That requirement does not mean every cryptocurrency token is covered. The GENIUS Act specifically concerns payment stablecoins that meet its legal definition and are issued within the framework it establishes.

Reserve requirements can improve transparency and financial discipline, but they cannot eliminate every risk. Users still need to consider an issuer's financial position, redemption procedures, operational resilience and the legal protections applicable to their holdings.

### 2. Redemption and Risk Management

A stablecoin's usefulness depends partly on whether holders can redeem it under the issuer's applicable terms.

The OCC identified redemption and risk management as important elements of its final regulatory framework. Issuers and relevant supervised entities will need to pay attention to the requirements applicable to their activities.

Effective risk management matters because stablecoin operations involve more than holding reserve assets. Issuers must also manage liquidity, operational disruptions, custody arrangements and other risks associated with maintaining and redeeming digital tokens.

The precise obligations applicable to an individual company will depend on its regulatory status, activities and the provisions governing its operations.

### 3. Federal Oversight and Issuer Approval

The GENIUS Act establishes an approval framework for entities issuing payment stablecoins in the United States.

According to the OCC's October 21 guidance, the general requirement for an issuer to be approved as a permitted payment-stablecoin issuer begins on January 18, 2027.

Issuing a payment stablecoin in the United States without the required status can carry criminal penalties under the law.

The OCC has also clarified that its application process will begin after the Office of Management and Budget completes the relevant approval process for the application information collection. The agency said it will issue a separate announcement when it begins accepting and processing applications and waiver requests.

This is an important distinction for existing stablecoin businesses: publication of the final rule does not itself grant every issuer a licence or establish that an application has been approved.

## Are All US Stablecoin Regulations Now Finalized?

Not yet.

Although the OCC has finalized its rule for the areas within its implementation responsibility, it explicitly stated that certain requirements related to the Bank Secrecy Act, anti-money-laundering compliance and sanctions administered by the Office of Foreign Assets Control will be handled through separate rulemaking coordinated with the Treasury Department.

Other federal agencies also have responsibilities under the GENIUS Act.

For example, the Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation proposed rules in April 2026 concerning requirements for payment-stablecoin issuers under its supervision, including reserves, redemption, capital and risk management. The Federal Reserve also published a proposed rule in September 2026 addressing responsibilities under the legislation.

A proposed rule and a final rule are not the same thing. Proposed rules set out requirements for public comment and further regulatory consideration; a final rule establishes the requirements adopted by the relevant agency, subject to its effective date and applicable legal processes.

Consequently, the October 21 announcement should be described accurately as the OCC finalizing its GENIUS Act implementation rule, rather than every US stablecoin regulation being completed at once.

## What Could the New Rules Mean for Stablecoin Issuers?

The framework could have practical consequences for businesses that issue payment stablecoins or plan to enter the US market.

First, issuers will need to assess whether their business structure and regulatory status meet the applicable approval requirements. Companies that do not qualify for the relevant framework may need to reconsider their issuance arrangements.

Second, reserve management will remain central. Maintaining qualifying assets, documenting reserve positions and meeting applicable reporting requirements can require more robust financial controls and operational processes.

Third, issuers will need to prepare for supervision and compliance obligations. The exact requirements will depend on which regulator has authority over the entity and which provisions apply to its activities.

For established issuers, adapting to the framework may involve legal reviews, compliance staffing, reporting systems and engagement with regulators. New entrants may need to factor these requirements into their launch plans.

The consequences should not be assumed to be identical for every company. The GENIUS Act includes provisions for different regulatory pathways, and the OCC's rule applies according to its defined jurisdiction.

## What Does This Mean for USDC, USDT and Other Stablecoins?

The new framework is relevant to the wider stablecoin market, but it would be premature to claim that every existing stablecoin has automatically become compliant or non-compliant.

USDC, issued by Circle's stablecoin business, and USDT, issued by Tether, are prominent dollar-linked stablecoins. Their regulatory treatment depends on the legal and regulatory requirements applicable to their issuers, their structures and the activities through which they are offered.

The OCC's announcement does not, by itself, confirm the approval status of either issuer. Nor does it establish that every stablecoin must immediately stop operating.

Existing issuers will need to assess their position under the law and applicable regulations, including approval requirements and any available transition or waiver provisions.

The OCC has specifically acknowledged that existing issuers may seek waivers in certain circumstances while their applications are pending. Such waivers are subject to the statutory framework and the agency's requirements; they should not be treated as automatic exemptions.

For exchanges, payment providers and institutional users, a key question will be how the evolving rules affect the stablecoins they support and the counterparties with which they work.

## Could the GENIUS Act Affect Crypto Payments and Financial Institutions?

Stablecoins are increasingly being considered for payment settlement, digital commerce and transfers between financial platforms.

A clearer regulatory framework could make it easier for some banks, payment companies and other institutions to assess whether and how they can participate in permitted stablecoin activities.

For businesses, regulatory certainty can help with planning, risk assessment and discussions with banking partners. It may also create incentives to strengthen compliance systems and reserve disclosures.

However, regulation alone does not guarantee widespread adoption. Commercial demand, transaction costs, liquidity, interoperability, cybersecurity and customer protections will continue to influence whether stablecoin payment services succeed.

The legislation also does not guarantee that every bank will issue a stablecoin or that every payment provider will adopt blockchain-based settlement.

The practical impact will become clearer as agencies complete their remaining implementation work, issuers seek the necessary approvals and companies adapt their products to the rules.

## What Happens Next? The January 18, 2027 Deadline

The next major milestone is January 18, 2027, when the GENIUS Act and the OCC's final rule are scheduled to take effect.

Before that date, affected issuers will need to evaluate the requirements relevant to their operations and determine what approvals, applications and compliance measures are necessary.

The OCC has said it will announce separately when it begins accepting applications and waiver requests. The timing and progress of that process will matter to existing issuers and businesses planning to enter the market.

Further rulemaking on anti-money-laundering and sanctions obligations is also important. Those provisions form part of the wider implementation process and should not be confused with requirements already finalized in the OCC's October 21 announcement.

For the stablecoin industry, the coming months are likely to be focused on regulatory preparation rather than an immediate, uniform change across every token and platform.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What did the US finalize under the GENIUS Act on October 21, 2026?

The OCC announced a final rule implementing GENIUS Act requirements for payment-stablecoin issuers and certain custody activities under its jurisdiction. Some other regulatory requirements remain subject to separate rulemaking.

### When do the new GENIUS Act rules take effect?

The OCC's final rule and the GENIUS Act are scheduled to take effect on January 18, 2027.

### Does the GENIUS Act require stablecoins to be backed one-to-one?

The law requires permitted payment-stablecoin issuers to maintain identifiable reserves backing outstanding payment stablecoins at a minimum one-to-one ratio, using qualifying reserve assets.

### Are USDC and USDT automatically approved under the new rules?

No. The OCC's announcement does not automatically approve either stablecoin or establish the regulatory status of every existing issuer. The applicable requirements depend on the issuer's circumstances and regulatory framework.

### Have all US stablecoin regulations been finalized?

No. The OCC has finalized its rule for the areas covered by its announcement, but certain anti-money-laundering and sanctions requirements remain subject to separate rulemaking. Other agencies also have implementation responsibilities.

### Can a stablecoin issuer continue operating while its application is pending?

The OCC has outlined circumstances in which eligible applicants may request waivers. Approval is not automatic, and the outcome depends on the applicable law and regulatory process.

### Does the GENIUS Act guarantee that stablecoins cannot lose their peg?

No. Reserve and supervisory requirements are intended to strengthen the framework for payment stablecoins, but they do not eliminate every financial, operational, liquidity or market risk.

## Conclusion

The OCC's October 21 announcement marks an important step in the US implementation of the GENIUS Act. By finalizing requirements covering payment-stablecoin issuers and certain custody activities, the agency has established a clearer regulatory milestone ahead of January 2027.

For stablecoin businesses, the immediate priority is to understand the requirements applicable to their activities, prepare for approval and compliance processes, and monitor further regulatory announcements.

For users and institutions, the key distinction is between a stronger regulatory framework and a guarantee of safety. Reserve requirements, supervision and redemption standards can improve accountability, but they do not remove every risk associated with digital assets.

The next stage will depend on how issuers prepare, how regulators implement the remaining provisions and how the industry adapts as the January 18, 2027 effective date approaches.
