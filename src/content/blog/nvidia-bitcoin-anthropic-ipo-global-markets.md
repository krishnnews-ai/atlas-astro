Global markets are heading into an unusually busy week, with developments across artificial intelligence, cryptocurrency, monetary policy, technology stocks and geopolitics competing for investors' attention.

Nvidia's upcoming earnings report will be closely watched following the departure of its longtime chief executive Jensen Huang. Russia is preparing to open regulated cryptocurrency trading to non-qualified investors, while the Federal Reserve is scheduled to release minutes from its latest policy meeting.

At the same time, AI company Anthropic is moving forward with its proposed initial public offering, and a planned meeting involving Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin could put international relations back at the centre of market discussions.

These developments could influence investor sentiment across several sectors. Here are six important market events to watch between November 17 and November 23.

## Nvidia Earnings: First Major Test After Jensen Huang's Departure

Nvidia's financial results will be one of the biggest market events of the week.

The company is scheduled to report its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, November 17. The report comes just days after Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive officer and a member of its board, effective immediately.

Nvidia's board appointed CFO Colette Kress as interim chief executive.

The leadership change has added an unusual layer of uncertainty to an earnings report that would already attract significant attention. Nvidia remains one of the most important companies in the global artificial intelligence industry, supplying advanced processors and systems used to build and operate AI infrastructure.

The financial expectations are substantial. Nvidia previously forecast third-quarter revenue of approximately $108 billion, plus or minus 2%.

Investors will be looking beyond the headline revenue number. Important questions include whether demand for AI computing remains strong, whether major cloud providers are maintaining their infrastructure spending and how the company plans to manage its leadership transition.

The results could also influence sentiment across semiconductor companies, data-centre operators and other businesses connected to the AI investment cycle.

However, one earnings report will not establish whether Nvidia's long-term growth trajectory has changed. Investors will also need to assess management guidance, margins and future demand.

## Russia Opens a New Door for Retail Cryptocurrency Trading

Russia is preparing to introduce a significant change to its cryptocurrency market on November 17.

The Bank of Russia has approved a framework allowing non-qualified investors to purchase selected cryptocurrencies through authorised intermediaries. The initial list includes Bitcoin, Ethereum and USDT.

Under the new rules, purchases by non-qualified investors through a single intermediary will be limited to 300,000 rubles per year.

The framework allows access through authorised intermediaries, subject to applicable requirements. Investors will also have to pass the relevant knowledge test and review the risks associated with cryptocurrency investments.

Qualified investors will have broader access to cryptoassets without the same purchase restrictions.

The move is important because it represents a more structured approach to cryptocurrency access in one of the world's major economies. Rather than treating the market only as an unregulated trading activity, the central bank is establishing a framework that defines which assets can be publicly traded and how retail participation will work.

Bitcoin and Ethereum are included alongside USDT, a stablecoin widely used in cryptocurrency markets for trading and transferring value.

The annual purchase limit, however, means the new framework should not be confused with unrestricted retail access. The rules introduce a controlled route into the market, not unlimited cryptocurrency investment.

For global crypto investors, the development will be worth watching as regulators in different countries continue to experiment with ways of bringing digital assets into formal financial systems.

## Federal Reserve Minutes Could Offer Clues About a December Rate Decision

The Federal Reserve will release minutes from its October 27–28 policy meeting on Wednesday, November 18.

At that meeting, the central bank kept its benchmark interest-rate range unchanged at 3.75% to 4%. The decision was approved by a 10–2 vote.

The two dissenting members, Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack, preferred a quarter-percentage-point rate increase.

That disagreement is one reason investors will pay close attention to the minutes.

The document may provide more detail about how policymakers assessed inflation, economic activity and the risks of keeping interest rates at their current level. It could also help investors understand whether the two officials' preference for a rate increase reflected a wider concern about persistent inflation.

For financial markets, the distinction matters.

Interest-rate expectations influence borrowing costs, bond yields, the US dollar and the valuation of growth-oriented companies. Higher rates can make future corporate earnings less attractive in today's valuation calculations, while lower-rate expectations can support riskier assets.

Cryptocurrencies are also sensitive to changes in monetary-policy expectations. Bitcoin and other digital assets can face pressure when investors move towards safer assets or when financial conditions become tighter.

Still, the minutes will not automatically signal a December rate hike. They describe discussions from the October meeting, and the Federal Reserve's next policy decision will depend on the economic information available at that time.

## Anthropic's Proposed IPO Puts the AI Industry in the Stock Market Spotlight

Anthropic's planned stock-market debut could become another defining moment for the artificial intelligence industry.

The company has filed its registration statement with the US Securities and Exchange Commission and launched the roadshow for its proposed initial public offering.

According to the preliminary prospectus, Anthropic plans to offer 825 million shares, including shares sold by existing shareholders. The proposed price range is $70 to $78 per share, and the company has applied to list its Class A common stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol ANT.

At the top of that price range, the total offering value could reach approximately $64.35 billion before any additional shares are considered. This is an estimated offering value, not a confirmed company valuation.

The proposed listing is attracting attention because Anthropic is one of the leading developers of advanced AI systems. Its Claude models compete in a market where companies are investing heavily in computing infrastructure, enterprise software and AI-powered services.

The IPO also gives public-market investors a potential new way to gain exposure to the commercial AI sector.

However, the proposed offering remains subject to the SEC registration process and market conditions. Investors should distinguish the preliminary terms from the final offer price and confirmed trading schedule.

If the listing proceeds as expected, it could become an important test of public-market appetite for major AI businesses.

It may also provide a useful reference point for how investors value companies whose growth prospects depend heavily on AI adoption, infrastructure spending and the ability to turn advanced technology into sustainable revenue.

## Trump, Xi and Putin Prepare for a Major Three-Way Meeting

Geopolitics will also take centre stage, with Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin expected to meet in Shenzhen on November 19 during the APEC summit.

The planned meeting has been reported as confirmed by officials from the United States and Russia.

A gathering involving the leaders of the three countries would be a major diplomatic development. Their governments have significant influence over global trade, energy markets, security policy and international financial conditions.

The discussions could attract attention around several issues, including nuclear arms control, US-China relations and broader geopolitical tensions.

For financial markets, the importance of the meeting lies less in the announcement itself and more in whether it produces any meaningful change in the direction of international relations.

A reduction in tensions could improve investor confidence, while disagreements or uncertainty could increase demand for defensive assets.

Energy prices, currencies, equities and digital assets can all react to major geopolitical developments, although the direction and scale of any reaction will depend on what actually emerges from the discussions.

The meeting should therefore be treated as a major event to monitor, rather than an indication that any agreement has already been reached.

## OKX and ICE Prepare to Launch 24/7 Tokenized Securities Trading

The next important date comes on November 23, when OKX and Intercontinental Exchange are preparing to launch their tokenized securities venue, known as OKXICE.

The platform is expected to allow eligible US customers to trade digital securities around the clock through the OKX application.

More than 70 assets are expected to be available at launch, including securities linked to companies in the digital-asset industry and 59 Direxion exchange-traded funds.

The development is significant because traditional stock markets generally operate within defined trading hours. A venue designed for continuous trading could bring a different structure to the way eligible investors access tokenized securities.

Tokenization involves representing financial assets through digital tokens on a blockchain-based system. Depending on the structure, these tokens may be designed to provide economic exposure or rights associated with the underlying securities.

But the distinction between tokenized securities and ordinary shares is important. Investors need to understand the legal structure, ownership rights, custody arrangements and trading rules before assuming that every token provides the same rights as a conventional share.

The project has also faced objections from a growing number of public companies over the listing of tokenized versions of their shares. Those objections highlight an important question for the industry: how much control should companies have over third-party platforms that offer digital representations of their securities?

The dispute could become an important part of the wider debate over the future of tokenized financial markets.

## What These Events Could Mean for Investors

The coming week brings together several developments that normally influence different parts of the financial system.

Nvidia's earnings could provide a fresh indication of whether demand for AI infrastructure remains strong. Russia's cryptocurrency rules will offer an example of how a major regulator is opening a controlled route for retail participation. Federal Reserve minutes may change expectations around US monetary policy, while Anthropic's proposed IPO could test investor demand for publicly traded AI businesses.

The planned meeting between Trump, Xi and Putin adds another layer of uncertainty, particularly for markets sensitive to trade, energy and international security.

Finally, the OKXICE launch could offer a glimpse into how blockchain technology may be used to expand trading access beyond traditional market hours.

None of these developments guarantees a particular market reaction. Strong Nvidia results, for example, may already be reflected in share prices, while an IPO can attract attention without necessarily producing immediate gains for investors.

The same caution applies to cryptocurrency markets. Regulatory access does not remove investment risk, and tokenized securities are not automatically equivalent to directly held traditional shares.

For investors, the key question this week is not simply which announcement attracts the most attention. It is whether these developments produce lasting changes in corporate earnings expectations, monetary policy, regulatory access and the structure of financial markets.

With AI, crypto, central banking and geopolitics all on the calendar, the next several trading sessions could provide important signals about where global markets are heading as November progresses.
